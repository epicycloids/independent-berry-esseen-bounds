\title{The sharp Berry--Esseen bound for independent arrays\\
with small Lyapunov ratio}
\author{Logan Bell}
\date{11 September 2026}

\begin{abstract}
There is a universal number $\ell_0>0$ such that every finite independent
centered array with positive total variance and finite third absolute
moments satisfies $\Delta\le C_{\mathrm E}\ell$ whenever
$\ell\le\ell_0$, where
$C_{\mathrm E}=(3+\sqrt{10})/(6\sqrt{2\pi})$. Here $\Delta$ is the
Kolmogorov distance of the standardized sum from the standard normal law,
and $\ell$ is its Lyapunov ratio. The constant is optimal. The proof adapts the uniform
jitter, support contact, and local comparison arguments of He and Cheng
to independent summands with different laws. A penalized maximization
preserves small Lyapunov ratio. For the maximizing arrays, a Fourier
expansion and the equality case of Esseen's moment inequality determine a
common scale. The variational equations confine each of their supports to
two small clusters or to a small neighborhood of zero. An exact comparison
with common-diameter Bernoulli arrays completes the proof. The latter bound
is supplied by a companion paper and uses validated finite calculations.
The present argument gives no numerical value of $\ell_0$.
\end{abstract}

\section{The theorem and its inputs}\label{sec:theorem}
Let $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be independent centered real random variables. Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:basic-data}
 B^2=\sum_{i=1}^n\E X_i^2>0,\qquad
 T=\sum_{i=1}^n\E|X_i|^3<\infty,\qquad
 \ell=\frac{T}{B^3},
\end{equation}
and let
\[
 \Delta=\sup_{x\in\R}
 \left|\Pp\left\{B^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^nX_i\le x\right\}-\Phi(x)\right|,
 \qquad
 \mathcal R(X_1,\ldots,X_n)=\frac{\Delta}{\ell}.
\]
Here $\Phi$ and $\phi$ are the standard normal distribution function and
density. Zero summands are allowed. Set
\[
 \ce=3+\sqrt{10},\qquad \be=\frac{\ce\phz}{6}.
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There exists an absolute constant $\ell_0>0$ such that, for every integer
$n\ge1$ and every array satisfying \eqref{eq:basic-data},
\[
             \ell\le\ell_0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
             \Delta\le\be\ell.
\]
The constant $\be$ cannot be decreased, even if all summands have the
same two-point law.
\end{theorem}

The unrestricted conjecture $\Delta\le\be\ell$ remains open for general
independent arrays. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} excludes violations whose
Lyapunov ratios tend to zero; its proof does not give an explicit
threshold.

He and Cheng \cite{HeCheng2026} prove the sharp bound for iid summands
once the sample size exceeds a universal threshold. We use their
uniform-jitter expansion and exact local comparison as the starting
points for the independent-array argument. Their contamination method
also supplies the support equations used below. The replacements for
identical distribution are proved here: selection by a smooth moment
penalty, a variance-weighted Fourier construction, arithmetic restrictions
on every support point, and conditional estimates for a weighted
Poisson-binomial sum.

We record the two sharp inputs. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:esseen-parameters}
 \pe=\frac{4-\sqrt{10}}2,\qquad \qe=1-\pe,\qquad
 \de=\sqrt{\pe\qe},\qquad \te=\pe^2+\qe^2.
\end{equation}
Let $Z_{\mathrm E}$ have the centered variance-one law
\[
 \Law(Z_{\mathrm E})
 =\qe\delta_{-\pe/\de}+\pe\delta_{\qe/\de},
 \qquad
 \beta_{\mathrm E}=\E|Z_{\mathrm E}|^3=\frac{\te}{\de},
 \qquad \re=\beta_{\mathrm E}^{-1}.
\]
Its third moment is positive.

Esseen's lattice moment theorem \cite[pp.~161--162]{Esseen1956} has the
following homogeneous form. If a centered random variable $Y$ with
finite third absolute moment is supported on a translate of $h\Z$,
$h>0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:esseen-moment}
 |\E Y^3|+3h\E Y^2\le\ce\E|Y|^3.
\end{equation}
For positive variance, equality holds exactly for
$\Law(Y)=\Law(\pm h\de Z_{\mathrm E})$. In particular, the maximal
span of an equality law is $h$. A constant centered variable has both
sides zero. The corresponding iid asymptotic formula gives
$\mathcal R(Z_{\mathrm E},\ldots,Z_{\mathrm E})\to\be$
as the number of summands tends to infinity
\cite{Esseen1956,MattnerShevtsova2019}. Its Lyapunov ratio is
$\beta_{\mathrm E}/\sqrt n$, proving the optimality assertion in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

The finite input is the common-diameter theorem of the companion paper
\cite[Theorem~1.2(ii)]{BellFinite2026}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-input}
 \mathcal R\bigl(h(B_1-p_1),\ldots,h(B_m-p_m)\bigr)\le\be
 \quad(h>0),
\end{equation}
where the $B_i$ are independent Bernoulli variables with arbitrary
parameters $p_i\in[0,1]$ and positive total variance. That theorem uses
Schulz's binomial bound \cite[Theorem~1]{Schulz2016} and the validated
finite calculations described in the companion paper. The numerical
dependency of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is through
\eqref{eq:finite-input}.

We also use the usual independent Berry--Esseen inequality with some
finite universal constant $C_U$; no particular numerical value is needed
\cite{Shevtsova2013}. For a real random variable $S$, put
$F_S(x)=\Pp(S\le x)$ and $F_S^-(x)=\Pp(S<x)$. Since the normal
distribution function is continuous,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:branches}
 \sup_x|F_S(x)-\Phi(x/B)|
 =\sup_x\max\{F_S(x)-\Phi(x/B),\ \Phi(x/B)-F_S^-(x)\}
\end{equation}
when $S$ is centered with variance $B^2$. Reflection changes the second
branch into the first. Constants denoted by $C$ and $c$ below may change
between estimates. Subscripts specify fixed parameters on which they
may depend. We write $A\asymp_\alpha B$ when
$c_\alpha B\le A\le C_\alpha B$ for positive constants depending only
on $\alpha$. Limits of arrays always refer to finite rows; their lengths
may grow without a prescribed rate.

The proof proceeds by contradiction. Section~\ref{sec:selection}
selects exact maximizers whose Lyapunov ratios tend to zero and bounds
each support at that scale. Sections~\ref{sec:jitter}
and~\ref{sec:confinement} identify the common scale and confine the
supports. Section~\ref{sec:local} proves the exact local comparison
needed for the final contradiction.

\section{Selecting maximizers and bounding their supports}
\label{sec:selection}
Suppose that Theorem~\ref{thm:main} fails. Normalize each array to
$B=1$. For integers $j\ge1$, let
\[
 \eta_j=j^{-1},\qquad t_j=e^{-j^2},\qquad
 f(r)=
 \begin{cases}
  0,&r\le0,\\
  r^2/(1+r),&r>0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \psi_j(t)=\eta_j f(\log(t/t_j)).
\]
The function $\psi_j$ is continuously differentiable and nondecreasing,
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penalty}
 \psi_j\ge0,\quad
 \psi_j(t)=0\ (t\le t_j),\quad
 0\le t\psi_j'(t)\le\eta_j,\quad
 \psi_j(t)\ge\eta_j\{\log(t/t_j)-1\}.
\end{equation}
Choose a violating array with $\ell\le t_j$, and let $n_j$ be its
length. In that fixed dimension maximize, over independent centered
arrays of total variance one and thresholds $z\in\R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penalized-objective}
 \frac{F_S(z)-\Phi(z)}{\ell}-\psi_j(\ell),
 \qquad S=\sum_iX_i.
\end{equation}
Its supremum exceeds $\be$, since reflection permits a positive branch
of the chosen violation.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:attainment}
The supremum in \eqref{eq:penalized-objective} is attained. For a
maximizer, write
\[
 R_j=\frac{F_{S_j}(z_j)-\Phi(z_j)}{\ell_j}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selected}
 R_j>\be,\qquad R_j\le C_U,\qquad
 \ell_j\le t_j\exp\{1+(C_U-\be)/\eta_j\}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Moreover, $\mathcal R(X_{1,j},\ldots,X_{n_j,j})=R_j$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed dimension $n$, Lyapunov's inequality and convexity give
\[
 \ell\ge\sum_i(\E X_i^2)^{3/2}\ge n^{-1/2}.
\]
Along a maximizing sequence with objective greater than a fixed
$r_0>0$, the positive discrepancy is at least $r_0\ell$ and at most
one, so $\ell\le r_0^{-1}$. Each coordinate has a bounded third
absolute moment. Coordinatewise weak subsequences preserve means and
variances, because this bound gives uniform integrability of squares.
The limiting total variance is therefore one.

The thresholds stay bounded. Uniformly over centered variance-one sums,
Chebyshev's inequality makes their positive discrepancies tend to zero
as the threshold tends to either infinity, whereas here they are at
least $r_0/\sqrt n$. Pass to a subsequence on which the thresholds
converge to $z$ and the third-moment sums converge to $L$.
Moving-threshold Portmanteau gives
$F_S(z)\ge\limsup F_{S_r}(z_r)$, and lower semicontinuity gives the
limiting third-moment sum $\ell\le L$. If $\ell<L$, the limiting
positive ratio strictly increases and its nondecreasing penalty
weakly decreases, exceeding the supremum. Thus $\ell=L$ and the
limit attains the supremum. This is the fixed-dimension compactness
argument of \citet[Lemma~4.1]{HeCheng2026}, applied coordinatewise.

At the maximizer, $R_j-\psi_j(\ell_j)>\be$ and $R_j\le C_U$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:penalty} gives \eqref{eq:selected}.
A larger discrepancy of either sign in the same array, with reflection
if necessary, would increase \eqref{eq:penalized-objective}; hence its
full ratio is $R_j$.
\end{proof}

We suppress $j$ temporarily. Put
\[
 s_i=\E X_i^2,\qquad b_i=\E|X_i|^3,\qquad
 M_i=\E[X_i|X_i|],\qquad
 K=R+\ell\psi_j'(\ell).
\]
Thus $R\le K\le R+\eta_j$. Let $F=F_S$ and let $G_i$ be the CDF
of $S-X_i$.

\begin{lemma}[Support contact]\label{lem:contact}
For every $y\in\R$,
\begin{align}
 D_i(y):={}&G_i(z-y)-F(z)+\phi(z)y
       +\frac{z\phi(z)}2(y^2-s_i)\notag\\
 &+\frac32K\ell(y^2-s_i)
       -K\{|y|^3-b_i-3M_i y\}\le0.                 \label{eq:contact}
\end{align}
Equality holds at every $y\in\supp X_i$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Replace the raw law of coordinate $i$ by
$(1-e)\Law(X_i)+e\delta_y$, keeping the raw threshold $z$ fixed,
and then center and standardize the resulting array. The derivatives
at $e=0$ of its total mean, total variance, and sum of absolute third
central moments are, respectively,
\[
 y,\qquad y^2-s_i,\qquad |y|^3-b_i-3M_i y.
\]
For the third identity, differentiation under the expectation is
justified by a bound $C_y(1+X_i^2)$. The raw CDF derivative is
$G_i(z-y)-F(z)$. Consequently the derivative of the normalized
third-moment sum is
\[
 |y|^3-b_i-3M_i y-\frac32\ell(y^2-s_i).
\]
Differentiating \eqref{eq:penalized-objective} and multiplying by
$\ell$ gives \eqref{eq:contact}. Maximality makes it nonpositive.

Its integral under $\Law(X_i)$ is zero: each displayed moment term
integrates to zero, and $\E G_i(z-X_i)=F(z)$. Thus $D_i=0$
almost surely. A CDF is upper semicontinuous, so $D_i$ is upper
semicontinuous. The closed set $\{D_i=0\}$ therefore contains the
entire support. This is the independent-coordinate version of
\citet[Lemma~4.3]{HeCheng2026}; the moment coefficient $K$ includes
the derivative of the penalty.
\end{proof}

For $0\le s\le1/2$ define the Gaussian remainder
\[
 H_s(z,y)=
 \Phi\left(\frac{z-y}{\sqrt{1-s}}\right)-\Phi(z)
 +\phi(z)y+\frac{z\phi(z)}2(y^2-s).
\]
The cancellation used in \citet[Lemma~4.4]{HeCheng2026} gives
\begin{align}
 |H_s(z,y)|
 &\le\frac{\phz}{6}|y|^3+C(s|y|+s^2),                  \label{eq:H-global}\\
 H_s(z,y)
 &=\phi''(z)\left(\frac{sy}{2}-\frac{y^3}{6}\right)
     +O(y^4+sy^2+s^2)                                  \label{eq:H-local}
\end{align}
in the second formula for bounded $y$, uniformly in $z$.
For completeness, translation Taylor expansion has remainder at most
$\|\phi''\|_\infty|y|^3/6=\phz|y|^3/6$. For $u=z-y$,
differentiating $\lambda\mapsto\Phi(u/\sqrt{1-\lambda})$ twice
shows that its second derivative is uniformly bounded for
$0\le\lambda\le1/2$. The scale correction, after cancellation of
the term $sz\phi(z)/2$, is
\[
 \frac{s}{2}\{(z-y)\phi(z-y)-z\phi(z)\}+O(s^2).
\]
The derivative of $z\phi(z)$ is $-\phi''(z)$ and all the Gaussian
derivatives in question are bounded. These facts give both formulas.

Delete coordinate $i$, and suppose $s_i\le1/2$. Its Lyapunov ratio is
\[
 \ell_-=\frac{\ell-b_i}{(1-s_i)^{3/2}}.
\]
Adding a zero coordinate puts the deleted array in the same
maximization class. Its positive signed ratio at every threshold is
therefore at most $R+\psi_j(\ell_-)-\psi_j(\ell)$. With
$\log_+t=\max(\log t,0)$, equation~\eqref{eq:penalty} gives
\[
 \psi_j(\ell_-)-\psi_j(\ell)
 \le\eta_j\log_+(\ell_-/\ell)\le3\eta_j s_i.
\]
Substitution into the contact equality yields, for $y\in\supp X_i$,
\begin{align}
0\le{}&H_{s_i}(z,y)-K|y|^3+\frac32K\ell y^2+3KM_i y\notag\\
 &+C\ell s_i^2+\eta_j b_i+C\eta_j\ell s_i.             \label{eq:deletion}
\end{align}
Here and below these estimates are used for large $j$, so all the
constants are uniform. To verify the remainder, the constant terms
before applying upper bounds are
\[
 R(\ell_--\ell)-\frac32K\ell s_i+Kb_i
 +\{\psi_j(\ell_-)-\psi_j(\ell)\}\ell_-.
\]
Writing $K=R+\vartheta$, $0\le\vartheta\le\eta_j$, the terms with
$R$ equal
\[
 R\ell\{(1-s_i)^{-3/2}-1-\tfrac32s_i\}
 -Rb_i\{(1-s_i)^{-3/2}-1\}.
\]
The second is nonpositive. The remaining terms are bounded above
by those in \eqref{eq:deletion}.

The support estimate below adapts the cubic domination argument of
\citet[Proposition~4.5]{HeCheng2026} to the coordinate variance scales.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:support-bound}
There is a finite constant $L$ such that the selected arrays satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:common-support}
                 \supp X_{i,j}\subset[-L\ell_j,L\ell_j]
                 \quad\hbox{for all $i$ and all sufficiently large $j$}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The contact equality first bounds every support by one fixed interval:
its cubic coefficient is at most $-\be$, while all coefficients of
lower degree are uniformly bounded. Indeed $\ell\le1/\be$,
$b_i\le\ell$, $s_i\le1$, $|M_i|\le s_i$, and
$\phi(z),z\phi(z),R,K$ are bounded. Thus each support is compact.
Let $L_i=\max\{|y|:y\in\supp X_i\}$. Since
$s_i^{3/2}\le b_i\le\ell_j\to0$, eventually every $s_i\le1/2$.
Also $b_i\le s_iL_i$.

Use \eqref{eq:H-global} in \eqref{eq:deletion} at an extreme
support point. Since $\gamma:=\be-\phz/6>0$,
\[
 \gamma L_i^3
 \le C\{\ell L_i^2+s_iL_i+s_i^2+\eta_j\ell s_i\}.
\]
If $L_i$ is a sufficiently large multiple of $\ell+\sqrt{s_i}$,
division by $L_i^3$ contradicts this inequality. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-support}
                     L_i\le C(\ell+\sqrt{s_i}).
\end{equation}

Suppose now that some sequence of coordinates has
$\sigma_j/\ell_j\to\infty$, where $\sigma_j=\sqrt{s_{i,j}}$.
Then $\sigma_j\to0$, and \eqref{eq:first-support} bounds the
centered variance-one laws $X_{i,j}/\sigma_j$ in a common compact
interval. Extract a weak limit $Q$, still centered with variance one,
and further subsequences such that
\[
 R_j\to r\ge\be,\qquad
 \frac{\phi''(z_j)}6\to d,\qquad
 \frac{M_{i,j}}{\sigma_j^2}\to m,\qquad |d|\le\phz/6.
\]
For any $u\in\supp Q$ choose support points
$\sigma_j u_j$ with $u_j\to u$. Divide \eqref{eq:deletion} by
$\sigma_j^3$ and use \eqref{eq:H-local}. The quadratic term tends
to zero. The remainders tend to zero because
\[
 \frac{\ell s_i^2}{\sigma_j^3}=\ell_j\sigma_j\to0,\quad
 \frac{\eta_j b_i}{\sigma_j^3}
 \le C\eta_j(1+\ell_j/\sigma_j)\to0,\quad
 \frac{\eta_j\ell s_i}{\sigma_j^3}
 =\eta_j\ell_j/\sigma_j\to0.
\]
We obtain
\[
              0\le -du^3-r|u|^3+A u,\qquad A=3(d+rm).
\]
The support of $Q$ contains $u_+>0$ and $u_-<0$. The first requires
$A\ge(r+d)u_+^2>0$, while the second requires
$A\le-(r-d)|u_-|^2<0$. This contradiction proves
$\sup_i\sigma_{i,j}=O(\ell_j)$, and \eqref{eq:first-support}
proves \eqref{eq:common-support}.
\end{proof}

\section{Uniform jitter and equality in the moment bound}
\label{sec:jitter}
We give the Fourier argument for arbitrary independent rows satisfying
\[
 \sum_i\E X_{i,j}^2=1,\qquad
 \ell_j=\sum_i\E|X_{i,j}|^3\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 |X_{i,j}|\le L\ell_j.
\]
Put $Y_{i,j}=X_{i,j}/\ell_j$ and take independent copies $Y'_{i,j}$.
The measures
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diff-measure}
 \mu_j(A)=\frac{\ell_j^2}{2}\sum_i
 \E\bigl[(Y_{i,j}-Y'_{i,j})^2
           \ind_{\{Y_{i,j}-Y'_{i,j}\in A\}}\bigr]
\end{equation}
are probability measures supported in $[-2L,2L]$. Pass to a
subsequence on which $\mu_j\Rightarrow\mu$ and
\[
 \theta_j=\ell_j^{-1}\sum_i\E X_{i,j}^3\longrightarrow\theta,
 \qquad |\theta|\le1.
\]
Write $f_{i,j}(u)=\E e^{iuY_{i,j}}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g}
 g_j(u)=\ell_j^2\sum_i(1-|f_{i,j}(u)|^2)
 =2\int\frac{1-\cos(ud)}{d^2}\,\mu_j(dd)
 \longrightarrow
 g(u)=2\int\frac{1-\cos(ud)}{d^2}\,\mu(dd).
\end{equation}
The quotient at $d=0$ is $u^2/2$. This convergence holds locally
in $C^2$. A nonzero zero $u$ of $g$ requires
$\mu(\{0\})=0$ and $ud\in2\pi\Z$ for $\mu$-almost every $d$.
The zero set is consequently a closed additive subgroup. Since
$g''(0)=2$, zero is isolated, and the subgroup is either $\{0\}$
or $(2\pi/h)\Z$ for some $h>0$. Put $h=0$ in the first case.
At every zero of $g$, its second derivative is two.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform-jitter expansion]\label{lem:jitter}
Let $V_h$ be independent and uniform on $[-h/2,h/2]$, with $V_0=0$,
and put $\kappa_j=\sum_i\E X_{i,j}^3$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jitter}
 \sup_x\left|
 \Pp\left\{\sum_iX_{i,j}+\ell_jV_h\le x\right\}
 -\Phi(x)-\frac{\kappa_j}{6}(1-x^2)\phi(x)
 \right|=o(\ell_j).
\end{equation}
In particular, uniformly in $x$,
\begin{align}
 F_{S_j}(x)-\Phi(x)
 &\le\ell_j\phi(x)\left\{\frac h2+
                      \frac{\theta_j}{6}(1-x^2)\right\}
                      +o(\ell_j),                    \label{eq:upper-envelope}\\
 \Phi(x)-F_{S_j}^-(x)
 &\le\ell_j\phi(x)\left\{\frac h2-
                      \frac{\theta_j}{6}(1-x^2)\right\}
                      +o(\ell_j).                    \label{eq:lower-envelope}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We use the signed smoothing inequality of
\citet[Lemma~2.1]{HeCheng2026}. In its needed form, if $F$ is a
probability CDF with finite first absolute moment, and $\nu$ is a
finite signed measure of mass one with
$\int|x|\,d|\nu|(x)<\infty$ whose CDF $G$ is globally Lipschitz
with constant $M$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:signed-smoothing}
 \|F-G\|_\infty
 \le C_1\int_{-A}^A
       \frac{|\widehat F(t)-\widehat\nu(t)|}{|t|}\,dt
       +\frac{C_2M}{A}\qquad(A>0).
\end{equation}
Our Fourier convention is $\widehat\nu(t)=\int e^{itx}\nu(dx)$,
and $\sinc u=\sin(u)/u$, with $\sinc0=1$.
The signed CDF
$G_j(x)=\Phi(x)+\kappa_j(1-x^2)\phi(x)/6$
satisfies these hypotheses with a uniform Lipschitz constant, and
\[
 \widehat\nu_j(t)=e^{-t^2/2}\{1+\kappa_j(it)^3/6\}.
\]

The product bound replacing the iid power is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product}
 \left|\prod_i f_{i,j}(u)\right|
 \le \exp\{-g_j(u)/(2\ell_j^2)\}.
\end{equation}
At low standardized frequencies,
$\sum_i\E X_{i,j}^4\le L\ell_j^2$ and
$\sum_i(\E X_{i,j}^2)^2\le L^2\ell_j^2$. Taylor expansion of
the logarithms, for $|t|\ell_j\le\delta_L$ with $\delta_L>0$
sufficiently small, gives
\[
 \sum_i\log\E e^{itX_{i,j}}
 =-\frac{t^2}{2}+\frac{(it)^3\kappa_j}{6}
       +O_L(t^4\ell_j^2).
\]
The real part of the remainder is at most a fixed fraction of
$t^2$. Since the cubic term is purely imaginary, the inequalities
$|e^{iv}-1-iv|\le v^2/2$ for real $v$ and
$|e^w-1|\le|w|e^{|w|}$ imply
\[
 \left|\prod_i\E e^{itX_{i,j}}-\widehat\nu_j(t)\right|
 \le C_L e^{-ct^2}\ell_j^2(t^4+t^6)
 \qquad(|t|\ell_j\le\delta_L).
\]
Multiplication by $\sinc(h\ell_jt/2)$ adds an integrable
$O_h(\ell_j^2t^2e^{-ct^2})$ term. Thus the low-frequency part
of the integral in \eqref{eq:signed-smoothing} is $O_L(\ell_j^2)$.

Fix a raw cutoff $T>\delta_L$ whose endpoints are not zeros of
$g$, and set $A=T/\ell_j$. Near each nonzero zero
$u_k=2\pi k/h$ in $[-T,T]$, local $C^2$ convergence makes $g_j$
strictly convex. Its unique nearby minimum $v_{j,k}$ tends to
$u_k$, and nonnegativity gives
$g_j(u)\ge(u-v_{j,k})^2/2$ on a fixed small neighborhood.
The jitter multiplier vanishes at $u_k$, and is bounded there by
$C(|u-v_{j,k}|+|v_{j,k}-u_k|)$. Since $|u|$ is bounded away
from zero in this neighborhood, its contribution to the smoothing
integral is at most
\[
 C\int_\R
 (|u-v_{j,k}|+|v_{j,k}-u_k|)
 e^{-(u-v_{j,k})^2/(4\ell_j^2)}\,du
 =O(\ell_j^2)+o(\ell_j).
\]
On the remaining compact raw-frequency set $g$ has a positive
minimum, and \eqref{eq:product} gives exponential decay. If $h=0$
there are no nonzero zeros to consider. The Gaussian comparison
transform is itself exponentially small at these frequencies.
Equation~\eqref{eq:signed-smoothing} leaves $C\ell_j/T$.
Taking the row limit first and then $T\to\infty$ proves
\eqref{eq:jitter}. This is the displaced-resonance argument of
\citet[Lemma~2.2]{HeCheng2026}, with \eqref{eq:diff-measure}
controlling the independent product.

Let $J_j$ be the jittered CDF. Bounded support of the jitter gives
$J_j(x-h\ell_j/2)\le F_{S_j}^-(x)\le F_{S_j}(x)
\le J_j(x+h\ell_j/2)$ when $h>0$. Taylor expansion gives
\eqref{eq:upper-envelope}--\eqref{eq:lower-envelope}.
For $h=0$, \eqref{eq:jitter} also holds for left limits by
continuity of $G_j$. This is the envelope step of
\citet[Corollary~2.4]{HeCheng2026}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Equality in the array bound]\label{prop:equality}
For the selected arrays of Section~\ref{sec:selection},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equality-limits}
 R_j\longrightarrow\be,\qquad z_j\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 h=\frac1{\te}=\frac{\ce}{\sqrt{10}},\qquad
 \theta=\frac1{\sqrt{10}}
\end{equation}
on every subsequence chosen above. In addition,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law-weight}
 \sum_i \E X_{i,j}^2\,\delta_{\Law(X_{i,j}/\ell_j)}
 \ \Longrightarrow\
 \delta_{\Law(\re Z_{\mathrm E})}.
\end{equation}
The outer measures in \eqref{eq:law-weight} are probability measures
on the space of laws supported in $[-L,L]$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The envelopes give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral-envelope}
 \limsup_j R_j\le\frac{\phz}{6}(|\theta|+3h).
\end{equation}
If $h=0$, the right side is at most $\phz/6<\be$, which is
impossible. Suppose $h>0$ and put $u_0=2\pi/h$.
For each $i,j$, choose the phase of $f_{i,j}(u_0)$, arbitrarily
when that value is zero, and round $Y_{i,j}$ to the nearest point
on the corresponding translate of $h\Z$. Call the rounded
variable $Z_{i,j}$ and its centered version $Q_{i,j}$.
If $d$ is the distance to the selected lattice, $|d|\le h/2$,
then
$|e^{iu_0Y_{i,j}}-e^{i\arg f_{i,j}(u_0)}|\ge4|d|/h$.
It follows that
\[
 \E|Y_{i,j}-Q_{i,j}|^2
 \le \E|Y_{i,j}-Z_{i,j}|^2
 \le \frac{h^2}{8}(1-|f_{i,j}(u_0)|^2).
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:round-error}
              e_j:=\ell_j^2\sum_i
                    \E|Y_{i,j}-Q_{i,j}|^2\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
All centered rounded variables lie in a fixed compact interval.
For variables $Y,Q$ in that interval, the differences of their
second moments, signed third moments, and absolute third moments
are bounded by a constant times
$\E[|Y-Q|(|Y|+|Q|)]$. Summing by Cauchy--Schwarz and using
\eqref{eq:round-error} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aggregate-rounding}
 \ell_j^2\sum_i\E Q_{i,j}^2\to1,\qquad
 \ell_j^2\sum_i\E|Q_{i,j}|^3\to1,\qquad
 \ell_j^2\sum_i\E Q_{i,j}^3\to\theta.
\end{equation}
The second moments in the Cauchy--Schwarz bound have bounded sum:
$\ell_j^2\sum_i\E(|Y_{i,j}|+|Q_{i,j}|)^2\le6+4e_j$.
These estimates impose no bound on $n_j\ell_j^2$.

Apply \eqref{eq:esseen-moment} to each $Q_{i,j}$ and sum.
Equations~\eqref{eq:aggregate-rounding} imply
\[
                         |\theta|+3h\le\ce.
\]
Since $R_j>\be$, equality holds here and in
\eqref{eq:spectral-envelope}, and $R_j\to\be$.
In particular, both nonnegative quantities
\begin{align*}
 &\ell_j^2\sum_i
   \{\ce\E|Q_{i,j}|^3-|\E Q_{i,j}^3|-3h\E Q_{i,j}^2\},\\
 &\ell_j^2\left\{\sum_i|\E Q_{i,j}^3|
                        -\left|\sum_i\E Q_{i,j}^3\right|\right\}
\end{align*}
tend to zero.

Consider the compact class of centered laws on translates of $h\Z$
in a fixed bounded interval. For such a law $Q$ write
\[
 v(Q)=\int y^2Q(dy),\qquad
 d(Q)=\ce\int |y|^3Q(dy)-\left|\int y^3Q(dy)\right|-3hv(Q).
\]
On subsets where $v(Q)$ is bounded below, continuity and Esseen's
equality case imply that $d(Q)/v(Q)$ is bounded below by a positive
number away from the two laws $\Law(\pm h\de Z_{\mathrm E})$.
The same conclusion holds near variance zero. Indeed, if nondegenerate
laws $Q_r$ tend to $\delta_0$, its mass tends to one at a unique lattice
site $c_r\to0$. Represent a variable of law $Q_r$ as
$V_r=c_r+D_r$, where $D_r\in h\Z$,
and let $a_r=\Pp(D_r\ne0)\to0$. Bounded support and centering give
$|c_r|=O(a_r)$ and $\E D_r^2\ge h^2a_r$. Hence
\[
 \Var V_r=\E D_r^2\{1+O(a_r)\},\qquad
 \frac{\E|V_r|^3}{\Var V_r}\ge h-o(1).
\]
Using $|\E V_r^3|\le\E|V_r|^3$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zero-variance-gap}
 \frac{d(Q_r)}{v(Q_r)}
 \ge(\ce-4)h-o(1)=(\sqrt{10}-1)h-o(1)>0.
\end{equation}

The vanishing first deficit therefore concentrates variance weight
on $\Law(\pm h\de Z_{\mathrm E})$. At these two laws, the signed
third moment divided by variance is the nonzero number
$\pm h(\qe-\pe)$. The vanishing second deficit forces one of the
two orientation weights to tend to zero. The ratio of the
aggregate absolute third moment to variance in
\eqref{eq:aggregate-rounding} is one, whereas at either equality
law it is $h\de\beta_{\mathrm E}=h\te$. Thus
$h=1/\te$ and the magnitude of $\theta$ is
$h(\qe-\pe)=1/\sqrt{10}$.

The positive branch determines its sign. Write
$A=h/2$ and $D=|\theta|/6$. The possible positive envelopes are
$E_\pm(x)=\phi(x)(A\pm D\mp Dx^2)$. Since $A>3D$,
\[
 E_-'(x)=x\phi(x)(3D-A-Dx^2)<0\qquad(x>0),
\]
so its maximum is $\phz(A-D)<\be$. The negative orientation
cannot support $R_j>\be$. The function $E_+$ has its unique
global maximum $\phz(A+D)=\be$ at zero, by
$\phi(x)\le\phz$ and $(1-x^2)\phi(x)\le\phz$.
Both envelopes tend to zero at infinity. Uniformity in
\eqref{eq:upper-envelope} now gives $z_j\to0$ and
$\theta>0$. This is the active-branch argument used in
\citet[Lemma~5.1]{HeCheng2026}.

Finally, \eqref{eq:round-error} transfers the conclusion from
rounded laws and their weights to the original ones. Explicitly,
\[
 \ell_j^2\sum_i
   |\Var Y_{i,j}-\Var Q_{i,j}|
 \le e_j^{1/2}
 \left(\ell_j^2\sum_i\E(|Y_{i,j}|+|Q_{i,j}|)^2\right)^{1/2}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
If $W_1(P,Q)$ denotes the infimum of $\E|Y-Z|$ over couplings
with laws $P,Q$, the same coupling
gives
\[
 \ell_j^2\sum_i\Var(Y_{i,j})\,
       W_1(\Law(Y_{i,j}),\Law(Q_{i,j}))^2\le L^2e_j\to0.
\]
On the compact space of bounded-support laws, this proves
\eqref{eq:law-weight}. Every subsequence admits the preceding
argument with the same scale and positive orientation, so the
assertions hold for the selected sequence.
\end{proof}

\section{Confinement of every support}\label{sec:confinement}
Throughout this section we use the selected arrays, the scale
$h=1/\te$, and the limits in Proposition~\ref{prop:equality}.
We first show that two support points of the same coordinate can
have only an asymptotically integral separation in units of $h\ell_j$.
This extends the support-separation argument of
\citet[Lemma~5.3]{HeCheng2026}.

\begin{lemma}[Arithmetic support differences]\label{lem:arithmetic}
Choose arbitrary coordinate indices $i_j$ and support points
$x_j,y_j\in\supp X_{i_j,j}$. Every subsequential limit of
$(y_j-x_j)/\ell_j$ belongs to $h\Z$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Deleting any coordinate changes $g_j$ and its first two derivatives
on a fixed compact interval by $O(\ell_j^2)$, uniformly over that
coordinate. Also, by Proposition~\ref{prop:support-bound},
\[
 s_i=O(\ell_j^2),\quad b_i=O(\ell_j^3),\quad
 |M_i|=O(\ell_j^2),\quad
 \ell_-=\ell_j+O(\ell_j^3).
\]
The deleted arrays therefore have the same limiting function $g$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:jitter} applies uniformly to their standardized laws.
Indeed, failure of uniformity would select one failing coordinate per
row and contradict that lemma for the resulting sequence. We may use
raw jitter width exactly $h\ell_j$: the natural width of a standardized
deleted array, after returning to raw units, differs from it by
$O(\ell_j^3)$. Coupling the two uniform jitters and using the
bounded derivative of their smooth comparison CDF gives an
$o(\ell_j)$ error.

At a support point $y=O(\ell_j)$, the contact equality gives
\[
 G_i(z_j-y)-F(z_j)
 =-\phi(z_j)y-\frac{z_j\phi(z_j)}2(y^2-s_i)+O(\ell_j^3).
\]
The Gaussian expansion has the same first two terms. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deleted-saturation}
 G_i(z_j-y)-\Phi\left(\frac{z_j-y}{\sqrt{1-s_i}}\right)
 =R_j\ell_j+O(\ell_j^3),
\end{equation}
uniformly over all coordinates and their support points.

Fix the chosen coordinate $i_j$, put $T_j=S_j-X_{i_j,j}$ and
$u_j=z_j-x_j$, and define locally finite measures
\[
       \nu_j(A)=\ell_j^{-1}
          \Pp\{(T_j-u_j)/\ell_j\in A\}.
\]
Let $J_j$ be the CDF of $T_j+\ell_jV_h$.
The jitter envelopes bound $\nu_j$ on every compact interval:
for fixed $a<b$, the mass on $[a,b]$ is at most
\[
 \ell_j^{-1}
 \{J_j(u_j+\ell_j(b+h/2))
        -J_j(u_j+\ell_j(a-h/2))\}.
\]
Using slightly enlarged endpoints gives the same bound if needed.
The jitter expansion makes this bounded, so a subsequence has
a vague limit $\nu$. Since $u_j\to0$, that expansion also gives
\[
 \ell_j^{-1}
 \{J_j(u_j+\ell_j b)-J_j(u_j+\ell_j a)\}
 \longrightarrow \phz(b-a).
\]
Convolution with a compactly supported measure passes to vague
limits when tested against continuous functions of compact support.
It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-convolution}
              \nu*\operatorname{Unif}[-h/2,h/2]=\phz\,dx .
\end{equation}
Differentiation in the sense of distributions gives equality of
the translates of $\nu$ by $h/2$ and $-h/2$. Hence $\nu$ is
$h$-periodic.

Equations~\eqref{eq:deleted-saturation} and \eqref{eq:equality-limits}
give
$J_j(u_j+h\ell_j/2)-G_{i_j}(u_j)=o(\ell_j)$.
The exact identity
\[
 J_j(u_j+h\ell_j/2)-G_{i_j}(u_j)
 =\E\left[
 \left(1-\frac{T_j-u_j}{h\ell_j}\right)
 \ind_{\{u_j<T_j<u_j+h\ell_j\}}\right]
\]
has a nonnegative integrand. After division by $\ell_j$, it
forces the limiting mass of each compact subinterval of $(0,h)$
to be zero. Therefore $\nu((0,h))=0$. Periodicity and
\eqref{eq:local-convolution} now identify
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comb}
                  \nu=\phz h\sum_{k\in\Z}\delta_{kh}.
\end{equation}

Suppose $(y_j-x_j)/\ell_j\to d$. The second support contact is at
$z_j-y_j=u_j-\ell_j d_j$. Its saturation, by the same nonnegative
integral, gives no limiting mass in $(-d,h-d)$. If $d\notin h\Z$,
one atom of \eqref{eq:comb} lies strictly inside that interval.
A compact neighborhood of that atom contradicts saturation.
Thus $d\in h\Z$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Limits of entire coordinate supports]\label{lem:support-limits}
Choose any coordinate $i_j$ from each row, and pass to a subsequence
on which $\Law(X_{i_j,j}/\ell_j)$ converges weakly to $Q$ and its
support converges in Hausdorff distance to a nonempty compact set $A$.
Then either $A=\{0\}$ and $Q=\delta_0$, or $A$ consists of exactly
two points at distance $h$ and $Q$ gives positive mass to both.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
All laws and supports lie in the fixed interval $[-L,L]$, so these
subsequences exist. Put $v=\int y^2Q(dy)$ and
$M=\int y|y|Q(dy)$.
For every $t\in A$, choose support points $y_j=\ell_j t_j$ with
$t_j\to t$. In \eqref{eq:deletion}, set
$s_{i_j,j}=\ell_j^2v_j$ and $M_{i_j,j}=\ell_j^2M_j$.
Formula~\eqref{eq:H-local} is uniform for bounded $t_j,v_j$,
including $v_j\to0$. Divide by $\phz\ell_j^3/6$ and use
$R_j,K_j\to\be$ and $z_j\to0$. The remainder in
\eqref{eq:deletion}, divided by $\ell_j^3$, is $O(\eta_j)+o(1)$.
We obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:support-polynomial}
 t^3-\ce|t|^3+\frac32\ce t^2+3(\ce M-v)t\ge0
 \qquad(t\in A).
\end{equation}
This is the independent-coordinate counterpart of the limiting
support polynomial in \citet[Lemma~5.2]{HeCheng2026}.

If $Q$ is nondegenerate, the extreme points of $A$ can be written
$-a,b$ with $a,b>0$, because $\supp Q\subset A$ and $Q$ is
centered. Evaluating \eqref{eq:support-polynomial} at these two
points and eliminating its linear coefficient gives
\[
        (\ce+1)a^2+(\ce-1)b^2\le\frac32\ce(a+b).
\]
At fixed $a+b$, the left side is at least
$(\ce^2-1)(a+b)^2/(2\ce)$. Since $\ce^2-1=6\ce$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diameter}
               a+b\le\frac{3\ce^2}{\ce^2-1}
                      =\frac{\ce}{2}<2h.
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:arithmetic} says that every difference between
points of $A$ belongs to $h\Z$. Hence $A$ has exactly two
points and their distance is $h$. The limiting law has positive
variance, so both masses are positive.

If $Q$ is degenerate, centering makes it $\delta_0$, and
$v=M=0$. Equation~\eqref{eq:support-polynomial} confines $A$ to
\[
 \left[-\frac{3\ce}{2(\ce+1)},\
             \frac{3\ce}{2(\ce-1)}\right]\subset(-h,h).
\]
Since $0\in A$, Lemma~\ref{lem:arithmetic} forces $A=\{0\}$.
In particular, support points whose probabilities vanish in the
weak limit are also covered.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Two clusters and the remaining coordinates]
\label{prop:partition}
For all sufficiently large $j$, partition the coordinates according
to whether the support diameter of $Y_{i,j}=X_{i,j}/\ell_j$ is at
least $h/2$. Let $m_j$ count the coordinates in that class.
There is an $\alpha\in(0,1/2)$ for which those coordinates have
representations
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core-representation}
 Y_{i,j}=h_{i,j}(B_{i,j}-p_{i,j})+U_{i,j},\qquad
 p_{i,j}\in[\alpha,1-\alpha],\qquad
 \E(U_{i,j}\mid B_{i,j})=0,
\end{equation}
with independent pairs, such that
\[
 \max_i|h_{i,j}-h|\to0,\qquad
 \max_i\|U_{i,j}\|_\infty\to0.
\]
The other coordinates, denoted $Z_{r,j}$ in these same scaled units,
are centered, independent of all those pairs, and satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tiny}
 \max_r\|Z_{r,j}\|_\infty\to0,\qquad
 m_j\asymp\ell_j^{-2}\to\infty,\qquad
 \sum_r\Var Z_{r,j}=o(m_j).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:support-limits} and compactness make the following
conclusions uniform over coordinates. Supports with diameter below
$h/2$ have diameters tending to zero. The other diameters tend to
$h$, and their supports approach their two extreme points.
Otherwise one could select a coordinate per row whose simultaneous
law and support limit violated that lemma.

Divide each such support at the midpoint of its extreme points,
and let $B_{i,j}$ indicate the upper cluster. Both cluster
probabilities are bounded away from zero uniformly. If one
tended to zero, the weak limit would be a point mass, while the
full support limit would retain two points at distance $h$,
contrary to Lemma~\ref{lem:support-limits}. Choose $\alpha>0$
accordingly. The difference of the two conditional means is
$h_{i,j}\to h$ uniformly. Subtracting these means defines
$U_{i,j}$ and gives \eqref{eq:core-representation}; its supremum
norm tends uniformly to zero.

Every remaining centered support contains zero in its convex hull,
so its shrinking diameter bounds its absolute values as well.
Equation~\eqref{eq:law-weight} then implies that their total
original variance tends to zero: their scaled laws converge
uniformly to $\delta_0$, which is separated from
$\Law(\re Z_{\mathrm E})$. The variances of the two-cluster
coordinates in scaled units are bounded above and below by fixed
positive constants. Their total original variance tends to one,
and so $m_j\asymp\ell_j^{-2}$. Finally,
\[
 \sum_r\Var Z_{r,j}
 =\ell_j^{-2}\sum_{\text{remaining }r}\Var X_{r,j}
 =o(\ell_j^{-2})=o(m_j).
\]
\end{proof}

\section{An exact comparison near a common diameter}\label{sec:local}
The following local theorem includes the additional coordinates in
Proposition~\ref{prop:partition}. Its proof adapts the two regimes
of \citet[Section~3]{HeCheng2026}. Conditioning on a Bernoulli
count now leaves different label probabilities and different gaps;
we prove the conditional estimates explicitly.

\begin{theorem}[Local comparison]\label{thm:local}
For each $\alpha\in(0,1/2)$ there exist $\eta>0$ and an integer
$N$ with the following property. Suppose $m\ge N$, $h>0$, and
\[
 X_i=h_i(B_i-p_i)+U_i,\qquad 1\le i\le m,
\]
where the pairs $(B_i,U_i)$ are independent,
$B_i\sim\Bern(p_i)$, $p_i\in[\alpha,1-\alpha]$,
$\E(U_i\mid B_i)=0$, and
\[
 |h_i/h-1|\le\eta,\qquad |U_i|\le\eta h.
\]
There may also be any finite family of centered variables $Z_r$,
mutually independent and independent of all the pairs, satisfying
\[
 |Z_r|\le\eta h,\qquad \sum_r\Var Z_r\le\eta m h^2.
\]
Then the entire array satisfies
\[
               \mathcal R(X_1,\ldots,X_m,Z_1,\ldots)\le\be.
\]
\end{theorem}

We prove this by considering any sequence with $m\to\infty$,
$p_i\in[\alpha,1-\alpha]$, gap and individual residual bounds
tending to zero, and total extra variance $o(mh^2)$.
If the theorem were false, taking $\eta\downarrow0$ and
$N\to\infty$ would give a violating sequence of exactly this kind.
Within a row put
\[
 v_i=p_i(1-p_i),\qquad
 \tau_i=p_i^2+(1-p_i)^2,\qquad
 V=\sum_i v_i,\qquad T_0=\sum_i v_i\tau_i,\qquad
 \mu=\sum_i p_i .
\]
Scale the whole array by the variance-weighted mean gap
$\bar h=V^{-1}\sum_i v_i h_i$. In the new units write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projected-gaps}
\begin{gathered}
 h_i=1+a_i,\qquad \sum_i v_i a_i=0,\qquad
 K=\sum_i B_i,\\
 A=\sum_i a_i(B_i-p_i),\qquad
 U=\sum_i U_i+\sum_r Z_r,\qquad W=A+U .
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The uncentered sum $S=K+W$ has mean $\mu$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-data}
 \Lambda_A=\sum_i v_i a_i^2,\qquad
 \Lambda_U=\sum_i\E U_i^2+\sum_r\E Z_r^2,\qquad
 \Lambda=\Lambda_A+\Lambda_U,\qquad B^2=V+\Lambda .
\end{equation}
Conditional centering and \eqref{eq:projected-gaps} give exactly
$\Cov(K,W)=0$ and $\Var S=B^2$. Along the sequence,
\[
 V\asymp_\alpha m,\quad T_0\asymp_\alpha m,\quad
 \max_i|a_i|\to0,\quad
 \eps:=\max\{\max_i\|U_i\|_\infty,\max_r\|Z_r\|_\infty\}\to0,
 \quad \Lambda=o(m).
\]
Empty maxima are zero.

Let $T_X$ be the actual sum of absolute third moments of all the
centered coordinates, and let $\kappa$ be their signed third-moment
sum. We will use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget-comparison}
 |T_X-T_0|\le C_\alpha\{\sqrt{m\Lambda_A}+\Lambda\},
 \qquad
 |\kappa-\kappa_0|\le C_\alpha\{\sqrt{m\Lambda_A}+\Lambda\},
 \quad \kappa_0=\sum_i v_i(1-2p_i).
\end{equation}
To prove these bounds, for large rows the signs of
$(1+a_i)(B_i-p_i)+U_i$ on the two clusters agree with those
of $B_i-p_i$. Expand the signed and absolute cubes. The terms
linear in $U_i$ vanish conditionally; the others are bounded by
$C_\alpha\E U_i^2$. The pure two-point absolute third moment is
$(1+a_i)^3v_i\tau_i$, whose difference from $v_i\tau_i$ is
bounded by $C_\alpha v_i(|a_i|+a_i^2)$.
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$\sum_i v_i|a_i|\le\sqrt{V\Lambda_A}$. The signed moment has
the same bounds, and the extra variables contribute at most
$\eps\sum_r\E Z_r^2$. This proves \eqref{eq:budget-comparison}.
In particular $T_X=T_0+o(m)$ and $B^2=V+o(m)$.

\subsection{The count and its conditional moments}\label{subsec:count}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:count}
Uniformly in $p_i\in[\alpha,1-\alpha]$ and integers $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count-local}
 b_k:=\Pp(K=k)=
 \frac{\phi((k-\mu)/\sqrt V)}{\sqrt V}+O_\alpha(m^{-1}),
 \qquad \sup_k b_k\le C_\alpha m^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
For each fixed $M<\infty$, uniformly when $|k-\mu|\le M\sqrt m$,
\begin{align}
 m_k:=\E(A\mid K=k)
     &=O_{\alpha,M}(\sqrt{\Lambda_A}/\sqrt m),\label{eq:cond-mean}\\
 \Var(A\mid K=k)
     &=\Lambda_A\{1+O_{\alpha,M}(m^{-1/2})\},\label{eq:cond-var}\\
 \E[(A-m_k)^4\mid K=k]
     &\le C_{\alpha,M}\Lambda_A^2.             \label{eq:cond-four}
\end{align}
Every error in these last three assertions has the displayed
homogeneous dependence on the weight vector $(a_i)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fourier inversion on $[-\pi,\pi]$ applies to the count. For its
centered characteristic function $F$,
\[
 |F(t)|\le
 \exp\{-2V\sin^2(t/2)\}\le e^{-c_\alpha mt^2}.
\]
On a sufficiently small fixed interval,
$\log F(t)=-Vt^2/2+O_\alpha(m|t|^3)$.
The real remainder can be absorbed in the quadratic term, and hence
the difference from $e^{-Vt^2/2}$ has integral
$O_\alpha(m\int_\R|t|^3e^{-cmt^2}\,dt)=O_\alpha(m^{-1})$.
Off that interval both the count transform and Gaussian integral
have exponentially small tails. Inversion proves the first formula
in \eqref{eq:count-local}, uniformly because its translating phase
has modulus one. Integrating $|F|$ gives the second. The same
statements hold after deleting any fixed number of coordinates.
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delete-ratio}
 \frac{\Pp(K-B_i=k-b)}{\Pp(K=k)}
 =1+O_{\alpha,M}(m^{-1/2}),\qquad b=0,1,\quad
 |k-\mu|\le M\sqrt m.
\end{equation}
Indeed the means, variances and thresholds in numerator and
denominator differ by bounded amounts, their central Gaussian
masses have relative error $O_{\alpha,M}(m^{-1/2})$, and the
absolute error in \eqref{eq:count-local} has that same relative
order in this region.

For a central $k$, choose the unique real $\theta$ such that
\[
 r_i=\frac{p_i e^\theta}{1-p_i+p_i e^\theta},
 \qquad \sum_i r_i=k.
\]
Then $\theta=O_{\alpha,M}(m^{-1/2})$, since the derivative of the
last sum is of order $m$ on a fixed neighborhood of zero.
All $r_i$ remain in one compact subinterval of $(0,1)$.
Tilting by $e^{\theta K}$ leaves the conditional law given $K=k$
unchanged. With expectations under this tilted product law denoted
by $\E_\theta$, set
\[
 w_i=r_i(1-r_i),\qquad V_\theta=\sum_iw_i,\qquad
 c_\theta=\frac{\sum_iw_i a_i}{V_\theta},\qquad b_i'=a_i-c_\theta.
\]
On $K=k$ we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-projection}
 A=\sum_i a_i(r_i-p_i)+L,\qquad
 L=\sum_i b_i'(B_i-r_i),\qquad
 \sum_iw_i b_i'=0.
\end{equation}
The expansions
$r_i-p_i=\theta v_i+O_\alpha(\theta^2v_i)$ and
$w_i=v_i\{1+O_\alpha(|\theta|)\}$ give
\begin{align}
 \left|\sum_i a_i(r_i-p_i)\right|
 &\le C_{\alpha,M}\sqrt{\Lambda_A}/\sqrt m,\label{eq:tilt-drift}\\
 Q_\theta:=\sum_iw_i(b_i')^2
 &=\Lambda_A\{1+O_{\alpha,M}(m^{-1/2})\},\qquad
 Q:=\sum_i(b_i')^2\asymp_\alpha\Lambda_A. \label{eq:tilt-Q}
\end{align}
For the first estimate the linear term is exactly zero by
\eqref{eq:projected-gaps}. For the second, expand
$Q_\theta=\sum_iw_i a_i^2-(\sum_iw_i a_i)^2/V_\theta$;
the subtracted term is $O_{\alpha,M}(\Lambda_A/m)$.
If $\Lambda_A=0$, all the conditional assertions are immediate.
Assume henceforth $Q>0$.

Here are the required Fourier moment estimates. Put
$Z_i'=B_i-r_i$, $f_i(t)=\E_\theta e^{itZ_i'}$, and
$F_\theta(t)=\prod_i f_i(t)$.
For small fixed $|t|$, these factors are bounded away from zero.
Let $c_{i,r}(t)$ be the $r$th derivative at $u=0$ of
\[
 \log\frac{\E_\theta e^{(it+u)Z_i'}}{\E_\theta e^{itZ_i'}}.
\]
Uniformly on that interval,
\[
 c_{i,1}(t)=iw_i t+O_\alpha(t^2),\qquad
 c_{i,2}(t)=w_i+O_\alpha(|t|),\qquad
 |c_{i,3}(t)|+|c_{i,4}(t)|\le C_\alpha .
\]
Define $M_r(t)=\sum_i(b_i')^r c_{i,r}(t)$. Cancellation in
\eqref{eq:tilted-projection}, together with
$\sum_i|b_i'|\le\sqrt{mQ}$ and
$\sum_i|b_i'|^r\le Q^{r/2}$ for $r\ge2$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourier-M}
 \begin{gathered}
 |M_1(t)|\le C_\alpha\sqrt{mQ}\,t^2,\qquad
 M_2(t)=Q_\theta+O_\alpha(Q|t|),\\
 |M_3(t)|\le C_\alpha Q^{3/2},\qquad
 |M_4(t)|\le C_\alpha Q^2.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Differentiating the exponential of the sum of logarithms shows
that the Fourier numerators for $L,L^2,L^4$ are
\begin{align*}
 &F_\theta M_1,\qquad
 F_\theta(M_1^2+M_2),\\
 &F_\theta(M_1^4+6M_1^2M_2+3M_2^2+4M_1M_3+M_4).
\end{align*}
Their integrals over $[-\pi,\pi]$, divided by $2\pi$, are
$\E_\theta[L^r\ind_{\{K=k\}}]$, since
$\sum_i r_i=k$ is an integer.
On the central frequency interval use
$|F_\theta(t)|\le e^{-c_\alpha mt^2}$ and
\[
 \int_\R |t|^r e^{-cmt^2}\,dt=C_{c,r}m^{-(r+1)/2}.
\]
After division by
$\Pp_\theta(K=k)\asymp_\alpha m^{-1/2}$,
the first numerator gives $O(\sqrt Q/\sqrt m)$.
In the second, the constant $Q_\theta F_\theta$, integrated over the full
frequency interval, gives $Q_\theta$ exactly; its omitted central-interval
tail is exponentially small. The $O(Q|t|)$ term gives
$O(Q/\sqrt m)$, and $M_1^2$ gives $O(Q/m)$.
For the fourth numerator, the five displayed terms give,
respectively,
\[
 O(Q^2/m^2),\quad O(Q^2/m),\quad O(Q^2),\quad
 O(Q^2/\sqrt m),\quad O(Q^2).
\]

Away from the central interval, expand
$\E_\theta[L^r e^{it(K-k)}]$ before factoring. Each term contains
at most $r$ chosen coordinate factors and retains exponential
decay from the remaining factors. Its absolute integral is at
most
\[
 C_{\alpha,r}e^{-c_\alpha m}
       \left(\sum_i|b_i'|\right)^r
 \le C_{\alpha,r}e^{-c_\alpha m}(mQ)^{r/2}
 \qquad(r=1,2,4).
\]
This is absorbed in the bounds just obtained after conditioning;
in particular it contains no additive error independent of $Q$.
We have proved
\[
 \E_\theta(L\mid K=k)=O(\sqrt Q/\sqrt m),\quad
 \E_\theta(L^2\mid K=k)=Q_\theta+O(Q/\sqrt m),\quad
 \E_\theta(L^4\mid K=k)\le C Q^2 .
\]
Subtracting the conditional mean, and using
\eqref{eq:tilt-drift}--\eqref{eq:tilt-Q}, proves
\eqref{eq:cond-mean}--\eqref{eq:cond-four}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:residual-moments}
For every fixed $M$, uniformly when $|k-\mu|\le M\sqrt m$,
\[
 \E(W\mid K=k)=m_k,\qquad
 \Var(W\mid K=k)=\Lambda\{1+O_{\alpha,M}(m^{-1/2})\}.
\]
Put
\[
 D=\Lambda^2+\eps^2\Lambda_U,\qquad
 H=\max\left\{\sqrt{\Lambda_A},
          \frac{\Lambda}{\sqrt{\Lambda+\eps^2}}\right\}
 \quad(\Lambda>0).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-moments}
 \E[(W-m_k)^4\mid K=k]\le C_{\alpha,M}D,\qquad
 \E(|W-m_k|\mid K=k)\ge c_{\alpha,M}H,\qquad
 |m_k|\le C_{\alpha,M}H/\sqrt m.
\end{equation}
Also $\E W^4\le C_\alpha D$ without conditioning.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $s_{ib}=\E(U_i^2\mid B_i=b)$. Given the full label vector
$\mathbf b$, the summands in $U$ are independent and centered,
and their variance is
$T_{\mathbf b}=\sum_i s_{i,b_i}+\sum_r\E Z_r^2$.
Since both label probabilities are at least $\alpha$,
$T_{\mathbf b}\le\Lambda_U/\alpha$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:delete-ratio} gives the relative estimate
\[
 \E(T_{\mathbf B}\mid K=k)
      =\Lambda_U\{1+O_{\alpha,M}(m^{-1/2})\}.
\]
For independent centered variables, expansion of the fourth
power gives a fourth moment at most the sum of fourth moments
plus three times the squared variance. Thus
\[
 \E(U^4\mid\mathbf B)\le3T_{\mathbf B}^2+\eps^2T_{\mathbf B},
\]
and averaging preserves the bound
$C_\alpha(\Lambda_U^2+\eps^2\Lambda_U)$.
The conditional covariance between $A$ and $U$ is zero, because
$A$ is determined by the labels and $\E(U\mid\mathbf B)=0$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:count} proves the mean, variance and fourth-moment
claims for $W$.

Interpolation in the form
$\E|Y|\ge(\E Y^2)^{3/2}/(\E Y^4)^{1/2}$ gives
\[
 \E(|W-m_k|\mid K=k)
 \ge c_{\alpha,M}
          \frac{\Lambda}{\sqrt{\Lambda+\eps^2}}.
\]
There is also a bound from the gap residual alone. Conditional
Jensen, first given the full labels, gives
\[
 \E(|A+U-m_k|\mid K=k)
 \ge\E(|A-m_k|\mid K=k)\ge c_{\alpha,M}\sqrt{\Lambda_A},
\]
using \eqref{eq:cond-var}--\eqref{eq:cond-four}.
These prove the bound by $H$, and \eqref{eq:cond-mean} proves
the drift bound. Finally,
$\max_i a_i^2\le\Lambda_A/[\alpha(1-\alpha)]$, so direct fourth
moment expansion gives $\E A^4\le C_\alpha\Lambda_A^2$.
The preceding full-label bound for $U$ then gives
$\E W^4\le C_\alpha D$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Uniform jitter before standardization}\label{subsec:local-jitter}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:unit-jitter}
Along the sequences specified after Theorem~\ref{thm:local}, let $J$
be the CDF of $S+V_1$, where $V_1$ is independent and uniform on
$[-1/2,1/2]$. Uniformly over $x\in\R$, with $z=(x-\mu)/B$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unit-jitter}
 J(x)=\Phi(z)+\frac{\kappa}{6B^3}(1-z^2)\phi(z)
                         +o(m^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
Both signed discrepancies of $S$, multiplied by $B^3/T_X$, are
therefore bounded above by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-envelope}
 \phi(z)\left\{\frac{B^2}{2T_X}
                 +\frac{|\kappa|}{6T_X}|1-z^2|\right\}+o(1).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set $d_m=T_X/B^2$. By \eqref{eq:budget-comparison},
$d_m$ lies eventually in a fixed compact subinterval of $(0,\infty)$,
and each subsequence has a further one with $d_m\to d>0$.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:jitter} to the centered array divided by $B$.
Its Lyapunov ratio is $d_m/B\asymp_\alpha m^{-1/2}$ and each
coordinate is bounded by a fixed multiple of that ratio.
In the measure \eqref{eq:diff-measure}, the variables before
symmetrization are the raw coordinates divided by $d_m$.

The total squared difference between two-cluster coordinates and
$B_i-p_i$, divided by $B^2$, is $O(\Lambda/m)=o(1)$.
The additional coordinates contribute
$\sum_r\Var Z_r/B^2=o(1)$ to this variance-weighted measure.
For the Bernoulli coordinates, the nonzero symmetrized differences
are $1$ and $-1$, each with probability $v_i$; their difference
variance is $2v_i$. Thus the limit of \eqref{eq:diff-measure} is
\[
                    \frac12\delta_{-1/d}+\frac12\delta_{1/d}.
\]
Thus the jitter parameter in Lemma~\ref{lem:jitter} is $h=1/d$.
That lemma gives \eqref{eq:unit-jitter} with raw jitter width $d_m/d\to1$.
Couple this jitter with width one. Their difference is $o(1)$
in raw units, or $o(B^{-1})$ after standardization. Bounding
the two CDFs by shifted versions of one another, using the bounded
derivative of the smooth comparison function, preserves the
$o(m^{-1/2})$ error. The subsequence argument proves the asserted
expansion along the original sequence. Shift the jittered CDF
by $\pm1/2$ to obtain \eqref{eq:local-envelope}, also for the
strict lower branch.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Comparison when the residual variance tends to zero}
\label{subsec:small-residual}
We need a one-sided estimate that keeps the size of the residual
in its error. The following slightly enlarged version of
\citet[Lemma~3.2]{HeCheng2026} follows from their clipping proof;
we include the enlargement to accommodate the conditional mean.

\begin{lemma}[One-sided loss]\label{lem:one-sided}
There are absolute constants $c_0,C,\kappa_*>0$ such that, if
$\E Y=0$, $\E Y^4\le\kappa_*$, and
$f\in C^1([-7/10,7/10])$ satisfies $f(0)=0$ and
$3/4\le f'\le5/4$, then for $|u|\le3/5$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-sided}
 \Pp(Y>u)+f(u)\ge c_0\E|Y|-C\E Y^4,\qquad
 \Pp(Y<u)-f(u)\ge c_0\E|Y|-C\E Y^4.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First let $V$ be centered with $|V|\le a=1/10$ and
$|u|\le13/20$. Writing $V_+=\max(V,0)$, for $u\ge0$ the bounds
$\E V_+\le u+a\Pp(V>u)$ and $f(u)\ge3u/4$ give
$\Pp(V>u)+f(u)\ge3\E|V|/8$. If $u=-r$ with $0<r\le a$, put
$t=\Pp(V>-r)$. Centering implies
$0\le at-r(1-t)$, so $r\le2at$. Also
$\E|V|=2\E V_+\le2at$. Hence
$t+f(-r)\ge t-5r/4\ge3t/4\ge3\E|V|/8$.
For $a<r\le13/20$ the probability is one, and
$1+f(-r)\ge3/16\ge3\E|V|/8$.

For general $Y$, clip it at $a_0=1/20$: put
$T=\max(-a_0,\min(Y,a_0))$, $b=\E T$, and $V=T-b$.
Writing $M_4=\E Y^4$,
\[
 |b|\le a_0^{-3}M_4,\qquad
 \Pp(T\ne Y)\le a_0^{-4}M_4,\qquad
 \E|V|\ge\E|Y|-2a_0^{-3}M_4 .
\]
Choose $\kappa_*$ so that $|b|\le1/20$. Then $|V|\le1/10$
and $|u-b|\le13/20$. Apply the preceding estimate at $u-b$,
using
\[
 \Pp(Y>u)\ge\Pp(V>u-b)-\Pp(T\ne Y),\qquad
 |f(u)-f(u-b)|\le5|b|/4 .
\]
This proves the first inequality. Apply it to $-Y$ and
$-f(-\,\cdot\,)$ to prove the second. The strict inequalities
in the events throughout also cover atoms at $u$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:small-residual}
Along any subsequence of the local arrays for which $\Lambda\to0$,
\[
 \mathcal R(X_1,\ldots,X_m,Z_1,\ldots)
 \le\mathcal R(B_1-p_1,\ldots,B_m-p_m)\le\be
\]
for all sufficiently large rows. The first inequality is strict
when $\Lambda>0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $\Lambda=0$, all residuals vanish and
\eqref{eq:finite-input} applies directly. Consider the rows with
$\Lambda>0$. Write $R_0=\mathcal R(B_1-p_1,\ldots,B_m-p_m)$.
We compare both signed branches at an arbitrary threshold
$x=k+u$, where $k$ is a nearest integer and $|u|\le1/2$.
The exact count decompositions are
\begin{align}
 F_S(k+u)
 &=F_K(k)-b_k\Pp(W>u\mid K=k)+e^+_{k,u},\label{eq:layer-plus}\\
 F_S^-(k+u)
 &=F_K(k-1)+b_k\Pp(W<u\mid K=k)+e^-_{k,u}.\label{eq:layer-minus}
\end{align}
For either remainder, the absolute value is bounded by
\[
 \sum_{j\ne k}b_j
 \Pp\{|W|\ge |j-k|-1/2\mid K=j\}.
\]
Fix $M$. For $|k-\mu|\le M\sqrt m$, split this sum at
$|j-k|\le\sqrt m$. For the nearby count values, the conditional fourth
moment is at most $C_{\alpha,M}D$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-moments}, including the fourth power of
the mean. Use $b_j\le C_\alpha/\sqrt m$ and the convergent
series $\sum_{r\ne0}(|r|-1/2)^{-4}$. For the remote count values,
the denominator is at least $cm^2$, and summing the conditional
fourth moments with their probabilities gives $\E W^4\le C_\alpha D$.
Therefore, uniformly in central $k$ and $|u|\le1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leakage}
                    |e^\pm_{k,u}|\le C_{\alpha,M}D/\sqrt m .
\end{equation}

Put
\[
 G_k(u)=\Phi((k-\mu+u)/B),\qquad
 f_k(u)=\{G_k(u)-G_k(0)\}/b_k .
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:count-local} implies
$3/4\le f_k'(u)\le5/4$ on $[-1,1]$ for all sufficiently
large rows, uniformly in central $k$. Indeed,
$f_k'(u)=\phi((k-\mu+u)/B)/(Bb_k)$, and $B^2=V+\Lambda$
with $\Lambda\to0$. This use of the relative count estimate
introduces no additive discrepancy error.

Let $m_k=\E(W\mid K=k)$ and
$Y=W-m_k$ under this conditional law. For large rows
$|m_k|\le1/20$ and $\E(Y^4\mid K=k)\le\kappa_*$.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:one-sided} to
$\widetilde f(v)=f_k(v+m_k)-f_k(m_k)$ at $v=u-m_k$.
The derivative bound on $[-1,1]$ covers all its evaluations.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-moments}, both losses satisfy
\begin{align}
 \Pp(W>u\mid K=k)+f_k(u)&\ge c_{\alpha,M}H
                                  -C_{\alpha,M}(D+|m_k|),\notag\\
 \Pp(W<u\mid K=k)-f_k(u)&\ge c_{\alpha,M}H
                                  -C_{\alpha,M}(D+|m_k|).
                                                        \label{eq:conditional-loss}
\end{align}

Substitute \eqref{eq:conditional-loss} into
\eqref{eq:layer-plus}--\eqref{eq:layer-minus}, subtract the
normal CDF, and multiply by $B^3/T_X\asymp_\alpha\sqrt m$.
The product $(B^3/T_X)b_k$ is bounded above and below by positive
constants on this central region. Changing the normal variance
from $V$ to $V+\Lambda$ costs at most
$C_\alpha\Lambda/m$ before this multiplication, because
$\sup_z|z\phi(z)|<\infty$. Changing the prefactor from
$V^{3/2}/T_0$ to $B^3/T_X$ costs at most
\[
 C_\alpha\{\sqrt{\Lambda_A}/\sqrt m+\Lambda/m\}
\]
after normalization: use \eqref{eq:budget-comparison} and the
bound $R_0\le C_U$. Including \eqref{eq:leakage} and the
conditional drift, either normalized central branch is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:central-comparison}
 R_0-c_{\alpha,M}H+
 C_{\alpha,M}\left\{D+
                \frac{\sqrt{\Lambda_A}+\Lambda}{\sqrt m}\right\}.
\end{equation}
All these errors are absorbable relative to $H$, since
\begin{equation}\label{eq:absorption}
 \frac{D}{H}\le(\Lambda+\eps^2)^{3/2},\qquad
 \frac{\sqrt{\Lambda_A}}{H}\le1,\qquad
 \frac{\Lambda}{H}\le\sqrt{\Lambda+\eps^2}.
\end{equation}
For sufficiently large rows, \eqref{eq:central-comparison} is
therefore at most $R_0-c_{\alpha,M}H/2$.
This conclusion holds for every positive $\Lambda$, however
small its value relative to $m$.

It remains to restrict the threshold to a fixed central region.
A jump of size $b_k$ gives a signed discrepancy on one of its
two sides of at least $b_k/2$. Choose $k$ nearest to $\mu$
in \eqref{eq:count-local}; since $T_0\le V$,
\[
          R_0\ge\frac{V^{3/2}}{2T_0}\max_k b_k
                  \ge\phz/4
\]
for large $m$. Meanwhile \eqref{eq:local-envelope} is bounded
by $C_\alpha(1+z^2)\phi(z)+o(1)$, uniformly over all thresholds.
Choose a fixed large radius so that this is below $\phz/8$
outside the radius for large rows. Since $B\asymp_\alpha\sqrt m$,
choose $M$ to contain the remaining thresholds and their nearest
integers. The far branches are at most $R_0-\phz/8$.
Together with the central comparison this proves the strict
inequality for $\Lambda>0$. This is the exact-loss comparison of
\citet[Lemma~3.3]{HeCheng2026}, with the conditional estimates
above in place of iid conditional symmetry.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Positive mass in intervals of fixed length}
\label{subsec:local-mass}
We next treat subsequences for which $\Lambda\ge\Lambda_*>0$.
The variance may grow, but remains $o(m)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:local-mass}
Fix $\Lambda_*>0$, $R<\infty$, and real numbers $a<b$.
Along a local sequence with $\Lambda\ge\Lambda_*$ there is
$c_*>0$, depending on these fixed parameters and $\alpha$, such
that eventually
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-mass}
 \inf_{|x-\mu|\le R\sqrt m}
          \Pp\{S\in(x+a,x+b)\}\ge c_*m^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For each $x$ in the indicated region choose all integers $k$
with $|k-x|\le r$, where $r=\max(1,\sqrt\Lambda)$.
There are at least $\sqrt\Lambda$ such integers. Since
$\Lambda=o(m)$, they lie in a fixed enlarged central region
for large rows, and each satisfies $b_k\ge c_{\alpha,R}/\sqrt m$.
Tilt the labels to mean $k$ and use the projection from the
proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:count}. Conditional on $K=k$,
\[
 W=m_k^0+R_k,\qquad
 m_k^0=\sum_i a_i(r_i-p_i),\qquad
 R_k=\sum_i\{b_i'(B_i-r_i)+U_i\}+\sum_r Z_r .
\]
Under the tilted independent law,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-data}
 \E_\theta R_k=0,\quad
 \Cov_\theta(K,R_k)=0,\quad
 L_k:=\Var_\theta R_k
   =\Lambda\{1+O_{\alpha,R}(m^{-1/2})\},\quad
 |m_k^0|\le C_{\alpha,R}\sqrt{\Lambda_A}/\sqrt m .
\end{equation}
The variance comparison follows from \eqref{eq:tilt-Q} and the
relative $O(m^{-1/2})$ change in each label probability; the
extra variables are unchanged. Every individual summand of
$R_k$ is bounded by a number $\delta_m\to0$, uniformly over
these $k$.

We prove a conditional local estimate for a fixed continuous
integrable function $g$ with integrable Fourier transform supported
in $[-T,T]$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-local}
 \sup_{y\in\R}\left|
 \E_\theta[g(R_k-y)\mid K=k]
       -\E g(\sqrt{L_k}\,Z-y)\right|
                  =o(L_k^{-1/2}),\qquad Z\sim N(0,1).
\end{equation}
All errors here are uniform over the selected values of $k$.
Let
$\chi_k(s,t)=\E_\theta e^{is(K-k)+itR_k}$.
For a sufficiently small fixed $\delta>0$, symmetrization of
each independent pair gives, for large rows,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-bound}
 |\chi_k(s,t)|
 \le \exp\{-c_\alpha(V_\theta s^2+L_k t^2)\},
 \qquad |s|\le\delta,\quad |t|\le T .
\end{equation}
In detail, each pair phase has diameter at most
$|s|+2T\delta_m$. For this diameter small enough,
$1-\cos u\ge c u^2$ bounds its characteristic-function
modulus by the exponential of minus a constant times its
phase variance. The sum of those variances is exactly
$V_\theta s^2+L_k t^2$, by the zero covariance in
\eqref{eq:joint-data}. Extra coordinates have count component
zero and contribute their actual variance to $L_k$.
For $\delta\le|s|\le\pi$, each factor corresponding to a pair $(B_i,U_i)$ differs
from $\E_\theta e^{is(B_i-r_i)}$ by at most $T\delta_m$.
The latter moduli are bounded away from one uniformly over
this compact range of $s$ and the allowed $r_i$. The product
is therefore $O(e^{-cm})$; additional factors have modulus
at most one.

For bounded $u,v$, at $s=u/\sqrt{V_\theta}$ and
$t=v/\sqrt{L_k}$ the sum of absolute third phase moments is
at most
\[
 C_\alpha\left\{
    \frac{|u|^3}{\sqrt m}
       +\frac{\delta_m|v|^3}{\sqrt{L_k}}\right\}=o(1),
\]
because $L_k\ge\Lambda_*/2$ eventually. The logarithmic
characteristic-function expansion hence gives
$\chi_k(s,t)-e^{-V_\theta s^2/2-L_k t^2/2}\to0$ uniformly
on bounded scaled frequency sets. The Gaussian majorant
\eqref{eq:joint-bound} makes its integrated absolute difference
on $[-\delta,\delta]\times[-T,T]$ equal to
$o((V_\theta L_k)^{-1/2})$: after the change of variables,
first restrict to a bounded rectangle and then use Gaussian
tails. The other count frequencies are exponentially small
at this scale, since $L_k=o(m)$.

Fourier inversion in the integer count coordinate and in the
residual coordinate gives
\[
 \E_\theta[g(R_k-y)\ind_{\{K=k\}}]
 =\frac1{(2\pi)^2}
   \int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-T}^T
       \widehat g(t)e^{ity}\chi_k(s,-t)\,dt\,ds .
\]
The translating factor has modulus one and
$|\widehat g|\le\|g\|_1$. The preceding integrated estimate
therefore applies uniformly in $y$. Replacing $\chi_k$ by
the Gaussian product gives
$(2\pi V_\theta)^{-1/2}\E g(\sqrt{L_k}Z-y)$, up to
an exponentially small count tail. Divide by
\[
 \Pp_\theta(K=k)
    =(2\pi V_\theta)^{-1/2}\{1+O_\alpha(m^{-1/2})\},
\]
from \eqref{eq:count-local}. The denominator replacement is
also $o(L_k^{-1/2})$, since
$|\E g(\sqrt{L_k}Z-y)|\le\phz\|g\|_1/\sqrt{L_k}$.
This proves \eqref{eq:conditional-local}, including when
$L_k\to\infty$.

To obtain an interval probability, put $w_0=(a+b)/2$ and
$d=(b-a)/2$, and for a fixed large $\omega>0$ define
\[
 g(w)=\left(1-\frac{(w-w_0)^2}{d^2}\right)
             \left(\frac{\sin(\omega(w-w_0))}{\omega(w-w_0)}\right)^4 .
\]
Then $g\le\ind_{(a,b)}$, including at the endpoints, and
$g\in L^1(\R)$. The Fourier transform of the fourth power
of sinc is a compactly supported cubic spline. Multiplication
by the quadratic corresponds to two derivatives, so
$\widehat g$ is continuous, integrable and supported in
$[-4\omega,4\omega]$.
For any fixed $C_0$, choose $\omega$ sufficiently large that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minorant}
 \E g(\sqrt\lambda Z-y)\ge c_1/\sqrt\lambda
 \quad\left(\lambda\ge\Lambda_*/2,\quad
                   |y|\le C_0\sqrt\lambda\right).
\end{equation}
Here is a direct lower bound. On $|w-w_0|\le1/\omega$ the
positive part of $g$ has integral at least $c/\omega$.
The Gaussian density there is bounded below by
$c_{\Lambda_*,C_0,a,b}/\sqrt\lambda$. The negative part
has integral at most
$2/(d^3\omega^4)$, while the density is at most
$\phz/\sqrt\lambda$. Fixing $\omega$ large enough makes the
positive contribution dominate and proves \eqref{eq:minorant}.

For each chosen $k$ set $y=x-k-m_k^0$.
By \eqref{eq:joint-data} and $|k-x|\le r$,
$|y|\le C_{\Lambda_*,R}\sqrt{L_k}$.
Use \eqref{eq:conditional-local}--\eqref{eq:minorant} to get
\[
 \Pp\{W\in(x+a-k,x+b-k)\mid K=k\}
 \ge c_2/\sqrt\Lambda .
\]
Each chosen value of $K$ has probability at least $c_{\alpha,R}/\sqrt m$,
and there are at least $\sqrt\Lambda$ such values. Summing proves
\eqref{eq:local-mass}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:large-residual}
Along a local sequence with $\Lambda\ge\Lambda_*>0$,
\[
 \limsup\mathcal R(X_1,\ldots,X_m,Z_1,\ldots)<\be .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The uniform-jitter identities, with strict events, are
\begin{align*}
 J(x+1/2)-F_S(x)
   &=\E[(x+1-S)\ind_{\{x<S<x+1\}}],\\
 F_S^-(x)-J(x-1/2)
   &=\E[(S-x+1)\ind_{\{x-1<S<x\}}].
\end{align*}
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-mass}, applied for example to the intervals
$(x+1/4,x+1/2)$ and $(x-1/2,x-1/4)$, makes both quantities
at least $c_R/\sqrt m$ in any fixed central region.
Combine this with \eqref{eq:unit-jitter} and Taylor expansion
at the shifts $\pm1/2$. Multiplying by $B^3/T_X$, either
central branch is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strict-envelope}
          \frac{\phz(3B^2+|\kappa|)}{6T_X}-c_R'+o(1).
\end{equation}
The Bernoulli case of \eqref{eq:esseen-moment}, with span one,
gives
$3V+|\kappa_0|\le\ce T_0$. Equations
\eqref{eq:budget-comparison} and $\Lambda=o(m)$ show that
the first term of \eqref{eq:strict-envelope} is at most
$\be+o(1)$. Outside a sufficiently large fixed standardized
region, the ordinary envelope \eqref{eq:local-envelope} is
less than $\be/2$ for large rows. Choose this region first,
then its positive central loss $c_R'$. This proves the strict
upper limit. The interval-loss step corresponds to
\citet[Lemmas~3.4--3.5]{HeCheng2026}; the two-dimensional
calculation above supplies the mass bound for unequal probabilities
and gaps.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:local}]
If the theorem failed, select violating rows with $m_j\ge j$,
gap and residual bounds at most $1/j$, and extra variance at
most $m_jh^2/j$. After the common normalization
\eqref{eq:projected-gaps}, these have exactly the sequential
hypotheses used above. If $\Lambda_j$ failed to tend to zero,
a subsequence would have $\Lambda_j\ge\Lambda_*>0$, contradicting
Proposition~\ref{prop:large-residual}. Thus $\Lambda_j\to0$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:small-residual} and
\eqref{eq:finite-input} then contradict the strict violations.
\end{proof}

\section{Completion of the proof}\label{sec:completion}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
If violations existed with arbitrarily small Lyapunov ratio,
Lemma~\ref{lem:attainment} would produce exact penalized
maximizers with $R_j>\be$ and $\ell_j\to0$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:support-bound} gives their common support
bound. Proposition~\ref{prop:equality} and the support arguments
of Section~\ref{sec:confinement} put every sufficiently late
row in the class of Theorem~\ref{thm:local}: use the fixed
nominal gap $h=1/\te$, the probability interval from
Proposition~\ref{prop:partition}, and
$m_j\asymp\ell_j^{-2}\to\infty$. The remaining coordinates
have shrinking support and total scaled variance $o(m_j)$,
as required. Theorem~\ref{thm:local}, which uses all actual
variances and third absolute moments, therefore gives
$R_j\le\be$, a contradiction. Some universal $\ell_0>0$
must exist. The Esseen iid sequence described in
Section~\ref{sec:theorem} proves optimality.
\end{proof}

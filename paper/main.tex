\begin{abstract}
Let $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be independent centered real random variables with
positive total variance $V=\sum_{j=1}^n\E X_j^2$ and finite third absolute
moments $R=\sum_{j=1}^n\E|X_j|^3$. We prove that the Kolmogorov distance
$\Delta$ between the standardized sum $V^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^nX_j$ and the
standard normal distribution satisfies $\Delta\le U_*L$, where
$L=R/V^{3/2}$ is the Lyapunov ratio and $U_*<\UpperBound$.
The proof combines Prawitz's smoothing inequality with two refinements of
the zero-bias characteristic-function method. First, we measure each
summand's departure from a symmetric two-point distribution by its
third-moment excess and aggregate the resulting single-summand zero-bias
bounds by their least concave majorant. Second, we decompose the difference
between the sum's characteristic function and the Gaussian characteristic
function through the product of cosines $\prod_{j=1}^n\cos(t\sqrt{\E X_j^2/V})$,
which is real and lies below $e^{-t^2/2}$ before its first zero; this
allows the smoothing integral to retain the sign of the reference
displacement while bounding the comparison error through a first-order
differential inequality. Isolating a summand of largest variance sharpens
both the product decay and the comparison forcing bound. Analytic estimates
cover small and large values of $L$, and a rigorous interval computation
covers the intermediate moment domain.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and main result}\label{sec:statement}

\subsection{The Berry--Esseen problem and main theorem}

Let $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be a finite family of independent centered real random
variables satisfying
\[
  0<V=\sum_{j=1}^n\E X_j^2<\infty,
  \qquad R=\sum_{j=1}^n\E|X_j|^3<\infty.
\]
Denote by $\Phi$ and $\phi$ the standard normal distribution function and
density, and define the standardized sum, the Lyapunov ratio, and the
Kolmogorov normal-approximation error by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:problem}
  S=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^nX_j}{\sqrt V},\qquad
  L=\frac{R}{V^{3/2}},\qquad
  \Delta=\sup_{x\in\R}|\Prob(S\le x)-\Phi(x)|.
\end{equation}
The universal Berry--Esseen constant for independent summands is
\[
  \Cind=\sup\frac{\Delta}{L},
\]
where the supremum is taken over all $n\ge1$ and all independent centered
arrays $(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ satisfying \eqref{eq:problem}.

In 1956, \citet{Esseen1956} proved that among identically distributed
summands, the largest asymptotic value of $\Delta/L$ as $n\to\infty$ is
achieved by two-point distributions with atom probabilities
$(4-\sqrt{10})/2$ and $(\sqrt{10}-2)/2$, yielding the lower bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:esseen}
  \CE=\frac{\sqrt{10}+3}{6\sqrt{2\pi}}
      =0.40973218370239634299\ldots\le\Cind;
\end{equation}
see also the discussion in \citet{Shevtsova2012}. It is a longstanding open
conjecture that $\Cind=\CE$. For unrestricted independent summands, the
best previously published upper bound is $\Cind\le0.5583$, due to
\citet{Shevtsova2013}.

The main result of this paper is the following unconditional upper bound.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:upper-bound}
For every finite independent array satisfying \eqref{eq:problem},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upper-bound}
  \begin{gathered}
   \Delta\le U_*L,\qquad \CE\le\Cind\le U_*<\UpperBound,\\
   U_*=\ExactUpper.
  \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Overview of the approach and open questions}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:upper-bound} builds on the zero-bias
transformation introduced by \citet{GoldsteinReinert1997} and the
characteristic-function and smoothing methods developed by \citet{Prawitz1972,Prawitz1975},
\citet{Tyurin2009}, \citet{Goldstein2010}, and \citet{Shevtsova2012}.
When $S$ is normalized to have variance one, its characteristic function
$f_S(t)=\E e^{\ii tS}$ satisfies $f_S'(t)=-tf_{S^*}(t)$, where $S^*$ has the
zero-biased distribution of $S$. Comparing this identity with the Gaussian
differential equation $(e^{-t^2/2})'=-te^{-t^2/2}$ gives
\[
  f_S(t)-e^{-t^2/2}
  =e^{-t^2/2}\int_0^t u\bigl(f_S(u)-f_{S^*}(u)\bigr)e^{u^2/2}\,du.
\]
By the independent-sum replacement property of zero biasing,
\[
  f_S(u)-f_{S^*}(u)
  =\sum_{i=1}^n v_i\bigl(f_{X_i}(u)-f_{X_i^*}(u)\bigr)\prod_{j\ne i}f_{X_j}(u),
\]
where $v_i=\E X_i^2$. In classical zero-bias arguments, one bounds the
modulus of each summand difference by $|f_{X_i}(u)-f_{X_i^*}(u)|\le u\E|X_i|^3/(2v_i)$
and multiplies by an upper bound on $\prod_{j\ne i}|f_{X_j}(u)|$.

While this direct modulus bound is sharp for a single two-point summand as
$u\to0$, it leaves three substantial sources of overestimation at moderate
frequencies and unequal variances:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item In the single-factor bound $|f_{X_j}(t)|\le\sqrt{1-2q_j(t)}$, passing to
the linear exponential bound $\exp(-\sum_jq_j(t))$ discards the nonlinear
terms in $-\frac12\log(1-2q_j)=q_j+q_j^2+\cdots$.
\item Bounding $|f_{X_i}(u)-f_{X_i^*}(u)|$ by $u\E|X_i|^3/(2v_i)$ ignores the
fact that when $\E|X_i|^3$ is close to its minimum $v_i^{3/2}$, the law of
$X_i/\sqrt{v_i}$ must be close to the symmetric Rademacher distribution on
$\{-1,1\}$, for which $(f_W(x)-f_{W^*}(x))/x=(\cos x-\sinc x)/x$ has a
strictly smaller coefficient than $1/2$ for $x>0$.
\item Taking the absolute value $|f_S(t)-e^{-t^2/2}|$ inside Prawitz's
smoothing integral discards the sign of the characteristic-function
displacement when the summands are nearly symmetric two-point variables.
\end{enumerate}

Our proof addresses each of these three mechanisms in turn:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Logarithmic product bounds (Section~\ref{sec:products}):} We
retain both the quadratic term $\sum_jq_j^2$ and certified tangent-line
lower bounds for the full series $-\frac12\log(1-2q_j)-q_j$, expressed in
terms of $L$ and the variance power sum $\tau=\sum_{j=1}^n v_j^{3/2}$.
\item \textbf{Third-moment excess and concave majorants
(Sections~\ref{sec:directional}--\ref{sec:current-scalar}):} For a
mean-zero, variance-one variable $W$ with $\rho=\E|W|^3$, the identity
$\E[(|W|-1)^2(|W|+1/2)]=\rho-1\ge0$ controls how far $f_W(x)$ and
$f_{W^*}(x)$ can deviate from $\cos x$ and $\sinc x$. We derive a two-sided
interval for $\Re(f_W-f_{W^*})$, a quadratic bound in $e=\rho-1$ for
$|\Im(f_W-f_{W^*})|^2$, and certified affine modulus and directional bounds.
Taking the least concave majorant in $e$ aggregates these single-summand
bounds across arrays with arbitrary variances.
\item \textbf{Isolation of a largest-variance summand and signed cosine
comparison (Sections~\ref{sec:distinguished}--\ref{sec:current-reference}):}
Writing $d=\max_j v_j$ and $b$ for the third absolute moment of a summand
achieving variance $d$, we separate that summand from the remaining array in
both the zero-bias product and the reference comparison. Following the
symmetric Bernoulli approximation studied by \citet[Theorem~1.2 and Example~1.4]{MattnerShevtsova2019},
we compare $f_S(t)$ with the cosine product $C_v(t)=\prod_{j=1}^n\cos(t\sqrt{v_j})$
via a first-order differential equation. Because $C_v(t)-e^{-t^2/2}\le0$ is
real prior to the first zero of the cosines, its negative sign is preserved
in the threshold-localized signed Prawitz smoothing inequality.
\item \textbf{Smoothing and certified interval cover
(Sections~\ref{sec:smoothing}--\ref{sec:certificate}):} Analytic estimates
bound $\Delta/L$ for $L\le L_-\approx0.0014$ (using Corollary~4.18 of
\citet{Shevtsova2012}) and for $L\ge L_+\approx1.21$ (using Cantelli's
inequality). Over $L\in[L_-,L_+]$, a rigorous interval computation partitions
the feasible region of $(L,d,b,\tau)$ into closed boxes and verifies
$\Delta/L\le U_*<\UpperBound$ on every box.
\end{itemize}

Closing the remaining gap between $\CE\approx0.409732$ and $U_*<\UpperBound$
on the intermediate range of $L$ remains open. In Fourier-smoothing
arguments, the principal remaining source of loss is the triangle inequality
applied across summands in the zero-bias and cosine-reference forcing terms,
which discards phase cancellation between summands of different skewnesses
and lattice spans. Companion papers in this collection establish the sharp
bound $\Delta\le\CE L$ for $L\le\exp(-\exp(97))$, for arrays of at most two
arbitrary summands ($C_2<49/120<\CE$), and for several classes of two-point
arrays.

\subsection{Normalization and moment parameters}

After replacing each $X_j$ by $X_j/\sqrt V$ and omitting any summands of
variance zero, we have $S=\sum_{j=1}^nX_j$ with total variance one. Write
\[
 v_j=\E X_j^2,\qquad b_j=\E|X_j|^3,\qquad
 \tau=\sum_{j=1}^nv_j^{3/2},\qquad \sum_{j=1}^nv_j=1,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^nb_j=L.
\]
By Lyapunov's inequality, each difference $b_j-v_j^{3/2}$ is nonnegative,
vanishing if and only if $X_j$ is a symmetric two-point variable on
$\{-\sqrt{v_j},\sqrt{v_j}\}$, and $\sum_{j=1}^n(b_j-v_j^{3/2})=L-\tau\ge0$.

Relabel the summands so that $v_1=\max_{1\le j\le n}v_j$, and set $d=v_1$
and $b=b_1$. The four moment parameters $(L,d,b,\tau)$ satisfy the necessary
constraints
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-domain}
 b\ge d^{3/2},\qquad
 d^{3/2}\le\tau\le\min\{L-b+d^{3/2},\sqrt d\},\qquad \tau\le L.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $b_j\ge v_j^{3/2}$ for $j\ge2$ implies $\tau-d^{3/2}\le L-b$, while
$v_j\le d$ implies $\tau\le\sqrt d\sum_{j=1}^nv_j=\sqrt d$; in particular,
$\tau^2\le d\le\tau^{2/3}$. For the subarray of remaining summands
$j\in\{2,\ldots,n\}$, we define the remainder parameters
\begin{equation}\label{eq:remainder-state}
 w=1-d,\qquad B_R=L-b,\qquad \tau_R=\tau-d^{3/2},\qquad
 a_R=\min\{d,w,B_R^{2/3},\tau_R^{2/3}\}.
\end{equation}
Every remaining summand $j\ge2$ satisfies $v_j\le d$, $v_j\le w$, and
$v_j^{3/2}\le\min\{b_j,\tau_R\}\le\min\{B_R,\tau_R\}$, so that $v_j\le a_R$.
Every finite independent array realizes a parameter tuple $(L,d,b,\tau)$ in
 the domain defined by \eqref{eq:moment-domain}--\eqref{eq:remainder-state},
and an upper bound valid across this domain bounds $\Delta/L$ for every
array.

\section{Bounds for characteristic-function products}\label{sec:products}

For a real random variable $X$, write $f_X(t)=\E e^{\ii tX}$ for its
characteristic function, and set $f_j=f_{X_j}$. Because
$f_X(-t)=\overline{f_X(t)}$, it suffices to bound $|f_X(t)|$ for $t\ge0$.
Given a nonempty subset of indices $J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$, put
\[
 V_0=\sum_{j\in J}v_j,\qquad B=\sum_{j\in J}b_j,\qquad
 \tau_0=\sum_{j\in J}v_j^{3/2},
\]
and let $a>0$ be an upper bound on $\max_{j\in J}v_j$. We require upper
bounds both for the full product $\prod_{j\in J}|f_j(t)|$ and for the
deleted products $\prod_{j\in J\setminus\{i\}}|f_j(t)|$ obtained by omitting
any single index $i\in J$ (with empty products equal to $1$). Deleted
products enter directly through the zero-bias identity \eqref{eq:sum-zero-bias},
which implies
\[
 |f_S(t)-f_{S^*}(t)|\le
 \sum_{i=1}^n v_i|f_{X_i}(t)-f_{X_i^*}(t)|\prod_{j\ne i}|f_j(t)|.
\]

\subsection{Cubic and trigonometric bounds}

Define the constant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa}
 \kappa_* = \sup_{x>0}\frac{\cos x-1+x^2/2}{x^3},
 \qquad \kappa=\frac{49581}{500000}=0.099162,
\end{equation}
and let $\theta\in(\pi,2\pi)$ denote the unique maximizer in
\eqref{eq:kappa}. For $V_0,E_0>0$, $t\ge0$, and $u=E_0t/V_0$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tyurin-exponent}
 \psi(t;V_0,E_0)=
 \begin{cases}
   V_0t^2/2-\kappa_*E_0t^3,&u<\theta,\\
   V_0^3E_0^{-2}(1-\cos u),&\theta\le u\le2\pi,\\
   0,&u>2\pi.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The characteristic-function inequalities of \citet{Prawitz1975}, in the form
given by \citet[Lemma~1]{Tyurin2009}, yield for any centered random variable
$X$ of variance $v>0$ and third absolute moment $b\ge v^{3/2}$,
\begin{align}
 \Big|\prod_{j\in J} f_{X_j}(t)\Big|
   &\le \exp\{-\psi(t;V_0,B+\tau_0)\},\label{eq:aggregate-cf}\\
 |f_X(t)|&\le A(t;v,b)
   :=\sqrt{1-2\psi(t;v,b+v^{3/2})}.\label{eq:single-cf}
\end{align}

Let us record the monotonicity properties of $\psi$ used to evaluate
\eqref{eq:aggregate-cf}--\eqref{eq:single-cf} on parameter intervals. For fixed
$(V_0,E_0)$, $\psi(t;V_0,E_0)$ increases with $t$ on $[0,V_0/(3\kappa_*E_0)]$,
decreases on $[V_0/(3\kappa_*E_0),2\pi V_0/E_0]$, and vanishes for
$t\ge2\pi V_0/E_0$: indeed, $\partial_t\psi=t(V_0-3\kappa_*E_0t)$ on the
cubic branch and $\partial_t\psi=(V_0^2/E_0)\sin u\le0$ on the cosine
branch, with matching values at $u=\theta$ by the stationarity condition for
$\theta$. Consequently, the upper bounds in \eqref{eq:aggregate-cf} and
\eqref{eq:single-cf} attain their maxima over any closed frequency interval
at one of its endpoints. With respect to the parameters $(V_0,E_0)$, we have
on the cosine branch
\begin{align*}
 \partial_{V_0}\psi
 &=\frac{V_0^2}{E_0^2}\{3(1-\cos u)-u\sin u\}\ge0,\\
 \partial_{E_0}\psi
 &=\frac{V_0^3}{E_0^3}\{u\sin u-2(1-\cos u)\}\le0,
\end{align*}
and the same signs hold on the cubic branch. Thus $\psi(t;V_0,E_0)$ is
nondecreasing in $V_0$ and nonincreasing in $E_0$.

To verify that the supremum in \eqref{eq:kappa} is uniquely attained and
bounded by $\kappa$, set $r(x)=(\cos x-1+x^2/2)/x^3$. Differentiation gives
\[
 r'(x)=-\frac{h(x)}{2x^4},\quad
 h(x)=x^2+2x\sin x+6(\cos x-1),\quad
 h'(x)=4\cos(x/2)\{x\cos(x/2)-2\sin(x/2)\}.
\]
Because $x\cos(x/2)-2\sin(x/2)$ is strictly negative on $(0,2\pi)$, $h$
decreases on $(0,\pi)$ and increases on $(\pi,2\pi)$, with $h(\pi)=\pi^2-12<0$
and $h(2\pi)=4\pi^2>0$. Thus $h$ has a unique zero $\theta\in(\pi,2\pi)$.
For $x\ge2\pi$, $r(x)\le1/(4\pi)<3/32\le r(4)$, so $\theta$ is the unique
global maximizer of $r$ on $(0,\infty)$. The scalar checker isolates
$\theta\in(3.99,4)$ and encloses $\kappa_*<0.099161913515<\kappa$ using Arb
ball arithmetic \citep{Johansson2017}. In interval evaluations, the cubic
lower bound $V_0t^2/2-\kappa E_0t^3\le\psi(t;V_0,E_0)$ is valid at all
frequencies, and the cosine branch is used when the entire interval of $u$
lies in $[4,2\pi]\subset[\theta,2\pi]$.

\subsection{Quadratic logarithmic correction}

For each $j\in J$, define
\[
 h_j=\frac{v_jt^2}{2}-\kappa(b_j+v_j^{3/2})t^3,
 \qquad q_j=\max(h_j,0),\qquad
 H=\frac{V_0t^2}{2}-\kappa(B+\tau_0)t^3.
\]
Since $b_j\ge v_j^{3/2}$ and $v_j\le a$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q-cap}
 0\le q_j\le m(t,a):=
 \max_{0\le x\le t\sqrt a}(x^2/2-2\kappa x^3)
 \le\frac1{216\kappa^2}<\frac12,
\end{equation}
where the maximum in $m(t,a)$ occurs at $x=\min(t\sqrt a,1/(6\kappa))$.
From \eqref{eq:single-cf} we have $|f_j(t)|\le\sqrt{1-2q_j}$. Applying the
inequality $-\tfrac12\log(1-2q)\ge q+q^2$ for $0\le q<1/2$ and noting that
$\sum_{j\in J}q_j\ge\sum_{j\in J}h_j=H$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:log-product}
 \prod_{j\in J}|f_j(t)|\le\exp\left\{-H-\sum_{j\in J}q_j^2\right\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:quadratic-charge}
Set $A_0=t^2/2-2\kappa\sqrt a\,t^3$. When $A_0\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q}
 \sum_{j\in J}q_j^2\ge
 Q:=\left(\max\left\{0,
       A_0\frac{\tau_0}{\sqrt{V_0}}-\kappa t^3(B-\tau_0)
     \right\}\right)^2.
\end{equation}
When $A_0<0$, the bound \eqref{eq:Q} holds with $Q=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $v_j\le a$ and $q_j\ge h_j$, each summand satisfies
\[
 q_j+\kappa t^3(b_j-v_j^{3/2})
 \ge v_j(t^2/2-2\kappa\sqrt{v_j}\,t^3)\ge A_0v_j.
\]
When $A_0\ge0$, the triangle inequality in $\ell_2(J)$ gives
\[
 \|(q_j)\|_2\ge A_0\|(v_j)\|_2-\kappa t^3(B-\tau_0).
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz, $\tau_0=\sum_{j\in J}v_j^{3/2}\le\sqrt{V_0}\|(v_j)\|_2$.
Substituting this lower bound for $\|(v_j)\|_2$, taking the positive part,
and squaring yields \eqref{eq:Q}.
\end{proof}

When a single index $i\in J$ is omitted, the linear sum satisfies
$\sum_{j\ne i}q_j\ge H-h_i\ge H-m(t,a)$, while the quadratic sum satisfies
$\sum_{j\ne i}q_j^2\ge\max(0,Q-m(t,a)^2)$. We therefore obtain the full and
deleted product bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrected-products}
 \begin{split}
 F_0(t;V_0,B,a,\tau_0)&=\min\{1,e^{-H-Q}\},\\
 D_0(t;V_0,B,a,\tau_0)&=\min\{1,e^{-H+m(t,a)-\max(0,Q-m(t,a)^2)}\},
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
setting $F_0=D_0=1$ for the empty index set.

To maximize the bounds \eqref{eq:corrected-products} as $\tau_0$ varies over
a closed interval $[\ell,h]$, suppose $A_0\ge0$ and $t>0$, and put
$k=\kappa t^3$, $a_0=A_0/\sqrt{V_0}+k$, and $c_0=kB$. Up to terms
independent of $\tau_0$, the negative of the exponent in either bound is
\[
 k\tau_0-\max\{0,(a_0\tau_0-c_0)_+^2-m_0^2\},
 \qquad z_+=\max(z,0),
\]
with $m_0=0$ for $F_0$ and $m_0=m(t,a)$ for $D_0$. This function of $\tau_0$
has derivative $k>0$ for $a_0\tau_0-c_0\le m_0$ and derivative
$k-2a_0(a_0\tau_0-c_0)$ for $a_0\tau_0-c_0>m_0$, so its maximum on
$[\ell,h]$ is attained at
\[
 \min\{h,\max\{\ell,\tau_*\}\},\qquad
 \tau_*=\frac{c_0+\max\{m_0,k/(2a_0)\}}{a_0}.
\]
When $A_0<0$, $Q=0$ and the expression increases linearly with $\tau_0$.

\subsection{Tangent lower bounds for the full logarithmic series}\label{sec:current-log}

To capture the higher-order terms in $-\tfrac12\log(1-2q)$ beyond the
quadratic term, put
\[
 g(q)=-\tfrac12\log(1-2q)-q=\sum_{p=2}^\infty\frac{2^{p-1}}p q^p,\qquad 0\le q<1/2.
\]
With $k=\kappa t^3$, $\epsilon_j=b_j-v_j^{3/2}\ge0$, and
$r_j=v_jt^2/2-2kv_j^{3/2}$, we have $q_j=(r_j-k\epsilon_j)_+\in[0,m(t,a)]$.
For any slope parameter $\lambda\ge0$ and $0\le r<1/2$, define the infimal
convolution
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-infimum}
 \varphi_\lambda(r)=\inf_{0\le q\le r}
       \{g(q)+\lambda(r-q)\}
 =\begin{cases}
   g(r),&r\le q_\lambda,\\
   \lambda r-c_\lambda,&r\ge q_\lambda,
  \end{cases}
 \quad
 q_\lambda=\frac{\lambda}{2(1+\lambda)},\quad
 c_\lambda=\frac{\lambda-\log(1+\lambda)}2,
\end{equation}
which follows directly from $\partial_q\{g(q)+\lambda(r-q)\}=2q/(1-2q)-\lambda$.

\begin{lemma}[Logarithmic correction via cubic lower bounds]\label{lem:current-log-support}
Set $A=1/(32\kappa^2)$ and $r(z)=Az^2(1-z)$. Suppose $\alpha,\beta\ge0$ and
$0<M\le1$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-support}
 \varphi_\lambda(r(z))\ge z^2(\alpha z-\beta)
 \qquad(0\le z\le M).
\end{equation}
If $4\kappa t\sqrt a\le M$, define
\begin{align}
 S_\lambda={}&\frac{V_0t^2}{2}-(1+\lambda)kB
           -16\kappa^2t^2\beta V_0
           +\{64\kappa^3t^3\alpha+(\lambda-1)k\}\tau_0,
             \label{eq:current-full-log}\\
 P(v)={}&64\kappa^3t^3\alpha v^{3/2}-16\kappa^2t^2\beta v,
 \qquad \delta=\max\{0,P(a)\}.
             \label{eq:current-log-loss}
\end{align}
Then for every index $i\in J$,
\[
 \prod_{j\in J}|f_j(t)|\le\min\{1,e^{-S_\lambda}\},\qquad
 \prod_{j\in J\setminus\{i\}}|f_j(t)|
       \le\min\{1,e^{-S_\lambda+m(t,a)+\delta}\}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The condition $4\kappa t\sqrt a\le M\le1$ ensures $z_j=4\kappa t\sqrt{v_j}\in[0,M]$
and $r_j=r(z_j)\ge0$. Since $0\le q_j\le r_j$ and $r_j-q_j\le k\epsilon_j$,
the definition \eqref{eq:current-infimum} and \eqref{eq:current-support} give
\[
 \sum_{j\in J}g(q_j)
 \ge\sum_{j\in J}\varphi_\lambda(r_j)-\lambda k(B-\tau_0)
 \ge64\kappa^3t^3\alpha\tau_0-16\kappa^2t^2\beta V_0-\lambda k(B-\tau_0).
\]
Adding $\sum_{j\in J}q_j\ge H$ yields $\sum_{j\in J}[q_j+g(q_j)]\ge S_\lambda$,
which proves the full-product bound. Deleting index $i$ removes $q_i\le m(t,a)$
from the linear sum and $P(v_i)\le\max\{P(0),P(a)\}=\delta$ (by convexity of
$P$ on $[0,a]$) from the support sum, while bounding the remaining moment
excess by $B-\tau_0$ preserves the inequality.
\end{proof}

The following criterion reduces verification of \eqref{eq:current-support}
on $[0,M]$ to checking its right endpoint $z=M$.

\begin{lemma}[Endpoint criterion for tangent lower bounds]\label{lem:current-log-tangent}
Let $\psi(z)=g(r(z))/z^2$ (extended by continuity at $z=0$) and
$D(z)=z^4\psi''(z)$. If $0<u<2/3$ satisfies $r(u)\le q_\lambda$ and
$D(u)\ge0$, then $\alpha=\psi'(u)$ and $\beta=u\psi'(u)-\psi(u)$ are
nonnegative, and for any $M\in[u,1]$, the inequality \eqref{eq:current-support}
holds on $[0,M]$ if and only if
\[
 \frac{\varphi_\lambda(r(M))}{M^2}\ge\alpha M-\beta.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Direct differentiation gives
\[
 D(z)=g''(r)\{Az^2(2-3z)\}^2-6rg'(r)+6g(r),
\]
\[
 D'(z)=\frac{4A^2z^3(2-3z)}{(1-2r)^3}\,B(z),\qquad
 B(z)=1-6z+2Az^2(3-5z+3z^2).
\]
Since $\max_{[0,1]}r=4A/27<1/2$, we have $A<27/8$. On $[0,2/3]$,
\[
 B'(z)=-6+6Az(2-5z+4z^2)<0,
\]
because $8/27-z(2-5z+4z^2)=(2/3-z)(4z^2-7z/3+4/9)\ge0$. As $B(0)=1$ and
$B(2/3)=-3+8A/9<0$, $D(z)$ first increases from $D(0)=0$ and then decreases
to $D(2/3)=6\{g(r)-rg'(r)\}<0$, so $D$ has a unique zero in $(0,2/3)$. Thus
$\psi$ is convex from $0$ up to that inflection point (which lies to the
right of $u$ since $D(u)\ge0$), concave on the remainder of $(0,2/3)$, and
decreasing on $[2/3,1]$. On the linear branch $r\ge q_\lambda$,
\[
 \left(\frac{\varphi_\lambda(r(z))}{z^2}\right)''
 =\left(\lambda A(1-z)-\frac{c_\lambda}{z^2}\right)''
 =-\frac{6c_\lambda}{z^4}\le0.
\]
Since $r(z)$ strictly increases on $[0,2/3]$ and $\varphi_\lambda(r(z))/z^2$
is $C^1$ across the transition $r(z)=q_\lambda$, on $[0,2/3]$ the derivative
of $\varphi_\lambda(r(z))/z^2$ increases on $[0,z_0]$ (where $z_0\ge u$ is the
smaller of the inflection point of $\psi$ and the transition point
$r(z)=q_\lambda$ on $[0,2/3]$, or equals the inflection point of $\psi$ when
$q_\lambda>4A/27=\max_{[0,1]}r$) and decreases on $[z_0,2/3]$, while on
$[2/3,1]$ it is nonpositive. Consequently, the difference
$\varphi_\lambda(r(z))/z^2-(\alpha z-\beta)$ decreases to zero at $z=u$, is
nondecreasing on $[u,z_0]$, and is concave on $[z_0,2/3]$ and nonincreasing on
$[2/3,1]$; its minimum on $[0,M]$ is therefore attained at $z=u$ or $z=M$.
Finally, convexity on $[0,u]$ and $\psi(0)=\psi'(0)=0$ imply
$\alpha,\beta\ge0$.
\end{proof}

tangent lower bounds across $\lambda\in\{3/4,1,3/2,2,3\}$, each verified
using Lemma~\ref{lem:current-log-tangent} in 192-bit Arb arithmetic. On any
parameter box, $S_\lambda$ is affine in $\tau_0$ and attains its minimum at
an endpoint of the $\tau_0$ interval (when $\lambda<1$, we bound the
nonnegative quantities $B+\tau_0$ and $B-\tau_0$ in
$-k(B+\tau_0)-\lambda k(B-\tau_0)$ before expanding). We denote by
$\mathcal F_0$ and $\mathcal D_0$ the pointwise minima of the quadratic
bounds \eqref{eq:corrected-products} and all applicable tangent bounds from
Lemma~\ref{lem:current-log-support} (setting $\mathcal F_0=\mathcal D_0=1$ for
the empty product).

\section{Real and imaginary zero-bias errors}\label{sec:directional}

For a centered random variable $X$ of variance $v>0$, its zero-biased
distribution $X^*$ is characterized by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zero-bias}
 v\E g'(X^*)=\E[Xg(X)]
\end{equation}
for all absolutely continuous functions $g$ with $\E|Xg(X)|<\infty$
\citep{GoldsteinReinert1997}. We write $f_j^*=f_{X_j^*}$. By the sharp
$L^1$-Wasserstein bound $W_1(X,X^*)\le\E|X|^3/(2v)$ proved independently by
\citet[Lemma~1.1]{Goldstein2010} and \citet[Theorem~3]{Tyurin2009}, the
Lipschitz function $x\mapsto e^{\ii tx}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:classical-zero-bias}
 |f_X(t)-f_{X^*}(t)|\le |t|\frac{\E|X|^3}{2v}.
\end{equation}

To refine \eqref{eq:classical-zero-bias} when $\E|X|^3$ is close to
$v^{3/2}$, consider a standardized random variable $W$ with $\E W=0$,
$\E W^2=1$, $Y=|W|$, $\rho=\E Y^3$, and third-moment excess $e=\rho-1\ge0$.
Because $\E Y^2=1$, the cubic polynomial $h(y)=(y-1)^2(y+1/2)=y^3-\frac32y^2+\frac12$
satisfies the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-excess}
 \E[(Y-1)^2(Y+1/2)]=e.
\end{equation}
In particular, $e=0$ if and only if $Y=1$ almost surely, which since $\E W=0$
means that $W$ has the symmetric Rademacher distribution
$\frac12\delta_{-1}+\frac12\delta_1$, with $f_W(x)=\cos x$ and
$f_{W^*}(x)=\sinc x=(\sin x)/x$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:peano}
For every $x\in\R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:real-cf}
 |\Re f_W(x)-\cos x|\le \frac{e|x|^3}{6},
 \qquad
 |\Re f_{W^*}(x)-\sinc x|\le \frac{e|x|}{2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For any even function $f$ with Lipschitz second derivative, the remainder
after subtracting its quadratic Taylor approximation in $y^2$ at $y=1$,
\[
 R_f(y)=f(y)-f(1)-\frac{f'(1)}2(y^2-1),\qquad y\ge0,
\]
admits the integral representation $R_f(y)=\int_0^{\max(1,y)}K_y(u)f'''(u)\,du$,
where the Peano kernel $K_y(u)$ is nonnegative on $[0,\max(1,y)]$ (specifically,
for $y\le1$, $K_y(u)=u(y-1)^2/2$ on $[0,y]$ and $(1-u)(u-y^2)/2$ on $[y,1]$;
for $y\ge1$, $K_y(u)=u(y-1)^2/2$ on $[0,1]$ and $(y-u)^2/2$ on $[1,y]$) and
has total integral $\int_0^{\max(1,y)}K_y(u)\,du=(y-1)^2(y+1/2)/6$. Applying
this identity to $f(y)=\cos(xy)$, where $\|f'''\|_\infty\le|x|^3$ and
$\E(Y^2-1)=0$, and taking expectations via \eqref{eq:moment-excess} yields
the first bound in \eqref{eq:real-cf}.

For the second bound, let $W^\square$ have the size-biased distribution
defined by $\E g(W^\square)=\E[W^2g(W)]$, so that $W^*\stackrel d=UW^\square$
with $U\sim\Unif[0,1]$ independent of $W^\square$. For every $z\ge0$,
\[
 \Prob(|W^*|\le z)=\E[Y\min(z,Y)]\le\min(z,1)=\Prob(U\le z),
\]
so $|W^*|$ stochastically dominates $U$. Their $L^1$-Wasserstein distance
is therefore equal to the difference of their means:
$W_1(|W^*|,U)=\E|W^*|-\E U=\rho/2-1/2=e/2$. Since $y\mapsto\cos(xy)$ is
$|x|$-Lipschitz and $\Re f_{W^*}(x)=\E\cos(x|W^*|)$ while $\sinc x=\E\cos(xU)$,
the second inequality in \eqref{eq:real-cf} follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:imaginary}
For every $x\in\R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:imaginary-cf}
 |\Im f_W(x)|\le \frac{|x|^3}{6}\sqrt{e(\rho+5/3)}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By symmetry we may assume $x>0$. Set $A(y)=x\sinc(xy)=(\sin(xy))/y$. Since
$\E W=0$, we have $\Im f_W(x)=\E\sin(xW)=\E[W(A(Y)-A(1))]$. Because
$|\sinc'(z)|\le|z|/3$, we have $|A'(y)|\le x^3y/3$, and integrating from $1$
to $y$ gives $|A(y)-A(1)|\le(x^3/6)|y^2-1|$. Applying the Cauchy--Schwarz
inequality and \eqref{eq:moment-excess} yields
\[
 \E[Y|Y^2-1|]\le
 \sqrt{\E[(Y-1)^2(Y+1/2)]\,\E\!\left[\frac{Y^2(Y+1)^2}{Y+1/2}\right]}
 \le\sqrt{e(\rho+5/3)},
\]
where the last step uses the algebraic identity and inequality
\[
 \frac{y^2(y+1)^2}{y+1/2}=y^3+\frac32y^2+\frac{y^2}{4(y+1/2)}
 \le y^3+\frac32y^2+\frac{2+4y^2}{36}=y^3+\frac{29}{18}y^2+\frac1{18},
\]
which holds for all $y\ge0$ since
$(2+4y^2)/9-y^2/(y+1/2)=(y-1)^2(4y+1)/[9(y+1/2)]\ge0$.
\end{proof}

\section{A one-sided lower bound for the real zero-bias difference}\label{sec:quadratic}

While Lemma~\ref{lem:peano} bounds $|\Re(f_W(x)-f_{W^*}(x))-(\cos x-\sinc x)|$
from both sides by $(x/2+x^3/6)e$, the lower side of this interval can be
improved on $0\le x\le2$: the Rademacher law actually minimizes
$\Re(f_W(x)-f_{W^*}(x))$ among all centered unit-variance distributions.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:supporting-quadratic}
For $0<x\le2$, set
\[
 F_x(y)=\cos(xy)-y\frac{\sin(xy)}x,\qquad
 A_x=\cos x+(1+x^2)\sinc x.
\]
Then $F_x(y)\ge F_x(1)-\frac12A_x(y^2-1)$ for all $y\ge0$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-sided-real}
 \Re(f_W(x)-f_{W^*}(x))\ge\cos x-\sinc x.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $h_x(z)=\cos z+(1+x^2)\sinc z$, so that $A_x=h_x(x)$. With $z=xy$, the
derivative with respect to $y$ of $x^2\{F_x(y)-F_x(1)+\frac12A_x(y^2-1)\}$
is $z[A_x-h_x(z)]$.

The function $h_x(z)$ is strictly decreasing on $(0,\pi)$, is at most $1$ on
$[\pi,2\pi]$, and is at most $1+5/(2\pi)<11/6$ for $z\ge2\pi$. On the other
hand, alternating Taylor series bounds give, for $0<x\le2$,
\[
 A_x\ge p_0(x^2),\qquad
 p_0(u)=2+\frac u3-\frac{7u^2}{60}+\frac{17u^3}{2520}-\frac{u^4}{5040}.
\]
On $[0,4]$, $p_0''(u)=(-98+17u-u^2)/420<0$, so $p_0(u)\ge\min(p_0(0),p_0(4))=194/105>11/6$.
Thus $A_x-h_x(z)$ is negative for $0<z<x$ and positive for $z>x$, so
$F_x(y)-F_x(1)+\frac12A_x(y^2-1)$ attains its global minimum on $[0,\infty)$
at $y=1$, where it equals zero. Taking expectations under $Y=|W|$ and using
$\E W^*=UW^\square$ gives $\E F_x(Y)=\Re(f_W(x)-f_{W^*}(x))$, proving
\eqref{eq:one-sided-real}.
\end{proof}

Combining Lemmas~\ref{lem:peano} and~\ref{lem:supporting-quadratic}, we see
that for $0\le x\le2$, with $g(x)=\sinc x-\cos x\ge0$ and $c(x)=x/2+x^3/6$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:real-interval}
 -g(x)\le\Re(f_W(x)-f_{W^*}(x))\le-g(x)+c(x)e.
\end{equation}
By the alternating Taylor series of $\sinc x-\cos x$, we have the increasing
upper bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha}
 \frac{2g(x)}{x}\le\alpha(x):=
 \begin{cases}
  2x/3-x^3/15+x^5/420,&0\le x\le2,\\
  2x/3,&x>2,
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
and we set $q(x)=2c(x)/x=1+x^2/3$.

\section{Quadratic and affine bounds in the third-moment excess}
\label{sec:current-scalar}

We next establish a quadratic bound in $e=\rho-1$ for $|\Im(f_W(x)-f_{W^*}(x))|^2$
and combine it with \eqref{eq:real-interval} via a least concave majorant.

\begin{lemma}[Quadratic imaginary-error bound]\label{lem:current-odd}
For $0<x\le2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-odd}
 |\Im(f_W(x)-f_{W^*}(x))|^2
 \le e\left\{\frac23(x\cos x-2\sin x)^2+\frac{x^2e}{4}\right\}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Define
\[
 A_x(y)=x\sinc(xy)+\frac{\cos(xy)}x,
 \qquad a_x=A_x'(1)=x\cos x-2\sin x,
\]
\[
 b_x=A_x''(1)=(2-x^2)\sin x-3x\cos x,
 \qquad
 J_x(y)=\frac{y^2}{y+1/2}
             \left(\frac{A_x(y)-A_x(1)}{y-1}\right)^2,
\]
with $J_x(1)=2a_x^2/3$ and $J_x'(1)=8a_x^2/9+2a_xb_x/3$ by continuity.
Centering $\E W=0$ and the zero-bias representation give
\[
 \Im\bigl(f_W(x)-f_{W^*}(x)\bigr)=\E\bigl[W\{A_x(Y)-A_x(1)\}\bigr].
\]
By the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality and \eqref{eq:moment-excess},
\[
 |\Im(f_W(x)-f_{W^*}(x))|^2
 \le\E[(Y-1)^2(Y+1/2)]\,\E J_x(Y)=e\,\E J_x(Y).
\]
In Appendix~\ref{app:odd-majorant} we verify the global scalar inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-J-majorant}
 J_x(y)\le J_x(1)+\frac{J_x'(1)}2(y^2-1)+\frac{x^2}4(y-1)^2(y+1/2)
 \qquad(y\ge0,\ 0<x\le2).
\end{equation}
Taking expectations under $Y=|W|$ and using $\E(Y^2-1)=0$ yields
$\E J_x(Y)\le 2a_x^2/3+x^2e/4$, which proves \eqref{eq:current-odd}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Aggregation across summands by the least concave majorant}

For $0\le x\le2$, set
\[
 p_1(x)=1+\frac{x^2}6-\frac{x^4}{40}+\frac{x^6}{1008},
 \qquad b_0(x)=\frac83p_1(x)^2.
\]
By alternating Taylor series, $2\sinc x-\cos x\le p_1(x)$. Combining
\eqref{eq:real-interval}, \eqref{eq:current-odd}, and the classical bound
$|2(f_W(x)-f_{W^*}(x))/x|\le\rho=1+e$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-two-branches}
 \left|\frac{2(f_W(x)-f_{W^*}(x))}{x}\right|
 \le\min\{1+e,\max(F_1(e),F_2(e))\},
\end{equation}
where
\[
 F_1(e)=\sqrt{\alpha(x)^2+b_0(x)e+e^2},\qquad
 F_2(e)=\sqrt{b_0(x)e+(1+q(x)^2)e^2},
\]
since $\max\{\gamma^2,(q(x)e-\gamma)^2\}\le\max\{\alpha(x)^2,q(x)^2e^2\}$ for
$0\le\gamma=2g(x)/x\le\alpha(x)$.

To aggregate \eqref{eq:current-two-branches} across summands with different
variances and third-moment excesses, we compute the explicit least concave
majorant of $\max(F_1(e),F_2(e))$ on $e\ge0$.

\begin{lemma}[Least concave majorant of two square-root branches]\label{lem:current-concave}
Let $a,b\ge0$, $0\le u<v$, and $F_1(e)=\sqrt{a^2+be+ue^2}$,
$F_2(e)=\sqrt{be+ve^2}$ for $e\ge0$. When $a=0$, the least concave majorant
of $\max(F_1,F_2)$ is $F_2$. When $a>0$ and $b\le2a\sqrt v$, it is the
affine function $H(e)=a+\sqrt v\,e$. When $a>0$ and $b>2a\sqrt v$, set
\[
 w=\sqrt{v-u},\quad s=\frac{u}{w^2},\quad r=\frac{b}{aw},\quad
 d_0=\sqrt{r^2-4s}>2,\quad \eta=\frac{d_0-2}{r}\in(0,1),
\]
\[
 c=\frac r{2\sqrt{d_0+s-1}},\quad
 m=\frac{d_0+2s}{2\sqrt{d_0+s-1}},\quad
 e_-=\frac{a}{w}\eta,\quad e_+=\frac{a}{w\eta}.
\]
Then the least concave majorant of $\max(F_1,F_2)$ on $[0,\infty)$ is
\[
 H(e)=\begin{cases}
 F_1(e),&0\le e\le e_-,\\
 ac+wm e,&e_-\le e\le e_+,\\
 F_2(e),&e\ge e_+.
 \end{cases}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $a>0$, rescale by $z=we/a$, so that $F_1^2/a^2=1+rz+sz^2$ and
$F_2^2/a^2=rz+(s+1)z^2$, which cross at $z=1$. When $r\le2\sqrt{s+1}$,
squaring shows that $1+\sqrt{s+1}\,z$ majorizes both branches; since any
concave majorant is at least $1$ at $z=0$ and has asymptotic slope at least
$\sqrt{s+1}$ as $z\to\infty$, $1+\sqrt{s+1}\,z$ is the least concave
majorant.

When $r>2\sqrt{s+1}$, both $F_1$ and $F_2$ are individually concave on
$[0,\infty)$, and direct algebra gives the exact square completions
\[
 (c+mz)^2-(1+rz+sz^2)=c^2(z-\eta)^2,
\]
\[
 (c+mz)^2-\bigl(rz+(s+1)z^2\bigr)=(c^2-1)(z-1/\eta)^2,
 \qquad c^2-1=\frac{(d_0-2)^2}{4(d_0+s-1)}>0.
\]
Thus the line $c+mz$ is tangent to $\sqrt{1+rz+sz^2}$ at $z=\eta<1$ and
tangent to $\sqrt{rz+(s+1)z^2}$ at $z=1/\eta>1$, and dominates both branches
globally. Because $H$ agrees with $F_1$ on $[0,e_-]$, with $F_2$ on
$[e_+,\infty)$, and with the chord joining their contact points on
$[e_-,e_+]$, it is concave and is dominated by every concave majorant of
$\max(F_1,F_2)$.
\end{proof}

Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:current-concave} with $a=\alpha(x)$, $b=b_0(x)$,
$u=1$, and $v=1+q(x)^2$, and setting $\widehat H_x(e)=\min\{1+e,H_x(e)\}$,
we obtain a concave majorant $\widehat H_x(e)$ of the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:current-two-branches}. For a subarray $J$ with $\max_{i\in J}v_i\le a$,
Jensen's inequality with weights $v_i^{3/2}/\tau_0$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-prefactor}
 \sum_{i\in J} v_i|f_i(t)-f_i^*(t)|
 \le\frac t2P_{\rm quad}(t;B,a,\tau_0),\qquad
 P_{\rm quad}(t;B,a,\tau_0)
 =\tau_0\widehat H_{t\sqrt a}\!\left(\frac{B-\tau_0}{\tau_0}\right),
\end{equation}
where we set $P_{\rm quad}=B$ if $\tau_0=0$ or $t\sqrt a>2$. Since
$\alpha(x),b_0(x),q(x)$ are increasing on $[0,2]$, $P_{\rm quad}$ is
nondecreasing in $t$, $a$, and $B$, and because all slopes of $H_x$ exceed
$1$ while $\alpha(x)<1$, $\widehat H_x(e)/(1+e)$ is nondecreasing in $e$,
which implies that $P_{\rm quad}$ is nonincreasing in $\tau_0$.

As a simpler closed-form alternative for interval evaluation, note that
$p_1(x)\le q(x)$ and $q(x)\ge1$ on $[0,2]$, so the single square-root
function $R_x(e)=\sqrt{\alpha(x)^2+\frac83q(x)^2e+2q(x)^2e^2}$ majorizes
both $F_1(e)$ and $F_2(e)$ and is concave in $e$ (since
$\frac{64}{9}q^4-8q^2\alpha^2>0$). Applying Jensen's inequality to $R_x$
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aggregate-error}
 \sum_{j\in J} v_j|f_j(t)-f_j^*(t)|\le\frac t2
 P_{\rm dir}(t;B,a,\tau_0),\qquad t\ge0,
\end{equation}
where, with $x=t\sqrt a$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:P}
 P_{\rm dir}(t;B,a,\tau_0)=\min\left\{B,
  \sqrt{\alpha(x)^2\tau_0^2+q(x)^2(B-\tau_0)(2B+2\tau_0/3)}\right\},
\end{equation}
which likewise increases in $t,a,B$ and decreases in $\tau_0$ (with
$P_{\rm dir}=0$ for the empty subset).

\subsection{Certified affine bounds in the third absolute moment}\label{sec:current-affine}

To complement the concave square-root bounds at moderate and large third-moment
excess, we also construct certified affine bounds in $\rho=\E|W|^3$ for the
normalized zero-bias difference
\[
 Z_W(x)=\frac{2(f_W(x)-f_{W^*}(x))}{x},\qquad
 Z_W(0)=-\ii \E W^3.
\]
With $\phi_2(z)=\int_0^1(1-u)e^{uz}\,du$ and the entire function
\[
 h_x(w)=-2w^2(x+\ii w)\phi_2(\ii xw),\qquad h_0(w)=-\ii w^3,
\]
the zero-bias identity and $\E W=0$, $\E W^2=1$ give $\E h_x(W)=Z_W(x)$.

Suppose that throughout a closed frequency interval $x\in[x_0,x_1]$ and for
all $w\in\R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-disk-certificate}
 |h_x(w)-\ii \beta w-\gamma(w^2-1)|
       \le c_0+\delta+c_2|w|^2+\Lambda|w|^3.
\end{equation}
Taking expectations under $W$ annihilates $\ii\beta W$ and $\gamma(W^2-1)$,
giving $|Z_W(x)|\le C+\Lambda\rho$ with $C=c_0+c_2+\delta$. For any common
slope $\lambda\ge\Lambda>0$, since $\rho\ge1$, this implies
$|Z_W(x)|\le(C+\Lambda-\lambda)+\lambda\rho$. Denoting by $C_\lambda(X)$ the
maximum of the intercept $C+\Lambda-\lambda$ over all frequency cells in
$[0,X]$, summing over $i\in J$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-affine-sum}
 \sum_{i\in J} v_i|f_i(t)-f_i^*(t)|
 \le\frac t2\{C_\lambda(X)\tau_0+\lambda B\}
 \qquad(t\sqrt a\le X).
\end{equation}

Two families of certificates in \path{certificates/scalar/} establish bounds
of the form \eqref{eq:current-affine-sum}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Modulus (disk) certificates (\path{disk.zip}):} Cover $x\in[0,2]$
in 128 cells of width $1/64$ at the five common slopes
$\lambda\in\{5/4,3/2,2,3,4\}$. On each bounded spatial interval $|w|\le R$,
outward-rounded Arb Taylor enclosures verify \eqref{eq:current-disk-certificate}.
For $|w|\ge R\ge1$, positivity of the explicit cubic tail polynomial
\[
 (\Lambda-1)y^3+(c_2-|\gamma|)y^2-(x_1^2/2+|\beta|)y+c_0+\delta+|\gamma|
\]
(for $\Lambda>1$, using $|\phi_2(\ii xy)|\le1/2$) or
\[
 \Lambda y^3+(c_2-G)y^2-(4/x_0^2+|\beta+2|)y+c_0+\delta+G-2/x_0
\]
with $G=\max_{j\in\{0,1\}}|\gamma-2/x_j|$ (for $0<\Lambda\le1$ and $x_0>0$, using
$h_x(y)=g_x(y)-2\ii y-2(1-y^2)/x$ with $|g_x(y)|\le2/x_0+4y/x_0^2$)
completes the verification on $\R$.
\item \textbf{Directional projection certificates (\path{directional.zip}):}
Cover 61 frequency cells and 5 slopes across 25 directions $\zeta$ in the
closed upper half-plane, which extend by complex conjugation
$h_x(-w)=\overline{h_x(w)}$ to 48 directions whose convex hull contains the
disk of radius $\eta=997/1000$ (equivalently, $\max_\zeta\Re(\zeta z)\ge\eta|z|$
for all $z\in\mathbb C$, verified via the supporting half-planes of the
48-gon). Maximizing the intercepts over all 48 directions and dividing the
entire resulting projection bound in \eqref{eq:current-affine-sum} by $\eta$
yields a second certified bound for $\sum_{i\in J}v_i|f_i(t)-f_i^*(t)|$.
\end{enumerate}

We denote by $P(t;B,a,\tau_0)$ the pointwise minimum of $P_{\rm quad}$,
$P_{\rm dir}$, and all applicable affine bounds \eqref{eq:current-affine-sum}
(with $P=0$ for the empty subset), so that
\[
 \sum_{i\in J} v_i|f_i(t)-f_i^*(t)|\le \frac t2P(t;B,a,\tau_0).
\]

\subsection{Single-summand modulus bound from third-moment excess}

Squaring the real-part bound in Lemma~\ref{lem:peano} and adding the square
of the imaginary-part bound in Lemma~\ref{lem:imaginary} gives a second
single-summand modulus bound: for a centered variable $X$ of variance $v$,
third absolute moment $b$, $\tau_j=v^{3/2}$, and $C=|\cos(t\sqrt v)|$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paired-cf}
 |f_X(t)|^2\le C^2+\frac{C(b-\tau_j)|t|^3}{3}
       +\frac{t^6(b-\tau_j)(2b+2\tau_j/3)}{36}.
\end{equation}
On any parameter box, after bounding $C=|\cos(t\sqrt v)|$ over the cosine
argument interval, the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:paired-cf} is increasing
in $C,b,|t|$ and decreasing in $\tau_j$.

\section{Isolating a summand of maximal variance}\label{sec:distinguished}

By the independent-sum zero-bias identity \citep[Lemma~2.1(5)]{GoldsteinReinert1997}
at total variance one,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sum-zero-bias}
 f_{S^*}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^n v_i f_{X_i^*}(t)\prod_{j\ne i}f_{X_j}(t).
\end{equation}
Isolating the largest-variance summand $X_1$ (with variance $d=v_1$ and
third absolute moment $b=b_1$) from the remaining summands (with parameters
$w,B_R,\tau_R,a_R$ from \eqref{eq:remainder-state}), we bound the remainder
product by
\[
 R(t)=\min\bigl\{e^{-\psi(t;w,B_R+\tau_R)},\
                  \mathcal F_0(t;w,B_R,a_R,\tau_R)\bigr\},
\]
the single-summand modulus $|f_1(t)|$ by $A(t)$, the minimum of
\eqref{eq:single-cf} and the square root of \eqref{eq:paired-cf}, the
deleted remainder product by $D_R(t)=\mathcal D_0(t;w,B_R,a_R,\tau_R)$ (if the
remainder is empty, we set $R(t)=D_R(t)=1$ and $P(t;B_R,a_R,\tau_R)=0$), and
the deleted whole-sum product by $D_S(t)=\mathcal D_0(t;1,L,d,\tau)$.
Splitting \eqref{eq:sum-zero-bias} into $i=1$ and $i\ge2$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distinguished-error}
 |f_S(t)-f_{S^*}(t)|\le\frac t2\Bigl\{
 P(t;b,d,d^{3/2})R(t)+P(t;B_R,a_R,\tau_R)A(t)D_R(t)\Bigr\}.
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:distinguished-error} with the unsplit bound
$(t/2)P(t;L,d,\tau)D_S(t)$ and the classical bound $tL/2$ yields
$|f_S(t)-f_{S^*}(t)|\le(tL/2)\mathcal B(t)$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B}
 \mathcal B(t)=\min\left\{1,\ \frac{P(t;L,d,\tau)D_S(t)}L,\
 \frac{P(t;b,d,d^{3/2})R(t)+P(t;B_R,a_R,\tau_R)A(t)D_R(t)}L
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
Similarly, for the full characteristic function $|f_S(t)|$, we use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:whole-cf}
 F_S(t)=\min\bigl\{e^{-\psi(t;1,L+\tau)},\ A(t)R(t),\
                    \mathcal F_0(t;1,L,d,\tau)\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
Notice that the third expression in \eqref{eq:B} can also be written as the
convex combination $\lambda U_1+(1-\lambda)U_R$, where $\lambda=b/L\in[0,1]$,
$U_1=P(t;b,d,d^{3/2})R(t)/b\in[0,1]$, and
$U_R=P(t;B_R,a_R,\tau_R)A(t)D_R(t)/B_R\in[0,1]$ (setting $U_R=0$ when
$B_R=0$). On a parameter box where
$\lambda\in[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]$ and $U_1\le\overline U_1$,
$U_R\le\overline U_R$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-fraction}
 \lambda U_1+(1-\lambda)U_R\le
 \max_{\lambda\in\{\lambda_-,\lambda_+\}}
       \{\lambda\overline U_1+(1-\lambda)\overline U_R\},
\end{equation}
and the evaluator takes the minimum of \eqref{eq:moment-fraction} and direct
interval division of the numerator in \eqref{eq:B} by $L_-$.

Finally, integrating $(f_S(t)-e^{-t^2/2})'+t(f_S(t)-e^{-t^2/2})=t(f_S(t)-f_{S^*}(t))$
as in \citet[Lemma~3]{Tyurin2009} yields the modulus bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ode-comparison}
 \frac{|f_S(t)-e^{-t^2/2}|}{L}
 \le R_\Delta(t):=e^{-t^2/2}
       \int_0^t \frac{u^2}{2}\mathcal B(u)e^{u^2/2}\,du.
\end{equation}

\section{Signed comparison with a product of cosines}\label{sec:current-reference}

To preserve the sign of the characteristic-function displacement when the
summands are close to symmetric two-point distributions, we compare $f_S(t)$
with the reference cosine product
\[
 C_v(t)=\prod_{i=1}^n\cos(t\sqrt{v_i}),
\]
which is the characteristic function of $\sum_{i=1}^n\sqrt{v_i}\varepsilon_i$
for independent Rademacher variables $\varepsilon_i$ \citep{MattnerShevtsova2019}.
Put $g(t)=e^{-t^2/2}$, $k_v(t)=\sum_{i=1}^n\sqrt{v_i}\tan(t\sqrt{v_i})$, and
$r_i(t)=f_i'(t)+\sqrt{v_i}\tan(t\sqrt{v_i})f_i(t)$. For $t\sqrt d<\pi/2$,
every cosine factor is positive, and differentiating $f_S(t)-C_v(t)$ by the
product rule yields the exact linear differential identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-reference-identity}
 (f_S-C_v)'(t)+k_v(t)\bigl(f_S(t)-C_v(t)\bigr)
       =\sum_{i=1}^n r_i(t)\prod_{j\ne i}f_j(t),
 \qquad f_S(0)-C_v(0)=0.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Bounding the forcing term}

For a standardized summand $W$ of mean zero and variance one, and
$0<x<\pi/2$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-transformed-target}
 T_x(w)=\frac2{x^2}\bigl\{(\ii w+\tan x)e^{\ii xw}
               -\ii w\sec x-x(1-w^2)\bigr\},\qquad T_0(w)=\ii (w-w^3).
\end{equation}
Since $\E W=0$ and $\E W^2=1$, we have
\[
 \E T_x(W)=\frac{2\{f_W'(x)+\tan(x)f_W(x)\}}{x^2},
 \qquad r_i(t)=\frac12t^2v_i^{3/2}\E T_{t\sqrt{v_i}}(W_i).
\]
An elementary Wronskian bound holds on the entire interval $0\le x<\pi/2$:
writing $q_0(u)=f_W''(u)+f_W(u)=\E[(1-W^2)(e^{\ii uW}-1)]$, the
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality from the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:imaginary} gives
$|q_0(u)|\le u\sqrt{(\rho-1)(\rho+5/3)}$. Since
$f_W'(x)\cos x+f_W(x)\sin x=\int_0^xq_0(u)\cos u\,du$ and $\cos u>0$, we
obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-elementary-energy}
 \begin{aligned}
 |\E T_x(W)|&\le2p(x)\sqrt{(\rho-1)(\rho+5/3)}
                  \quad(0\le x<\pi/2),\\
 p(x)&=\frac{x\tan x+1-\sec x}{x^2}=\int_0^1\frac{r\cos(rx)}{\cos x}\,dr,\quad p(0)=\frac12.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

On the subinterval $0\le x\le1$, Appendix~\ref{sec:current-energy-cert}
establishes the sharper quadratic inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-energy}
 |\E T_x(W)|^2\le(\rho-1)
     \left\{q_{\rm ref}(x)+\frac98(\rho-1)\right\},\qquad
 q_{\rm ref}(x)=\frac83\frac{\sinc^4x}{\cos^2x}=\frac83\left(\frac{\sec x-\cos x}{x^2}\right)^2.
\end{equation}
Let $D_{\rm del}(t)$ be an upper bound on $\max_i\prod_{j\ne i}|f_j(t)|$.
Applying Cauchy--Schwarz with $w_i=v_i^{3/2}$ and $e_i=b_i-w_i$ gives, for
$t\sqrt d\le1$,
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^n w_i|\E T_{t\sqrt{v_i}}(W_i)|
 \le\sqrt{(L-\tau)\left\{\sum_{i=1}^n w_iq_{\rm ref}(tw_i^{1/3})+\frac98(L-\tau)\right\}}.
\]
Because $\sec x$ has nonnegative even Taylor coefficients with
$[\sec x]_{x^{2n}}\ge1/(2n)!$, the function
$f_t(w)=wq_{\rm ref}(tw^{1/3})=\sum_{n\ge0}c_nt^{2n}w^{1+2n/3}$ has
$c_n\ge0$ and is increasing and convex on $w\ge0$. Subject to
$0\le w_i\le a=d^{3/2}$ and $\sum_i w_i=\tau$, a convex sum $\sum_i f_t(w_i)$
is maximized by packing as many weights as possible at the upper cap $a$:
setting $k=\lfloor\tau/a\rfloor$ and $r=\tau-ka$, we have
$\sum_{i=1}^n w_iq_{\rm ref}(tw_i^{1/3})\le F_t(a,\tau):=kf_t(a)+f_t(r)$.
Multiplying by $t^2D_{\rm del}(t)/2$ yields the packed forcing bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-packed-source}
 \left|\sum_{i=1}^n r_i(t)\prod_{j\ne i}f_j(t)\right|
 \le\frac{t^2D_{\rm del}(t)}2
 \sqrt{(L-\tau)\left\{F_t(a,\tau)+\frac98(L-\tau)\right\}}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Sharpening the forcing bound via the largest summand}
\label{sec:split-reference}

When the third absolute moment $b$ of the largest-variance summand is
tracked separately, write
\[
 w=d^{3/2},\quad e=b-w,\quad R=\tau-w,\quad
 e_R=L-b-R,\quad E=L-\tau=e+e_R,\quad
 \delta=Lw-b\tau.
\]
For any scalar bound of the form
$|\E T_x(W_i)|^2\le(\rho_i-1)\{Q+\lambda(\rho_i-1)\}$ with $Q>0$ and
$\lambda\ge0$, applying Cauchy--Schwarz only over the remaining summands
$i\ge2$ gives the split moment factor
\begin{equation}\label{eq:split-reference}
 S_Q=\sqrt{e(Qw+\lambda e)}
       +\sqrt{e_R(QR+\lambda e_R)}.
\end{equation}
Setting $A=Qw+\lambda e$, $B=QR+\lambda e_R$, and $G^2=E(Q\tau+\lambda E)$,
the identity $G^2-S_Q^2=(\sqrt{eB}-\sqrt{e_RA})^2$ together with
$eB-e_RA=-Q\delta$ and $(\sqrt{eB}+\sqrt{e_RA})^2\le G^2$ implies that when
$\delta>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reference-defect}
 S_Q\le G\sqrt{1-
       \left\{\frac{\delta}{E(\tau+(\lambda/Q)E)}\right\}^2}.
\end{equation}
Similarly, for the convex weight bound with $q=q_{\rm ref}$, $\lambda=9/8$,
$q_j=q(tw^{1/3})\ge8/3$, and $F_R=F_t(w,R)$, the split factor
\begin{equation}\label{eq:split-packed-reference}
 S_P=\sqrt{e(q_jw+\lambda e)}
       +\sqrt{e_R(F_R+\lambda e_R)}
\end{equation}
satisfies $F_t(w,\tau)=wq_j+F_R$ and $F_R\le q_jR$, so that
$eB-e_RA\le-q_j\delta$ when $\delta\ge0$, yielding
\begin{equation}\label{eq:packed-reference-defect}
 S_P\le
 \sqrt{E\left\{F_t(w,\tau)+\frac98E\right\}}\,
 \sqrt{1-\left\{\frac{\delta}{E(\tau+27E/64)}\right\}^2}
 \qquad(\delta\ge0).
\end{equation}
The same contraction factor applies to \eqref{eq:reference-defect} whenever
$\lambda/Q\le27/64$.

To bound \eqref{eq:split-reference} and \eqref{eq:split-packed-reference} on
a parameter box, choose $w_-\le d_-^{3/2}$ and $w_+\ge d_+^{3/2}$, and set
$M=L_+$ and $a=\tau_-$. Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reference-endpoint-condition}
             a-w_+\ge\frac{15}{23}(M-b_-).
\end{equation}
With $w,b$ fixed, both $S_Q$ and $S_P$ increase with $L\le M$. Putting
$H=M-b$, the derivative of the squared remainder term with respect to $R$ is
\[
 \frac{d}{dR}\{(H-R)[QR+\lambda(H-R)]\}
       =Q(e_R-R)-2\lambda e_R\le0\qquad(R\ge H/2),
\]
while for $S_P$, since $F_t(w,R)\ge8R/3$ and $0\le\partial_R F_t(w,R)<29/4$
between multiples of $w$,
\[
 \frac{d}{dR}\left\{(H-R)\left[F_t(w,R)+\frac98(H-R)\right]\right\}
       \le5e_R-\frac83R\le0\qquad(R\ge15H/23).
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:reference-endpoint-condition} implies
$S_Q(L,\tau,w,b)\le S_Q(M,a,w,b)$ and $S_P(L,\tau,w,b)\le S_P(M,a,w,b)$.
With $E_*=M-a$, $\delta_*=Mw_--b_+a$, and $D_*=E_*(a+27E_*/64)$, any
rational $\eta\in(0,1]$ satisfying $\eta^2+(\delta_*/D_*)^2\ge1$ for
$0<\delta_*\le D_*$ yields
\[
 S_Q(L,\tau,w,b)\le\eta\sqrt{E_*(Qa+\lambda E_*)},\qquad
 S_P(L,\tau,w,b)\le\eta\sqrt{E_*\{F_t(w,a)+9E_*/8\}}.
\]
When the remaining weight $R=\tau-w$ is smaller than $w$ throughout the box,
a relaxed endpoint condition suffices for $S_P$: with $R_-=a-w_+$,
$R_+=\tau_+-w_-$, and $0<W_0\le d_+^{3/2}$, if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:single-remainder-condition}
 0<R_-\le R_+<w_-,\qquad
 R_+\le(19/25)^3W_0,\qquad
 R_-\ge\frac{23}{55}(M-b_-),
\end{equation}
then $F_t(w,R)=f_t(R)$ with $tR^{1/3}\le19/25$, where $f_t'(R)<25/6$ gives
$\frac{d}{dR}\{e_R[f_t(R)+\frac98e_R]\}\le\frac{23}{12}e_R-\frac83R\le0$.
Both derivative bounds $\partial_R F_t<29/4$ (for $x\le10001/10000$) and
$f_t'(R)<25/6$ (for $x\le19/25$) follow by bounding the increasing function
\[
 g(x)=q(x)+\frac13xq'(x)
 =\frac{8\sin^3x\{2x(1+\cos^2x)-\sin x\cos x\}}{9x^4\cos^3x}
\]
via alternating Taylor polynomials of $\sin x$ and $\cos x$.

\subsection{Joint bound on the convex weight maximum and Cauchy--Schwarz defect}
\label{sec:weight-loss}

Because $F_t(w,a)$ increases with $w$ while the defect $\delta=Mw-ba$
vanishes at $w=ba/M$, their maxima cannot occur simultaneously when
$ba/M$ is strictly smaller than the weight cap $W\ge d_+^{3/2}$. Specifically,
suppose \eqref{eq:reference-endpoint-condition} or \eqref{eq:single-remainder-condition}
holds, set $E=M-a>0$ and $\lambda=9/8$, and choose $c,W_0$ with
$b_+a/M<c<W_0\le W$ such that $\lfloor a/u\rfloor=k\ge1$ on $(c,W_0)$.
Choose $d_0>\gamma\ge0$ with $u^{2/3}\ge d_0>\gamma\ge(a-ku)^{2/3}$ on
$[c,W_0]$. Using the Taylor expansion $q(x)=\sum_{n\ge0}q_nx^{2n}$ with
$q_0=8/3$, $q_1=8/9$, $q_2=8/15$, $q_3=736/2835$, and $q_n\ge0$, we have for
any $N\ge1$,
\[
 \frac{d}{du}F_t(u,a)
 =k\{f_t'(u)-f_t'(a-ku)\}
 \ge k\sum_{n=1}^Nq_n(1+2n/3)(d_0^n-\gamma^n)t^{2n}.
\]
On a frequency cell $[l,r]$ with $r\sqrt{d_+}\le1$ and $rW^{1/3}\le10001/10000$,
choose $\bar q\ge q(rW^{1/3})$ and set
\begin{align*}
 A&=\min\{(29/4)a+\lambda E,\ a\bar q+\lambda E\},\\
 P_N(y)&=\frac{k(W_0-c)}A
       \sum_{n=1}^Nq_n(1+2n/3)(d_0^n-\gamma^n)y^n,\\
 H&=\left\{\frac{Mc-b_+a}{E(a+27E/64)}\right\}^2\le1.
\end{align*}
Integrating $\frac{d}{du}F_t(u,a)$ over $[c,W_0]$ bounds $S_P^2$ for
$w\le c$ by $\{1-P_N(t^2)\}E\{F_t(W,a)+\lambda E\}$, while for $w\ge c$ the
defect bound \eqref{eq:packed-reference-defect} gives the factor $1-H$. Using
$\sqrt{1-x}\le1-x/2$, we obtain across the entire box and frequency cell
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight-loss}
 S_P(L,\tau,w,b)
 \le
 \left(1-\frac12\min\{P_N(l^2),H\}\right)
       \sqrt{E\{F_t(W,a)+\lambda E\}}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Enclosing the cosine product and integrating the differential recurrence}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:current-maximum}
Let $M=\sqrt d<1$ and $m=(\tau-M^3)/(1-M^2)$. For every increasing convex
function $h$ on $[0,M]$,
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^n v_ih(\sqrt{v_i})\ge M^2h(M)+(1-M^2)h(m).
\]
In particular, for $0\le l\le r$ with $rM<\pi/2$,
\begin{align*}
 C_v(t)&\le C_*(t):=\cos(tM)\cos(tm)^{(1-M^2)/m^2},\\
 k_v(t)&\ge K_*(t):=M\tan(tM)+(1-M^2)\frac{\tan(tm)}{m},\\
 \frac{C_v(r)}{C_v(l)}&\le \frac{C_*(r)}{C_*(l)}.
\end{align*}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Isolating one summand of variance $M^2=d$ and applying Jensen's inequality
with weights $v_i/(1-M^2)$ to the remaining summands (whose weighted mean of
$\sqrt{v_i}$ is $m\in[0,M]$) gives the first inequality. Applying it to the
increasing convex functions $-\log\cos(tx)/x^2$, $\tan(tx)/x$, and
$[\log\cos(lx)-\log\cos(rx)]/x^2$ yields the three cosine bounds.
\end{proof}

On a parameter box, setting $D_0=\max\{d_{lo},\tau_{lo}^2\}$,
$M_0=\sqrt{D_0}$, $\tau_0=\max\{\tau_{lo},M_0^3\}$, and
$m_0=(\tau_0-M_0^3)/(1-D_0)$ (when $D_0<1$), the lower corner $(M_0,\tau_0)$
minimizes $M^2h(M)+(1-M^2)h(m)$ over the feasible box because both partial
derivatives $\partial_M$ and $\partial_\tau$ are nonnegative. Writing
$J(z)=-\log\cos z-z^2/2$ and
$A_*(t)=D_0J(tM_0)/M_0^2+(1-D_0)J(tm_0)/m_0^2$, the chord upper bound for
$J(tx)/x^2$ on $[0,\sqrt d]$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:current-maximum} enclose the
real displacement $C_v(t)-g(t)$ on $[l,r]$ within
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-center-box}
 g(l)\operatorname{expm1}\!\left[-\frac{\tau_{hi}}{d_{hi}^{3/2}}
                       J(r\sqrt{d_{hi}})\right]
 \le C_v(t)-g(t)\le g(r)\operatorname{expm1}[-A_*(l)]\le0.
\end{equation}

Finally, if $A_j$ bounds the forcing right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:current-reference-identity} on $[l,r]$, $P_j$ bounds
$|f_S(l)-C_v(l)|$, $k_j$ is a lower bound for $k_v$ on $[l,r]$, and $Q_j$
bounds $C_v(r)/C_v(l)$, then integrating \eqref{eq:current-reference-identity}
with $h=r-l$ and $\Psi_j=(1-e^{-k_jh})/k_j$ gives the recurrence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-radius-recursion}
 E_j=\max\{P_j,e^{-k_jh}P_j+A_j\Psi_j\},\qquad
 P_{j+1}\le Q_jP_j+A_j\Psi_j,
\end{equation}
where $E_j$ bounds $|f_S(t)-C_v(t)|$ throughout $[l,r]$ and $P_{j+1}$ bounds
it at the right endpoint $r$.

\section{Prawitz smoothing and threshold partitions}\label{sec:smoothing}

For $0<u<1$, Prawitz's smoothing kernel is
\[
 K(u)=\frac{1-u}{2}+
 \frac{\ii}{2}\left((1-u)\cot(\pi u)+\frac1\pi\right).
\]
In its unsigned form \citep{Prawitz1972,Tyurin2009,Shevtsova2012}, for any
cutoff $T>0$ and split point $0<s<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prawitz}
 \frac\Delta L\le
 2\int_0^s|K(u)|R_\Delta(Tu)\,du+
 \frac{2\int_s^1|K(u)|F_S(Tu)\,du+G(T,s)}{L},
\end{equation}
where $E_1(z)=\int_z^\infty v^{-1}e^{-v}\,dv$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-correction}
 G(T,s)=2\int_0^s\left|K(u)-\frac{\ii}{2\pi u}\right|
          e^{-T^2u^2/2}\,du+\frac{E_1(T^2s^2/2)}{2\pi}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Cell integration and the Gaussian correction}

On a frequency subinterval $[a,b]\subseteq[0,Ts]$, let $\mathcal B_j$ bound
$\mathcal B(u)$ on $[a,b]$, let $I_a=\int_0^a\frac12u^2\mathcal B(u)e^{u^2/2}\,du$,
and let $A_j$ bound $2\pi u|K(u)|$ on $[a/T,b/T]$. Splitting the integral
defining $R_\Delta(t)$ at $a$, bounding $e^{-(t^2-u^2)/2}\le1$ on $a\le u\le t$,
and interchanging the order of integration bounds the contribution of $[a,b]$
to the first term of \eqref{eq:prawitz} by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fubini-cell}
 \frac{A_j}{\pi}\left\{
 I_a\frac{E_1(a^2/2)-E_1(b^2/2)}2+
 \mathcal B_j\left(\frac{b^3-a^3}{18}
                  -\frac{a^3}{6}\log\frac ba\right)\right\},
\end{equation}
where for $a>0$ we take the minimum with the direct integral of
$|f_S(t)-e^{-t^2/2}|\le F_S(t)+e^{-t^2/2}$.

For the Gaussian term $G(T,s)$, subtracting the pole $\ii/(2\pi u)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular-kernel}
 2\left|K(u)-\frac{\ii}{2\pi u}\right|
   =(1-u)\sqrt{1+h(u)^2},\qquad
 h(u)=\frac1{\pi u}-\cot(\pi u)=\frac{2u}{\pi}\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac1{n^2-u^2}
\end{equation}
\citep[eq.~4.22.3]{DLMF}. Truncating the partial-fraction series at $M=8$
with remainder cap $c_M(s)=(\pi^2/6-\sum_{n=1}^M n^{-2})/(1-s^2/(M+1)^2)$
gives the smooth upper integrand $h_M(u)=(2u/\pi)(\sum_{n=1}^M(n^2-u^2)^{-1}+c_M(s))$,
so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-majorant}
 G(T,s)\le \int_0^s(1-u)\sqrt{1+h_M(u)^2}e^{-T^2u^2/2}\,du
       +\frac{E_1(T^2s^2/2)}{2\pi},
\end{equation}
which is enclosed with Arb alongside the elementary bound of
\citet[Lemma~4.1]{Shevtsova2012}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simple-gaussian}
 G(T,s)\le\int_0^s\left(1-u+\frac{\pi^2u^2}{18}\right)e^{-T^2u^2/2}\,du
               +\frac{E_1(T^2s^2/2)}{2\pi}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Signed smoothing over a threshold partition}
\label{sec:current-smoothing}

With $k_T(t)=K(t/T)/T$ and $g(t)=e^{-t^2/2}$, the signed smoothing inequality
of \citet{Prawitz1972} (see also \citet[eqs.~(2.2)--(2.6)]{Pinelis2013})
states that for every $x\in\R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-signed-prawitz}
 \Prob\{S\le x\}\le\frac12+2\Re\int_0^Tk_T(t)e^{-\ii xt}f_S(t)\,dt.
\end{equation}
Define the signed Gaussian defect at intermediate cutoff $a\in(0,Ts]$ by
\[
 \Gamma_{T,a}(x)=\frac12+2\Re\int_0^ak_T(t)e^{-\ii xt}g(t)\,dt-\Phi(x).
\]
On the frequency mesh $t_j=Tsj/N$ ($0\le j\le N$), let $C_j$ bound
$(2/L)\int_{t_j}^{t_{j+1}}|k_T(t)|\,|f_S(t)-g(t)|\,dt$, let $A_j$ bound
$2\int_{t_j}^{t_{j+1}}|k_T(t)f_S(t)|\,dt$, and let $H$ bound
$2\int_{Ts}^T|k_T(t)f_S(t)|\,dt$. Splitting \eqref{eq:current-signed-prawitz}
at $t_k$ ($1\le k\le N$) yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-cutoff}
 \frac{\Prob\{S\le x\}-\Phi(x)}L
 \le\sum_{j<k}C_j+\frac{\sum_{j\ge k}A_j+H+\Gamma_{T,t_k}(x)}L.
\end{equation}

When $x$ is restricted to a closed threshold interval $I\subset\R$, the
cosine-product comparison of Section~\ref{sec:current-reference} sharpens the
cell bound on $[l,r]=[t_j,t_{j+1}]$ ($j\ge1$). Let $[\alpha,\beta]$ enclose
$\Re(k_T(t)e^{-\ii xt})$ for $(t,x)\in[l,r]\times I$. If $|f_S-C_v|\le E_j$
and $C_v-g\in[\ell_j,h_j]$ on $[l,r]$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-signed-cell}
 2\Re\int_l^rk_T(t)e^{-\ii xt}\bigl(f_S(t)-g(t)\bigr)\,dt
 \le J_{j,I}:=W_jE_j+2(r-l)
 \max\{\ell_j\alpha,\ell_j\beta,h_j\alpha,h_j\beta\},
\end{equation}
where $W_j\ge2\int_l^r|k_T(t)|\,dt$. Because $C_v-g\le0$, cells on which
$\Re(k_T(t)e^{-\ii xt})>0$ contribute a negative drift term to $J_{j,I}$.
For $L\in[L_{lo},L_{hi}]$, defining $\mathcal N(z)=z/L_{lo}$ for $z\ge0$ and
$\mathcal N(z)=z/L_{hi}$ for $z<0$ bounds $J_{j,I}/L$ by $\mathcal N(J_{j,I})$.

Furthermore, on any frequency--threshold rectangle $[l,r]\times I$ where
$|D|\le R$ (with $D=(f_S-g)/L$) and $|f_S|\le r$, any choice of a rational
complex multiplier $h\in\mathbb Q[\ii]$ gives the two-disk support inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-disk-support}
 \Re(kD)
 =\Re\bigl((k-h)D\bigr)+\frac{\Re(hf_S)-g\Re h}{L}
 \le R|k-h|+\mathcal N(r|h|-g\Re h),
 \qquad k=k_T(t)e^{-\ii xt},
\end{equation}
which interpolates between the discrepancy bound ($h=0$) and the modulus
bound ($h=k$). Letting $B_{c,j,I}$ denote the minimum of $C_j$,
$\mathcal N(J_{j,I})$, and any certified two-disk bound \eqref{eq:two-disk-support}
for smoothing profile $c$, and letting $\gamma_{c,k,I}$ bound
$\sup_{x\in I}\Gamma_{T_c,t_k}(x)$, we obtain on $I$
\[
 U_{c,k,I}=\sum_{j<k}B_{c,j,I}
      +\mathcal N\left(\sum_{j\ge k}A_{c,j}+H_c+\gamma_{c,k,I}\right).
\]
Covering $[-X,X]$ (with $X=2$ or $X=69/32$) by a finite partition of closed
intervals $I$ and bounding $|x|\ge X$ by Cantelli's inequality gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-threshold-cover}
 \frac{\Delta}{L}\le
 \max\left\{\max_I\min_{c,k}U_{c,k,I},\ \frac{\Delta_{\rm ext}(X)}{L_{lo}}\right\},
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_{\rm ext}(X)\le1/(1+X^2)$, and for $X=2$ the refined
Cantelli--normal tail bound gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-tail-cantelli}
 \Delta_{\rm ext}(2)=\sup_{|x|\ge2}|\Prob(S\le x)-\Phi(x)|
 \le \frac15-\bigl(1-\Phi(2)\bigr)<0.17724986805182083,
\end{equation}
since $h(x)=(1+x^2)^{-1}-(1-\Phi(x))$ dominates $1-\Phi(x)$ and decreases
on $[2,\infty)$. Reflection $S\mapsto-S$ preserves the moment box, the even
reference function $C_v$, and the modulus bounds, so \eqref{eq:current-threshold-cover}
bounds both one-sided Kolmogorov discrepancies.

\subsection{Enclosing the signed Gaussian defect $\Gamma_{T,a}(x)$}

With $a=Ts$ and $h(u)=1/(\pi u)-\cot(\pi u)$, $\Gamma_{T,a}(x)$ has the exact
representation
\[
 \Gamma_{T,a}(x)=\int_0^s(1-u)e^{-T^2u^2/2}
       \{\cos(xTu)-h(u)\sin(xTu)\}\,du
 -\frac1\pi\int_s^\infty e^{-T^2u^2/2}\frac{\sin(xTu)}u\,du.
\]
Approximating $h(u)$ by its Taylor polynomial
$p_m(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(2\zeta(2j+2)/\pi)u^{2j+1}$ incurs an integral error
at most $\epsilon_m=\pi s^{2m+2}/[3(1-s^2)(2m+2)]$ and reduces the main term
to the entire integrand
\[
 \Gamma^{(m)}(x)=-\frac12\operatorname{erf}(x/\sqrt2)
 +\int_0^ag(t)\left[\frac{1-t/T}{T}
       \{\cos(xt)-p_m(t/T)\sin(xt)\}
       +\frac{x}{\pi}\sinc(xt)\right]dt,
\]
which is enclosed at grid nodes using Arb's Petras integration algorithm
\citep{Johansson2018}. Between adjacent threshold nodes $u<v$ with upper
enclosures $G_u,G_v$, the global second-derivative bound
\[
 |\Gamma_{T,a}''(x)|\le M_G:=\left(1+\frac{\pi s}{3(1-s^2)}\right)
 \left\{\frac{\sqrt{\pi/2}}T\operatorname{erf}(a/\sqrt2)
                       -s e^{-a^2/2}\right\}+\frac{e^{-a^2/2}}\pi
\]
yields the concave quadratic upper envelope
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-gaussian-chord}
 \Gamma_{T,a}(x)\le\frac{(v-x)G_u+(x-u)G_v}{v-u}
                       +\frac{M_G}{2}(x-u)(v-x)\qquad(u\le x\le v).
\end{equation}

\section{The certified interval calculation}\label{sec:certificate}

The interval cover spans $L\in[L_-,L_+]$, with exact binary64 rational
endpoints
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-endpoints}
 L_-=\frac{6456360425798343}{4611686018427387904}\approx0.0014,
 \qquad
 L_+=\frac{1362338887279575}{1125899906842624}\approx1.21.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Analytic bounds for small and large $L$}

For $0<L\le L_-\le0.01$, Corollary~4.18 of \citet{Shevtsova2012} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small-L}
 \frac{\Delta}{L}\le\CE+0.3413L^{1/3}
      \le\CE+0.3413L_-^{1/3}<\SmallUpper.
\end{equation}
For $L\ge L_+$, Cantelli's inequality and reflection give, for every
mean-zero, variance-one random variable $S$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cantelli}
 \Delta\le\sup_{x>0}\left(\Phi(x)-\frac{x^2}{1+x^2}\right)
      <\VarianceUpper,
\end{equation}
where the unique stationary point of $\Phi(x)-x^2/(1+x^2)$ on $(0,\infty)$
is enclosed with Arb. Dividing by $L_+$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:large-L}
 \frac{\Delta}{L}<\LargeUpper\qquad(L\ge L_+).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Partition of the intermediate moment domain}

\section{Proof of the scalar majorant for the imaginary error}\label{app:odd-majorant}

To prove \eqref{eq:current-J-majorant} for $0<x\le2$ and $y\ge0$, set $z=xy$,
$q=1+x^2/3$, and
\[
 R(x,z)=\frac{\cos z+x^2\sinc z-\cos x-x^2\sinc x}{z^2-x^2},
\]
extended by continuity across $z=x$. Multiplying the difference between the
right- and left-hand sides of \eqref{eq:current-J-majorant} by $x(z+x/2)$ gives
\[
 \mathcal L(x,z)=x(z+x/2)\left\{J_x(1)
       +\frac{J_x'(1)}{2x^2}(z^2-x^2)\right\}
       +\frac{(z-x)^2(z+x/2)^2}{4}
       -z^2(z+x)^2R(x,z)^2,
\]
which has a double zero at $z=x$. Put
\[
 w_n(u)=\frac1{(2n)!}+\frac{u}{(2n+1)!},\qquad
 S_n(x,z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}x^{2j}z^{2(n-1-j)}.
\]
For $r=m+n$, the homogeneous polynomial of degree $2r-2$ defined by exact
division,
\[
 Q_{mn}(x,z)=\frac{
 z^2(z+x)^2S_m(x,z)S_n(x,z)
 -\frac{4mn}{9}x^{2r-3}(z+x/2)
        \{(5-3r)x^2+(3r+1)z^2\}}{(z-x)^2},
\]
has positive rational coefficients and satisfies $Q_{mn}(1,1)\le8mnr^2$.
Expanding $R(x,z)$ in powers of $x$ and $z$ yields the power-series identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-odd-series}
 \frac{\mathcal L(x,z)}{(z-x)^2}
 =\frac{q^2x(x+4z)}{144}
   -\frac{(q^2-1)(z+x/2)^2}{4}
   +\sum_{r\ge3}(-1)^{r+1}
       \sum_{m=1}^{r-1}w_m(x^2)w_{r-m}(x^2)Q_{m,r-m}(x,z).
\end{equation}

Truncating \eqref{eq:current-odd-series} at $r=24$ and decomposing the
resulting polynomial into parity components as
$x^2F_1(x^2,z^2)+z^4F_0(z^2)+xzG(x^2,z^2)$ on $(x^2,z^2)\in[0,4]\times[0,49]$,
exact tensor Bernstein conversion gives minimum Bernstein coefficients of
$1/144$ for $F_1$, $>1/200$ for $F_0$, and $1/36$ for $G$. Using
$w_n(x^2)\le7/[3(2n)!]$ and $\sum_{m=1}^{r-1}m(r-m)/[(2m)!(2r-2m)!]=2^{2r-5}/(2r-2)!$,
the degree-$r$ term at $(x,z)=(2,7)$ is bounded by
$B_r=(49/1764)r^2 196^r/(2r-2)!$, so each component tail for $r\ge25$ is at
most $5B_{25}/18<1/1250$. Subtracting this tail leaves each component
strictly above $1/250$, proving $\mathcal L(x,z)\ge0$ for $0\le z\le7$.

For $z\ge7$, the bound $|A_x(y)-A_x(1)|\le2(x+1/x)$ gives
\[
 \frac{J_x(y)}{(x^2/4)h(y)}
 \le\frac{16(1+x^2)^2z^2}{(z-x)^4(z+x/2)^2}
 \le\frac{400}{(z-2)^4}\le\frac{16}{25}<1,
\]
while $J_x(1)\ge0$ and $J_x'(1)=2x^2k_0(4k_0-3\ell_0)/9>0$ on $(0,2]$
(with $k_0=2\sinc x-\cos x>0$, $\ell_0=(2-x^2)\sinc x-3\cos x$, and
$4k_0-3\ell_0\ge257/105$). Thus
\eqref{eq:current-J-majorant} holds for all $y\ge0$.

\section{Proof of the scalar bound for the cosine comparison}\label{sec:current-energy-cert}

To prove \eqref{eq:current-energy} on $0\le x\le1$, set for $x>0$
\[
 a_x=\frac{2x-x\sec x-\sin x}{x^2},\qquad
 U_x(w)=T_x(w)-a_x(w^2-1),\qquad
 V_x(y)=\frac{x^2\cos x}{2}U_x(y).
\]
Writing $s=\sin x$, $c=\cos x$, $A=(x+sc)/2$, $B=s^2$, and $D=x^2+2xsc$, we
have $V_x(y)=(\ii yc+s)e^{\ii xy}-\ii y+A(y^2-1)$. With $q_v=2B^2/3$,
$r_v=2BD/3-4B^2/9$, $\lambda=9/8$, and $\lambda_v=\lambda x^4c^2/4$, we
verify the global nonnegativity of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-energy-gap}
 \mathcal D_x(y):=h(y)\{q_v+(r_v/2)(y^2-1)+\lambda_vh(y)\}
                         -|V_x(y)|^2\ge0\qquad(y\ge0),
\end{equation}
where $h(y)=(y-1)^2(y+1/2)$. Because $\mathcal D_x(y)$ vanishes to fourth
order at $y=1$, we factor out $(y-1)^4$ explicitly. Using
$E_n(\ii v)=\sum_{m\ge0}(\ii v)^m/(m+n)!=((n-1)!)^{-1}\int_0^1(1-u)^{n-1}e^{\ii vu}\,du$
and $z=y-1$, we have
\[
 \frac{\mathcal D_x(y)}{(y-1)^4}
 =\lambda_v(y+1/2)^2+r_v(y/2+5/4)-A^2
                  -\Re\sum_{n=1}^4c_nE_n(\ii xz),
\]
where $c_4=-2x^4$, $c_3=2x^4+2\ii x^3(1+c^2)$,
$c_2=x^2(4Asc+2c^2)-2\ii x^2\{A(1+2c^2)-sc\}$, and
$c_1=-2xAc^2-2\ii xAsc$. Dividing further by $x^4$ removes the fourth-order
vanishing at $x=0$: with $S=\sinc x$, $u=\sinc(2x)=Sc$, $a=(1+u)/2$,
$R=\frac23S^2(1+2u)-\frac49S^4$, and $K_0=-uS^2-4x^2[\Re E_3(2\ii x)]^2$,
the quotient $\mathcal H(x,y)=\mathcal D_x(y)/[x^4(y-1)^4]$ is
\begin{align}
 \mathcal H(x,y)={}&\frac\lambda4c^2(y+1/2)^2+R(y/2+5/4)+K_0
       -2au(z+2)\Re E_2(\ii xz)\notag\\
 &-2\{ac^2z^2+[a(1+2c^2)-u]z+1\}\Re E_3(\ii xz)
       +2\{c^2z^2+(1+c^2)z+1\}\Re E_4(\ii xz),
       \label{eq:current-energy-zero}
\end{align}
with $\mathcal H(0,y)=(4y+1)/144+(\lambda-1)(y+1/2)^2/4>0$.

The certificate \path{certificates/scalar/energy.json} covers
$(x,y)\in[0,1/2]\times[0,48]$ using \eqref{eq:current-energy-zero} and
$(x,y)\in[1/2,1]\times[0,16]$ using $\mathcal D_x(y)/(y-1)^4$. On the
unbounded spatial tails, $|U_x(y)|\le P(y):=y^3+\frac{20}{21}y^2+\frac87y+\frac4{21}$
for $0\le x\le1/2$, and $\lambda h(y)^2-P(y)^2$ has strictly positive
expansion coefficients in $z=y-48\ge0$; similarly, for $1/2\le x\le1$ and
$y=16+z\ge16$, $\mathcal D_x(y)\ge\frac1{100}(y-1)^4(y+1/2)^2-(3y^2/4+2y+1)^2$
has strictly positive coefficients in $z\ge0$. Dividing
\eqref{eq:current-energy-gap} by $h(Y)$ and applying Cauchy--Schwarz to
$\E T_x(W)=\E U_x(W)$ yields \eqref{eq:current-energy}.

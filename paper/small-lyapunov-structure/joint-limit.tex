\section{Joint integer and phase estimates}\label{sec:joint}
We first work under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:first-alias}.
Write $J_i=K_i-\E K_i$, $K=\sum K_i$, $\mu=\E K$,
$T=\sum Z_i$, $A=\Var K$, $Q=\Var T$, $S=K-\mu+T$,
and $V=\Var S$. The retained core has size $M$, converges uniformly
in $W_3$ to $\Bern(p)$ for a fixed $0<p<1$, and retains all but
$o(M)$ of integer variance and cubic price. In addition
$Q=O(1)$ and $\Cov(K,T)=o(\sqrt M)$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-moments}
 A\sim p(1-p)M,\quad V=A+o(\sqrt A),\quad
 \gamma=\frac{\sum_i\E J_i^3}{A}\longrightarrow1-2p.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Global integer estimates]\label{lem:integer}
Uniformly over every integer $j$, with $x_j=(j-\mu)/\sqrt A$,
\begin{align}
 \Pp(K\le j)&=\Phi(x_j)+\frac{\phi(x_j)}{\sqrt A}
             \left\{\frac12+\frac\gamma6(1-x_j^2)\right\}
                        +o(A^{-1/2}),\label{eq:integer-edgeworth}\\
 \sqrt A\Pp(K=j)&=\phi(x_j)+o(1).\label{eq:integer-local}
\end{align}
Also, uniformly over every integer $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:all-layer}
                  \E[T^2\ind_{\{K=k\}}]\le K_0 Q/\sqrt A.
\end{equation}
All assertions remain uniform after a fixed number of coordinate deletions
when their shifts and moments satisfy the same displayed hypotheses.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each retained integer law has its probabilities at $0,1$ bounded below.
After at most any fixed number of deletions its characteristic product
obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:torus-damping}
        |\chi_{\rm deleted}(u)|\le K\exp(-cM\sin^2(u/2)),
                                                   \quad |u|\le\pi.
\end{equation}
Other factors can only reduce the modulus. Uniform $W_3$ convergence
to the bounded binary target gives uniform integrability of the core
third moments and convergence of the first three characteristic
derivatives on compact intervals. In a fixed small zero cell its
logarithm has expansion
\[
 \log\chi_I(u)=-W u^2/2-i\kappa_Iu^3/6+\mathcal E(u),\quad
 |\mathcal E(u)|\le M(\epsilon_n|u|^3+K u^4),\quad\epsilon_n\to0.
\]
The fourth power here is a remainder for the bounded target, not a
fourth-moment assumption on the actual laws. Gaussian-weighted Fourier
integration divided by $|u|$ gives error
$O(\epsilon_n/\sqrt M+1/M)$; the complement of the zero cell is
exponentially small by \eqref{eq:torus-damping}.

For precise CDF inversion take the Gaussian--Hermite lattice sequence
\[
 b_j=\frac{\phi((j-\mu_I)/\sqrt W)}{\sqrt W}
       \left\{1+\frac{\kappa_I}{6W^{3/2}}
                         H_3((j-\mu_I)/\sqrt W)\right\},\quad
 H_3(x)=x^3-3x.
\]
Its mass error is exponentially small by Poisson summation; correct
that mass at the integer nearest $\mu_I$. The corrected Fourier
alias numerator vanishes at zero and is
$O(\operatorname{poly}(M)e^{-cM}|u|)$ there, so division by the CDF
denominator $1-e^{-iu}$ is integrable. Euler--Maclaurin summation
gives the closed half-step $\phi(x)/(2\sqrt W)$ and the integrated
cubic term, with uniform $O(W^{-1})$ error. Inversion without the
denominator gives the local estimate for the core.

Convolve with the omitted integer block. Its centered variance and
additive cubic price are $o(A)$. Transfer the half-step and cubic
correction using their bounded derivatives and its $o(\sqrt A)$
first absolute moment. Replace only the smooth leading normal CDF
coordinatewise by same-variance normals; third-order Taylor replacement
costs $K\sum_{\rm rem}\E|J_i|^3/A^{3/2}=o(A^{-1/2})$.
This proves \eqref{eq:integer-edgeworth}. Convolution and uniform
continuity of $\phi$ prove \eqref{eq:integer-local}.

For \eqref{eq:all-layer}, expand the Fourier transform of
$T^2\ind_{\{K=k\}}$ without dividing by any characteristic factor.
The diagonal numerators have summed magnitude at most $Q$; cross
numerators have factors $\E[Z_i e^{iuJ_i}]$, whose magnitude is at
most $|u|\sqrt{\Var(K_i)\E Z_i^2}$ by centering. Their squared
sum is at most $u^2AQ$. The product after at most two extra deletions
is bounded by \eqref{eq:torus-damping}. Integration of
$KQ(1+Au^2)e^{-cAu^2}$ proves the assertion. These proofs are uniform
under fixed deletions; they use products with those factors removed
and no characteristic-function quotients outside the zero cell.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Joint local limit]\label{lem:joint-local}
Along any subsequence with $T\Rightarrow U$,
\[
 ((K-\mu)/\sqrt A,T)\Rightarrow(G,U),
\]
where $G$ is standard normal independent of $U$, and $\E U=0$.
For every sequence $(k-\mu)/\sqrt A\to x\in\R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-local}
        \sqrt A\Pp(K=k,T\in du)\Rightarrow\phi(x)\Law(U)(du).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Bounded second phase moments give tightness and first-moment uniform
integrability. Fix real $u,v$. On the core,
$g_i(v)=\E e^{ivZ_i}\to1$ uniformly. At $s=u/\sqrt A$, expand
the logarithm of $\E e^{isJ_i+ivZ_i}/g_i(v)$ through second order.
The total third-order error is $O(M|s|^3)=o(1)$. Its quadratic
coefficient divided by $A$ tends to $-1$, by uniform integrability
of $J_i^2$ and uniform $Z_i\to0$ in $L^2$; the squared first
coefficients contribute only $O(Q)/A$.

For the linear term use
$\E[J_i e^{ivZ_i}]=iv\E[J_iZ_i]+O_v(\E[|J_i|Z_i^2])$.
The summed error is $o(\sqrt M)$: the bounded target contributes
$KQ$, and the coupling remainder contributes
$o(\sqrt M)(\sum\E Z_i^4)^{1/2}$.
Correcting the $g_i(v)$ denominators costs
$K_v\sum_I(\E Z_i^2)^{3/2}=o(1)$.
The omitted covariance is $o(\sqrt A)$, so the core linear sum
is also $o(\sqrt A)$. We obtain
\[
 \chi_I(u/\sqrt A,v)=e^{-u^2/2+o(1)}\chi_{T_I}(v).
\]
Replacing the omitted normalized integer count by zero costs at most
$|u|\sqrt{\Var K_{\rm rem}/A}=o(1)$, and leaves its complete phase
law. Multiplication proves the joint weak limit.

For the local assertion invert only the integer coordinate. On a
fixed zero cell the twisted core factors have uniform $C^2$
convergence to the centered binary factor, are nonzero, and have
log-modulus second derivatives at most $-c$. Their summed first
derivative is at most $K_v\sqrt A$. Thus
\[
 \left|\prod_I\E e^{isJ_i+ivZ_i}\right|
          \le e^{K_v|s|\sqrt A-cAs^2}.
\]
Outside this cell the uniformly binary-like twisted product is
exponentially small. With $w=s\sqrt A$, dominated inversion and
the just-proved joint characteristic limit give
$\phi(x)\E e^{ivU}$. The masses converge by
\eqref{eq:integer-local}, and \eqref{eq:all-layer} gives tightness
of these finite positive phase measures. Fourier tests followed by
compact approximation establish \eqref{eq:joint-local}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Endpoints and localization]\label{lem:endpoints}
If $y/\sqrt A\to x\in\R$, let
$j=\lfloor y+\mu\rfloor$ and, after a subsequence,
$\theta=y+\mu-j\to\theta_0\in[0,1]$. Then
\begin{align}
 \limsup\sqrt A\{F_S(y)-\Phi(y/\sqrt V)\}
 &\le\phi(x)\{\tfrac12+\tfrac{1-2p}{6}(1-x^2)
                         -\E\{\theta_0-U\}\},\label{eq:closed-envelope}\\
 \liminf\sqrt A\{F_S^-(y)-\Phi(y/\sqrt V)\}
 &\ge\phi(x)\{-\tfrac12+\tfrac{1-2p}{6}(1-x^2)\}.
                                                        \label{eq:strict-envelope}
\end{align}
If $|y|/\sqrt A\to\infty$, both discrepancies multiplied by
$\sqrt A$ tend to zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The actual-variance and count-variance normal baselines differ
uniformly by $o(A^{-1/2})$ from \eqref{eq:joint-moments}.
The exact closed crossing difference from the count CDF at $j$ is
\[
 \sum_{\ell\in\Z}\left\{\Pp(K=j+\ell,T\le\theta-\ell)
                            -\ind_{\{\ell\le0\}}\Pp(K=j+\ell)\right\}.
\]
For $|\ell|>H\ge3$ any crossing requires $|T|\ge|\ell|-1$.
The all-layer bound therefore gives total crossing probability at
most $KQ/(H\sqrt A)$, uniformly over all real thresholds.
First truncate to $|\ell|\le H$. The moving closed half-lines in
\eqref{eq:joint-local} give an upper bound with
$\Pp(U\le\theta_0-\ell)$. Now let $H\to\infty$.
The resulting sum is
\[
 \phi(x)\E\lfloor\theta_0-U\rfloor
    =\phi(x)\{\theta_0-\E U-\E\{\theta_0-U\}\}.
\]
Combine it with the count expansion about the real threshold, whose
coefficient contains $-\theta_0+1/2$. This proves the closed bound.
For strict CDFs moving open half-lines give a lower bound with
$\lceil\theta_0-U\rceil-1\ge\theta_0-U-1$, proving the strict bound.
These conventions include limiting phase atoms at integer boundaries.

For thresholds leaving every standardized compact set, fix $H$ first.
The finitely many crossing layers have masses $o(A^{-1/2})$ by the
global local estimate; the remaining layers cost at most
$KQ/(H\sqrt A)$. The global integer Edgeworth correction also tends
to zero there. Let the row tend to infinity and then $H\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Modular concentration and transport}
For the original near-Esseen rows take $p=\pe$. Their original
price in gap units $P$ and signed cubic $\kappa$ satisfy
$P/V\to\re$ and $\kappa/V\to\te$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:core}.
The negative strict envelope has maximal limiting normalized value
$\phz(1/2-\te/6)/\re<\ce$. The positive envelope has unique
standardized maximum at zero. Near-extremality, the localization lemma,
and \eqref{eq:closed-envelope} force $x=0$ and
$\E\{\theta_0-U\}=0$, so $U$ lies on one integer coset.
Consequently there are phases $\vartheta_n$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modular}
              \E\dist(S-\vartheta_n,\Z)\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Every subsequence admits this argument; minimizing the bounded
continuous circle-distance expectation over the compact circle gives
phases for the full sequence.

We give the transport implication directly. For $q\in(0,1)$, let
$t=2q-1$ and define the continuous periodic triangle
\[
 P_q(u)=\begin{cases}-2(1-q)u,&0\le u\le q,\\
                         2q(u-1),&q\le u\le1.
          \end{cases}
\]
Its mean is $-q(1-q)$ and its nonzero Fourier coefficients are
$(1-e^{-2\pi ikq})/(2\pi^2k^2)$. Zero bias gives
$\E S^*=\kappa/(2V)$ and
$|\E e^{iuS^*}|\le1/(|u|\sqrt V)$, by its defining identity.
The test $-tx+P_q(x-\vartheta)$ is $1$-Lipschitz. The Wasserstein
dual and its absolutely convergent Fourier series yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modular-dual}
 W_1(S,S^*)\ge\frac{t\kappa}{2V}+q(1-q)
  -2\E\dist(S-\vartheta,\Z)-\frac{\zeta(3)}{\pi^3\sqrt V}.
\end{equation}
With $q=\qe$ and \eqref{eq:modular}, the lower limit is
$\te^2/2+\pe\qe=\re/2$. The original additive bound
$W_1(S,S^*)\le P/(2V)\to\re/2$ matches it, proving
$\mathcal B(X_n)\to1$.

Modular concentration also strengthens the actual rounding.
It gives $|\E e^{2\pi iS}|\to1$, hence independence implies
$\sum_i(1-r_i^2)\le-\log\prod_i r_i^2\to0$.
The argument-aligned errors of Lemma~\ref{lem:first-alias} therefore
have $Q=\sum_i\E Z_i^2\to0$. Since $|Z_i|\le1$, their actual
independent couplings give
\[
 \sum_iW_2^2(Y_i,J_i)\le Q\to0,\qquad
 \sum_iW_3^3(Y_i,J_i)\le Q\to0.
\]
Restore the gap units to obtain \eqref{eq:strong-lattice}. This
completes Theorem~\ref{thm:near}.

\begin{remark}
The finite inequality $C\le\ce\mathcal B(X)$ is false. To see why,
replace the part of the standard normal law in $[-a,a]$ by atoms at
$-a,0,a$, preserving its mass and second moment. If
$P=\Pp(|Z|\le a)$ and $r=\E[Z^2\ind_{\{|Z|\le a\}}]/(a^2P)$,
these masses are $Pr/2,P(1-r),Pr/2$, with $0<r<1/3$.
The discrepancy is $P(1-r)/2$. Its zero-biased law instead replaces
the same interval by a uniform law, and
$W_1=(aP/2)((1-r)^2+r^2)$. The ratio of the Berry--Esseen statistic
to $\mathcal B$ is
$(1-r)/(2a((1-r)^2+r^2))\ge1/(2a)$, which diverges as $a\downarrow0$.
Both statistics tend to zero. Theorem~\ref{thm:near} uses competitive
near-Esseen value as well as small $L$.
\end{remark}

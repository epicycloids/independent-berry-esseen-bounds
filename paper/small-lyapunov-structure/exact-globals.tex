\section{Full contacts and every support phase}\label{sec:contacts}
For the remainder of the proof each row is an actual full global
maximizer and $L\to0$. The active count is at least $L^{-2}$, by
Lyapunov and the power-mean inequality, so its horizon tends to infinity.
An IID Esseen array at the same horizon has ratio tending to $\ce$
\cite[pp.~160--163]{Esseen1956};
globality and \eqref{eq:asymptotic-upper} therefore give $C\to\ce$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:near} and all its preceding estimates apply.

\begin{lemma}[Original support coercivity]\label{lem:coercivity}
Normalize total variance to one and let $R=L$.
For all sufficiently late global rows every original support point
satisfies $|u|\le246R^{1/3}$. At a positive closed maximizing threshold $z$,
put $D=F(z)-\Phi(z)$, $\alpha=\phi(z)$, $A_0=(3D+z\alpha)/2$.
For coordinate $i$, write $v=\E X_i^2$, $\rho=\E|X_i|^3$ and
$M_i=\E[X_i|X_i|]$. The full contact inequality is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-contact-unit}
 F_{-i}(z-u)+(\alpha+3CM_i)u+A_0u^2-C|u|^3
       \le F(z)+A_0v-C\rho,
\end{equation}
with equality at every topological support point of $X_i$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We use the independent-coordinate form of the contamination derivation
of \citet[Section~2.1, equations~(7)--(12)]{HeChengCurrent2026}.
Their variation changes the common IID law; here it changes just one
original coordinate and retains its contribution to the additive
absolute cubic price.
Replace the unstandardized coordinate law by
$(1-e)\Law(X_i)+e\delta_u$, keep its unstandardized closed threshold, and recenter
and restandardize. The derivatives of mean, total variance, and
original absolute cubic price are respectively
$u,u^2-v,|u|^3-\rho-3uM_i$.
The ratio derivative gives \eqref{eq:full-contact-unit} for every
real $u$. Its integral under $X_i$ is equality. The deleted closed
CDF is upper semicontinuous, so equality extends from a full-measure
set to every topological support point, including arbitrarily small
atoms.

Eventually $C\ge199/500$, $R<1/750$ and the general bound of
\citet[Theorem~2.1]{ChenShao2001} gives $C\le41/10$.
Lyapunov gives $v^{3/2}\le\rho\le R$ and $v<1/2$.
The deleted normal approximation has uniform error at most
$(123/10)R$. Put $s=\sqrt{1-v}$. Uniform Gaussian scale estimates are
\[
 |\Phi(z/s)-\Phi(z)-z\phi(z)v/2|\le2v^2,
\]
\[
 |\phi(z/s)/s-\phi(z)|\le3v/5,\qquad
 |z\phi(z/s)/(2s^3)-z\phi(z)/2|\le4v.
\]
They follow by differentiating in $v$ and using
$\|(1-x^2)\phi(x)\|_\infty=\phz<2/5$ and
$|x(3-x^2)\phi(x)|<4$. The cubic translation remainder is
at most $\phz|u|^3/(6s^3)<|u|^3/5$.
Substitute the deleted approximation into contact equality and use
$|M_i|\le v$, $D\le(41/10)R$, $v^2\le\rho\le R$. This gives
\[
 (C-1/5)|u|^3\le\frac{1023}{40}R+\frac{129}{10}v|u|
                      +\left(\frac{123}{20}R+4v\right)u^2.
\]
For $|u|=R^{1/3}w$, divide by $R$ and use $v\le R^{2/3}$:
\[
 \frac{99}{500}w^3\le\frac{1023}{40}+\frac{129}{10}w
                                             +\frac{203}{20}w^2.
\]
For $w\ge1$ the right side is at most $(389/8)w^2$, so
$w\le48625/198<246$. The other case is immediate. Reflection
of a strict negative threshold gives the same bound.
\end{proof}

In the selected gap units write the original laws as $Y_i=J_i+Z_i$
from Lemma~\ref{lem:first-alias}. Put
\[
 V=\sum_i\E Y_i^2,\quad d=V^{-1/2},\quad P=\sum_i\E|Y_i|^3,
 \quad Q=\sum_i\E Z_i^2,\quad T=\sum_iZ_i,
\]
and select a positive closed threshold $y$, with $z=y/\sqrt V$.
The preceding results give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contact-inputs}
 P/V\to\re,\quad \kappa/V\to\te,\quad Q\to0,\quad
 z\to0,\quad D/d\to\phz(1/2+\te/6),\quad
 \Cov(K,T)=o(\sqrt V).
\end{equation}
The support bound is $|Y_i|\le H=O(V^{1/3})$.
The uniform retained core and resource retention give
$\sup_i\E|Y_i|^3=o(V)$. Consequently
$\max_i\E Y_i^2=o(V)$ and $\sum_i\E Y_i^4=O(HV)$.

\begin{lemma}[Whole-support fractional confinement]\label{lem:whole-phase}
For the actual lifts just constructed,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:whole-phase}
       \eta_n:=\max_i\|Z_i\|_\infty\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The nearest representative of $\{y+\mu\}$ on the circle tends to zero.
If $t=\E|T|>0$, then $Q=o(t)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The modular concentration and $Q\to0$ already proved imply that
$\{y+\mu\}$ tends to zero on the circle. This also follows directly
from the saturated closed envelope and $T\to0$ in $L^2$.

Delete any one coordinate. Let its original variance be $v_i$, its
deleted original variance be $w_i=V-v_i$, and its integer deleted
variance be $A_i$. Uniformly in the deletion,
\[
 w_i\sim V,\quad A_i-w_i=o(\sqrt V),\quad
 \kappa(K_{-i})/A_i-\kappa(Y_{-i})/w_i=o(1).
\]
The total covariance is $o(\sqrt V)$ and each individual deleted
covariance is at most $(H+1)\sqrt Q=o(\sqrt V)$.
Cubic differences are at most
$K(\sqrt{HVQ}+HQ+Q)=o(V)$. The retained core still damps all
deleted products. Lemma~\ref{lem:integer} therefore applies uniformly
after these deletions. Its fourth-moment version has a zero-cell
CDF remainder $O(H/V)=o(d)$ and exponentially small outer-frequency
error; it follows by the same inversion with log remainder
$O(HV u^4)$.

For $|u|\le H$, set
\[
 x_i=(y-u)/\sqrt{w_i},\quad \gamma_i=\kappa(Y_{-i})/w_i,\quad
 G_i(u)=\Phi(x_i)+w_i^{-1/2}\phi(x_i)
                      \{1/2+\gamma_i(1-x_i^2)/6\},
\]
and $\tau_i(u)=\{y-u+\mu_{-i}\}$. If $\tau_i(u)$ stays in
$[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]$, the deleted all-layer bound gives a crossing
error at most
\[
 \sum_{\ell\in\Z}\frac{KQd}{(\ell-\tau_i(u))^2}=o(d).
\]
No stationary covariance is needed after deletion: the quotient-free
second-moment integrand is bounded by
$K[Q+A_iQu^2]e^{-cVu^2}$. Thus uniformly over this phase range,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:away-phase}
               F_{-i}(y-u)=G_i(u)-d\phz\tau_i(u)+o(d).
\end{equation}

In gap units the contact score and level are
\[
 g_i(u)=F_{-i}(y-u)+b_i u+A_0u^2-\lambda|u|^3,\quad
 \ell_i=F(y)+A_0v_i-\lambda\rho_i,
\]
\[
 b_i=d\phi(z)+3\lambda\E[Y_i|Y_i|],\quad
 \lambda=D/P=Cd^3,\quad A_0=(3D+z\phi(z))/(2V).
\]
They satisfy $g_i\le\ell_i$, with equality on all original supports.
We claim
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smooth-contact-bound}
       G_i(u)+b_iu+A_0u^2-\lambda|u|^3-\ell_i\le o(d)
\end{equation}
uniformly over $i$ and $|u|\le H$.
At $u=0$ the leading deleted-variance term is
$z\phi(z)v_i/(2V)$, exactly the same as its term in $A_0v_i$.
The second-order error is $O(d^4v_i^2)\le O(d^4H^4)=o(d)$;
the remaining level terms are $O(d^3v_i+d^3\rho_i)=o(d)$.
For changes from zero to $u$, cancel the normal quadratic threshold term
before expansion. The linear derivative error is $O(d^3v_i)$,
the remaining quadratic terms are $O(d^3u^2)$, and the normal cubic
remainder is at most $(\phz/6+o(1))d^3|u|^3$.
The even Edgeworth correction has changes at most
$K(d^2|z||u|+d^3u^2)$. Thus, since $C\to\ce>\phz/6$,
the left side of \eqref{eq:smooth-contact-bound} is at most
\[
 -cd^3|u|^3+K d^3u^2+K d^3v_i|u|+K d^2|z||u|+o(d).
\]
Young's inequality absorbs the positive terms, leaving
$O(d^3+d^3v_i^{3/2}+d^{3/2}|z|^{3/2})=o(d)$, since
$v_i^{3/2}\le\rho_i=o(V)$. This proves the claim.

If an original support phase $u+\E K_i$ stayed a positive circle
distance from zero, its deleted phase $\tau_i(u)$ would stay away
from the boundary because $\{y+\mu\}\to0$. Equations
\eqref{eq:away-phase} and \eqref{eq:smooth-contact-bound} would give
$g_i(u)<\ell_i$, contradicting its exact original contact.
Hence every support phase approaches its integer coset uniformly.
The aligned errors lie in $[-1/2,1/2]$ and their means tend uniformly
to zero, proving \eqref{eq:whole-phase}.
Finally independence gives
$\E T^4\le3Q^2+\eta_n^2Q$; interpolation implies
$t\ge Q/\sqrt{3Q+\eta_n^2}$, hence $Q=o(t)$ when $t>0$.
\end{proof}

\section{Relative conditional phase moments}\label{sec:relative}
We now retain the actual phase norm in the local estimates. Before
conditioning, we move the same integer labels to a stationary peak
of the full characteristic product. This supplies the stronger
covariance estimate required below.

\begin{lemma}[The same lifts at a full peak]\label{lem:full-peak}
After a common scaling tending to one, the original laws and the same
integer labels satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-hypotheses}
 A=\Var K\to\infty,\quad |J_i|\le H=O(A^{1/3}),\quad
 \sum_i\E|J_i|^3=O(A),\quad Q\to0,\quad
                 |\Cov(K,T)|\le KHQ,
\end{equation}
as well as \eqref{eq:whole-phase}. The retained core still has at
least $cA$ laws with masses at two adjacent integers bounded below
by $c>0$; the selected threshold has circle phase tending to zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For bounded $x$ the integer sum satisfies
$|\E e^{i(2\pi+x/\sqrt A)\sum J_i}|\to e^{-x^2/2}$ uniformly
on compact $x$ intervals, by its cubic $O(A)$ log expansion.
The original and integer characteristic functions differ there by at
most $K\E|T|\to0$. The actual full modulus at $2\pi$ tends to
one, while its values at $2\pi\pm1/\sqrt A$ tend to $e^{-1/2}$.
Its maximum on that interval is interior, at
$s=2\pi+x_n/\sqrt A$ with $x_n\to0$.
Put $a=s/(2\pi)=1+\delta$ and keep exactly the same $J_i$:
\[
        Y_i^{\rm new}=aY_i,\qquad Z_i^{\rm new}=Z_i+\delta Y_i.
\]
Then $\delta\sqrt A\to0$,
$\eta_{\mathrm{new}}\le\eta+|\delta|H\to0$, and
$Q_{\mathrm{new}}\le2Q+2\delta^2V\to0$.
The selected threshold's phase changes by $\delta y=o(1)$.

Align the individual arguments by
$\beta_i=(2\pi)^{-1}\arg\E e^{2\pi iZ_i^{\rm new}}$.
The errors $E_i=Z_i^{\rm new}-\beta_i$ remain inside
$[-1/2,1/2]$; their nearest integer labels are unchanged.
Centering them returns $Z_i^{\rm new}$ exactly. The sine identity
and $\E Z_i^{\rm new}=0$ give
\[
 |\beta_i|\le K\E|Z_i^{\rm new}|^3
 \le K\eta_{\mathrm{new}}\E(Z_i^{\rm new})^2,\qquad
 \sum_i\E E_i^2=Q_{\mathrm{new}}(1+o(1)).
\]
Full log-modulus stationarity at $2\pi$ is
$\sum_i\E[Y_i^{\rm new}\sin(2\pi E_i)]/r_i=0$.
Removing denominators and replacing sine by its linear term costs
at most $KH\sum_i\E E_i^2$, using bounded original support and
$1-r_i\le K\E E_i^2$.
Thus $|\Cov(\sum Y_i^{\rm new},T_{\mathrm{new}})|\le KHQ_{\mathrm{new}}$.
Subtract $Q_{\mathrm{new}}$ to obtain \eqref{eq:relative-hypotheses}.
All resource limits are invariant under the common scaling. No
stationarity has been imposed on the rounded comparator.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A multiplier first-moment bound]\label{lem:multiplier}
For independent centered $Z_i$ and $t=\E|\sum Z_i|$, any
same-coordinate multipliers $W_i$ with $\|W_i\|_\infty\le M_W$
satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multiplier}
 \left|\sum_i\E W_iZ_i\right|
       \le8t\left(\sum_i\E W_i^2\right)^{1/2}+5M_Wt.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $Z_i'$ be independent copies and $D_i=Z_i-Z_i'$. By the
second/fourth-moment Khintchine estimate,
$\E(\sum D_i^2)^{1/2}\le2\sqrt3\,t=:C_0t$.
Truncate at $b=4C_0t$. Markov gives probability at least $3/4$
that all other differences are small. Disjoint events with exactly
one large difference therefore give
$\sum\E[|D_i|\ind_{\{|D_i|>b\}}]\le(4/3)C_0t<5t$.
For $U=\sum\E[D_i^2\ind_{\{|D_i|\le b\}}]$, independence and
second/fourth interpolation give
$U/\sqrt{U+b^2}\le\E(\sum D_i^2\ind_{\{|D_i|\le b\}})^{1/2}
\le C_0t$, hence $U<60t^2$.
Since $\E W_iZ_i'=0$, apply Cauchy to the small differences and
the uniform multiplier bound to the others. If $t=0$, independence
forces every centered phase to be zero and the result is immediate.
\end{proof}

The inputs for the next lemma are the independent coordinate pairs
$(K_i,Z_i)$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:full-peak}, with $K_i\in\Z$,
$J_i=K_i-\E K_i$, and $\E Z_i=0$. They obey
\eqref{eq:relative-hypotheses}, $\max_i\|Z_i\|_\infty\to0$, and have
a core of at least $cA$ laws with two adjacent integer masses bounded
below. This core supplies the uniform local estimates after finitely
many deletions. The integer label and residual within one pair may
be dependent. There is no lower bound on the positive norm $t$.

\begin{lemma}[Relative conditional first moments]\label{lem:conditional-relative}
Under these hypotheses, whenever $t=\E|T|>0$, for
each fixed $B_0$ and uniformly over $|k-\mu|\le B_0\sqrt A$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-relative}
 c\,t\le\E(|T|\mid K=k)\le Kt,\qquad
                      |\E(T\mid K=k)|=o(t).
\end{equation}
The constants and the relative errors are uniform even if $t$ tends
to zero arbitrarily fast.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take two independent row copies, with differences
$D_i=Z_i-Z_i'$ and $D=\sum D_i$. Then
$t\le\E|D|\le2t$ and
$\E(\sum D_i^2)^{1/2}\le2\sqrt3\,t$.
Fix a large constant $B$, truncate at $b=Bt$, and put
\[
 d_i=D_i\ind_{\{|D_i|\le b\}},\quad W=\sum_i d_i,\quad
 U=\sum_i\E d_i^2,\quad
 \Delta=\sum_i\E[|D_i|\ind_{\{|D_i|>b\}}].
\]
The $d_i$ are independent, centered, and antisymmetric under exchange
of copies. The preceding truncation calculation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-copy-truncation}
 \Delta\le Kt,\quad \sum_i\Pp(|D_i|>b)\le K/B,\quad
                      U\le K_Bt^2,\qquad K_B=O(B).
\end{equation}
In the last bound use $\E S_b^2\le U^2+b^2U$ and
$\E\sqrt{S_b}\le2\sqrt3\,t$, where $S_b=\sum d_i^2$.

We establish, for bounded standardized count positions, the relative
two-count estimates
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-count-moments}
 \E(W^2\mid K=k_1,K'=k_2)=U+o(t^2),\qquad
 \E(W^4\mid K=k_1,K'=k_2)\le C_Bt^4.
\end{equation}
Here $C_B$ is distinct from the variance constant $K_B$:
we may take $C_B=O(B^3)$ for each fixed count range.
Indeed its leading terms are $U^2+b^2U$, and
$U\le K_Bt^2$ with $K_B=O(B)$ and $b=Bt$.
We fix $B$ before letting the row index tend to infinity.
The estimates hold uniformly after one pair coordinate is specified and
removed; its integer shifts have size $O(H)=o(\sqrt A)$.

Here are the Fourier estimates retaining their relative factors.
For $s=(s_1,s_2)$ in $\|s\|\le c/H$, let
$f_i(u)=\E e^{iuJ_i}$,
$\Psi(s)=\prod_i f_i(s_1)f_i(s_2)$, and
\[
 m_{ir}(s)=\frac{\E[d_i^r e^{i(s_1J_i+s_2J_i')}]}
                                      {f_i(s_1)f_i(s_2)},\quad1\le r\le4.
\]
The denominators are bounded away from zero in this cell only.
Antisymmetry gives $\E d_i=0$ and
$c_i=\E J_id_i=-\E J_i'd_i$.
For the untruncated differences the summed covariance is
$\Cov(K,T)=O(HQ)$; since $|Z_i|\le1$, interpolation gives $Q\le2t$
eventually. Removing tails costs at most $2H\Delta$ and removing
one pair at most $4Ht$, so $|\sum c_i|\le KHt$ uniformly.
Lemma~\ref{lem:multiplier}, applied to the independent $d_i$, gives
\[
 \sum_i\E[(J_i^2+J_i'^2)|d_i|]
                         \le K(\sqrt{HA}+H^2)t,
\]
\[
 \sum_i a_i|c_i|\le K(H^2\sqrt A+H^3)t,\qquad a_i=\E J_i^2.
\]
For these estimates the multipliers are respectively
$(J_i^2+J_i'^2)\operatorname{sign}d_i$ and
$a_i|J_i|\operatorname{sign}d_i$; their summed squared moments are
$O(HA)$ and $O(H^4A)$, and their uniform bounds are $O(H^2),O(H^3)$.
Also $\E|W|\le\E|D|+\Delta=O(t)$.
Numerator and denominator Taylor expansions now give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-Fourier}
 \left|\sum_i m_{i1}\right|\le Kt\left\{H\|s\|
  +(\sqrt{HA}+H^2)\|s\|^2+(H^2\sqrt A+H^3)\|s\|^3\right\}.
\end{equation}
All terms are $o(t)$ on the Gaussian integration scale. Further,
\[
 \left|\sum_i m_{i2}-U\right|\le KU(H\|s\|+H^2\|s\|^2),\quad
 \sum_i|m_{i1}|^2\le KH^2U\|s\|^2,
\]
\[
 \sum_i|m_{i2}|\le KU,\qquad\sum_i|m_{i3}|\le KbU,
                         \qquad\sum_i|m_{i4}|\le Kb^2U.
\]
The exact second transform is
$\Psi[(\sum m_{i1})^2+\sum(m_{i2}-m_{i1}^2)]$.
Its leading term $U\Psi$ integrates to
$U\Pp(K=k_1)\Pp(K'=k_2)$ exactly, and its remaining Gaussian
integral is $o(t^2/A)$. For the fourth transform use
\[
 \Psi(M_1^4+6M_1^2C_2+3C_2^2+4M_1C_3+C_4),
\]
where $M_1=\sum m_{i1}$ and $C_r$ are the sums of the individual
cumulant polynomials:
\[
 C_2=\sum(m_{i2}-m_{i1}^2),\quad
 C_3=\sum(m_{i3}-3m_{i2}m_{i1}+2m_{i1}^3),
\]
\[
 C_4=\sum(m_{i4}-4m_{i3}m_{i1}-3m_{i2}^2
                         +12m_{i2}m_{i1}^2-6m_{i1}^4).
\]
The displayed bounds give integral at most $C_Bt^4/A$.
Outside the zero cell use quotient-free partition formulas and
delete at most four pair coordinates. Every singleton keeps a
relative factor because antisymmetry gives
\[
 |\E[d_i e^{i(s_1J_i+s_2J_i')}]|
 \le K\|s\|\E[|d_i||J_i-J_i'|],\qquad
 \sum_i\E[|d_i||J_i-J_i'|]\le K\sqrt A\,t.
\]
The last bound is again Lemma~\ref{lem:multiplier}.
Other numerators sum to at most $U,bU,b^2U$.
Torus damping leaves a fixed polynomial in $A$ times $t^2$ or $t^4$
times $e^{-cA/H^2}$, which is negligible as $H=O(A^{1/3})$.
The ordinary integer local limit gives
$\Pp(K=k_1)\Pp(K'=k_2)\ge c(B_0)/A$ and deletion ratios $1+o(1)$
uniform over shifts $O(H)$. Divide these relative integrals to obtain
\eqref{eq:two-count-moments}.

If $\Delta\ge\epsilon t$, condition on an actually large coordinate
and its two integer labels. Fixed-deletion ratios preserve its tail
integral up to $1+o(1)$. Other large coordinates have conditional
union probability $O(1/B)$, and the truncated remainder has
conditional first absolute moment at most $K\sqrt B\,t$.
On the disjoint events with exactly one large coordinate,
\[
 \E[|D_i+\sum_{j\ne i}d_j|\ind_{\{\text{others small}\}}
                     \mid\text{labels and counts}]
 \ge(1-K/B)|D_i|-K\sqrt B\,t\ge |D_i|/2
\]
for sufficiently large fixed $B$. Thus conditional $\E|D|\ge c\Delta$.
Otherwise $\Delta<\epsilon t$ and
$\E|W|\ge(1-\epsilon)t$, so $U\ge(1-\epsilon)^2t^2$.
Interpolation in \eqref{eq:two-count-moments} gives conditional
$\E|W|\ge c_Bt$. Conditional discarded-tail first moments are
at most $2\Delta$, by one-pair deletion ratios. Choose
$\epsilon<c_B/4$ after $B$, to obtain conditional $\E|D|\ge c_Bt/2$.
The same decomposition gives the upper bound $K(B)t$.
With $k_1=k_2=k$, the two rows are conditionally independent and
$\E(|D|\mid K=K'=k)\le2\E(|T|\mid K=k)$, proving the lower bound.

For the one-count mean, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:multiplier} directly
to $Z_i$. Its zero-cell singleton sum has the analogue of
\eqref{eq:relative-Fourier}, using
$|\Cov(K,T)|=O(HQ)=O(Ht)$. Gaussian inversion gives $o(t/\sqrt A)$,
and quotient-free outer inversion retains $t$ by the singleton
multiplier bound. Divide by the central mass to obtain mean $o(t)$.
Finally conditional Jensen gives
\[
 \E(|T|\mid K=k)\le\E(|T-T'|\mid K=K'=k)
                                      +|\E(T\mid K=k)|\le K(B)t.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{An exact integer endpoint comparison}\label{sec:comparison}
Under the preceding hypotheses, $\eta\to0$, $Q=o(t)$ when $t>0$,
and the nearest selected integer phase $\theta$ tends to zero.
For its central layer $k$, put
\[
 w(\theta)=\Pp(T>\theta\mid K=k),\qquad
                        f(\theta)=\theta+w(\theta).
\]
The strict inequality in $w$ is dictated by the closed original CDF.

\begin{lemma}[Relative closed loss]\label{lem:closed-loss}
If $t>0$, then at the selected layer and phase
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-closed-loss}
        f(\theta)\ge ct,\qquad
                   |\theta|+w(\theta)+t\le K f(\theta).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Condition on $K=k$ and truncate $T$ to
$X=T\ind_{\{|T|\le1/4\}}$. The all-layer second-moment bound,
divided by the central count mass, gives $\E(T^2\mid K=k)\le KQ$.
Its CDF, mean and first absolute moment change under this truncation
by $O(Q)=o(t)$. Let $m=\E X=o(t)$ and $s=\E|X|\ge ct$.
For $0\le\theta\le1/4$, with $u=\Pp(X>\theta)$,
\[
        s=2\E X_+-m\le2\theta+u/2-m\le2(\theta+u)-m.
\]
For $\theta=-b\in[-1/4,0)$,
\[
 m\le(1/4+b)u-b\le u/2-b,\qquad s\le u/2-m.
\]
Thus $\theta+u\ge s+2m$ in the negative case, and
$u\le2(\theta+u-m)$, $b\le\theta+u-2m$.
These inequalities bound $|\theta|+u+t$ by a fixed multiple of
$\theta+u$. Transfer the $o(t)$ truncation errors back to $T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Collapse of original phase noise]\label{prop:collapse}
Every sufficiently late actual full global row has $T=0$ almost surely.
Each original coordinate is then exactly on its constructed lattice coset.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $t>0$ along a subsequence. Retain exactly the same integer
labels and replace the original laws $Y_i=J_i+Z_i$ by the centered
integer laws $J_i$ at the same horizon. Write $k$ for the integer
nearest $y+\mu$ and $\theta=y+\mu-k$. The exact closed-CDF difference is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-crossing}
 F_Y(k+\theta-\mu)=F_K(k)-p_kw(\theta)+E_{\rm cross},\qquad
                         |E_{\rm cross}|\le KdQ.
\end{equation}
The central term is exact. Every other count layer is at distance
at least $1/2$ from this threshold. Its crossing probability is bounded
by the all-layer second moment divided by its squared distance;
summing the inverse-square series proves the stated error.

Let $A=\Var K$, $V=\Var\sum Y_i$, $P_Y=\sum\E|Y_i|^3$, and
$P_J=\sum\E|J_i|^3$. Full-peak covariance gives
$|V-A|\le KHQ$. The integer local limit gives
$p_k=d\phi(z)+o(d)$, uniformly at this bounded standardized threshold.
The normal threshold shift is $d\phi(z)\theta+O(d^2\theta^2)$;
changing its variance costs $O(HQ/A)=o(df)$.
In \eqref{eq:exact-crossing}, $dQ=o(df)$ and both $|\theta|$ and
$w$ are controlled by $f$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:closed-loss}.
Consequently the selected discrepancies satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selected-comparison}
          D_Y=D_J-d\phi(z)f(\theta)+o(df(\theta)).
\end{equation}
The local-mass error multiplies $w$ and the normal error multiplies
$\theta$, so they remain relative to this actual loss.

For original cubic price use the centered path
$Y_i(s)=J_i+sZ_i$, $0\le s\le1$. Its support is $O(H)$, its
summed cubic moments are $O(A)$, and its fourth moments sum to $O(HA)$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:multiplier}, with
$W_i=3Y_i(s)|Y_i(s)|$, gives uniformly along the path
\[
 \left|\frac{d}{ds}\sum_i\E|Y_i(s)|^3\right|
           \le K(\sqrt{HA}+H^2)t=o(At).
\]
Thus $|P_Y-P_J|=o(At)$, retaining the original absolute price.
Both prices are asymptotic to $\re A$. The prefactors
$V^{3/2}/P_Y$ and $A^{3/2}/P_J$ differ by relative $o(t)$.
At the selected competitive threshold $D_Y=O(d)$, and
\eqref{eq:selected-comparison} gives $D_J=O(d)$ as well.
Multiplying that exact comparison by the prefactors yields a
positive ratio gain at least $cf\ge ct$, for all sufficiently late rows.
The full objective of the integer competitor is at least its selected
closed branch. It therefore contradicts full globality.

Hence $t=0$ eventually. Independence and centeredness imply
$\Var T=\sum_i\Var Z_i=0$, so every original $Z_i=0$ almost surely.
No collision set has been held fixed during the finite comparison.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact lattice contacts and binary support}\label{sec:lattice}
The original laws now lie exactly on individual cosets of the same
unit lattice in the selected gap units. The support coercivity bound
makes them finite, while $P/V\to\re$, $\kappa/V\to\te$ and $z\to0$.
The retained core has two adjacent atoms with masses bounded below,
so all deleted characteristic products satisfy torus damping.

\begin{lemma}[Adjacent original contacts]\label{lem:adjacent}
Eventually every original law is binary at adjacent integer sites or zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $v_i=\E Y_i^2$, $w_i=V-v_i$, and let $c_i(x)$ be the mass
function of the deleted original sum. For $x$ on its lattice coset,
Fourier inversion with multiplier $e^{-iu}-1$ gives uniformly in
the deletion and in $x$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pmf-first-difference}
 c_i(x+1)-c_i(x)=d_i^2\phi'(d_ix)+\frac{d_i^3}2\phi''(d_ix)
       +d_i^3\frac{\kappa_i}{6w_i}(H_3\phi)'(d_ix)+o(d^3),
\end{equation}
where $d_i=w_i^{-1/2}$ and $\kappa_i$ is the deleted signed cubic
sum. In the cell $|u|\le c/H$ the deleted log characteristic
function has remainder $O(HPu^4)$, since
$\sum\E Y_j^4\le HP=O(HV)$; summed squared variances are
absorbed by this fourth-moment bound. The first-difference multiplier
adds a factor $O(d|s|)$ on $u=d_is$, so Gaussian integration costs
$O(Hd^4+d^4)=o(d^3)$. Outside this cell torus damping is at most
$e^{-cV/H^2}$. This proves \eqref{eq:pmf-first-difference} without
dividing by an atom mass.

At the selected positive closed threshold, the finite sum attains its
discrepancy at an atom. Thus $y-u$ lies on the deleted coset for
every test site $u$ of the original coordinate coset.
For $|u|\le H+1$, Taylor expansion in $d_i u$, retaining $z$
before expansion, gives
\[
 c_i(y-u+1)-c_i(y-u)
 =-d^2z\phi(z)+d^3\phz\{u-(1+\te)/2\}
       +o(d^3(1+|u|))+O(d^4|z|v_i).
\]
The last term records the deletion variance; no rate for the
unstandardized selected threshold is imposed.

For the centered second difference
$\Delta^2g(u)=g(u+1)-2g(u)+g(u-1)$,
the contact score has exact deleted-CDF contribution equal to the
mass difference just displayed. Its term $2A_0=d^2z\phi(z)+3d^2D$
cancels the leading normal term exactly. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lattice-concavity}
 d^{-3}\Delta^2g_i(u)=\phz(1+u)-\ce
       \{|u+1|^3+|u-1|^3-2|u|^3\}
       +o(1+|u|)+O(d|z|v_i).
\end{equation}
The leading expression is at most $-c(1+|u|)$.
For $|u|\ge1$ use the exact absolute-cubic difference $6|u|$;
for $0\le u\le1$ use its bound $2+4u^2$, giving a negative
margin since $\ce>\phz$; the negative interval has still larger
margin. In particular there are fixed $c,K>0$ such that
\[
 \Delta^2g_i(u)\le d^3\{KE_i-c|u|\},\qquad
                           E_i=1+d|z|v_i.
\]

Two maximizing contacts at integer distance $L\ge2$ have all
intermediate grid tests bounded above by their common contact value.
For $j=\lfloor L/2\rfloor$ the discrete Green formula is
\[
 g_i(u_0+j)-g_i(u_0)
 =-\sum_{k=1}^{L-1}G_{jk}\Delta^2g_i(u_0+k),\quad
 G_{jk}=\frac{\min(j,k)(L-\max(j,k))}{L}.
\]
Here $\sum G_{jk}=j(L-j)/2$. For $L\ge16$ the weighted average
of $|u_0+k|$ is at least $L/32$, independently of the coset offset:
the interval $|u|<L/16$ contains at most $L/8+1$ test sites and
carries less than half of that Green mass. It follows that the
original span satisfies
\[
                 \operatorname{span}_i\le K(1+d|z|v_i).
\]
Centering and $\sqrt{v_i}\le H$ give
$\sqrt{v_i}\le K+Kd|z|H\sqrt{v_i}$.
Since $dH\to0$, this bounds all $v_i$ and then all spans by a
fixed constant. With these improved bounds the last term in
\eqref{eq:lattice-concavity} vanishes uniformly. The score is
strictly concave between original support extrema. Two contacts
at distance at least two would have an intermediate site strictly
above their contact value by the same Green formula, a contradiction.
Each nonconstant original therefore has two adjacent support points.
\end{proof}

All active coordinates now have a common diameter. Full globality
implies that they also maximize in the restricted common-diameter class
at this same horizon. The finite common-diameter classification of
\citet[Theorem~1.1]{BellBernoulli2026} says that every such maximizing
array is padded IID. Its theorem includes all horizons and has its
finite arithmetic premises supplied there. This proves the sequence
conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:global}.

If no absolute $\varepsilon_0$ existed, choose at horizon $N_j$ an
actual non-IID full global with $L<1/j$. The sequence conclusion
would make those same original arrays padded IID eventually, a
contradiction. This proves the qualitative threshold statement.

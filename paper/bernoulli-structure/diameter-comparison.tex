\section{Exact replacement of nearby diameters}\label{sec:spread}
The comparator preserves the individual probability vector. Unequal
probabilities make the conditional law given the count nonuniform;
small probabilities also change the scale of the possible improvement.
We keep weighted count moments throughout the proof and condition only
after locating a competitive threshold.

Scale by $h_0$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:spread}, and put
\[
 q_i=1-p_i,\quad r_i=p_i^2+q_i^2,\quad
 R_0=\sum_i a_i r_i,\quad\mu=\sum_i p_i,
 \quad\ell=\sum_i a_i|e_i|.
\]
Then $\sum_i a_i e_i=0$, $A/2\le R_0\le A$ and
$\ell\le\sqrt A\sigma$. With
\[
 K=\sum_i B_i,\qquad T=\sum_i e_i(B_i-p_i),
\]
the original centered sum is $K-\mu+T$, and its variance is
$A+\sigma^2$. Write $c_k=\Pp(K=k)$ and
$N_j(k)=\E[T^j\ind_{\{K=k\}}]$.

\subsection{Count estimates with no probability floor}
Uniformly over all individual probabilities and every integer $k$,
as $A\to\infty$,
\begin{align}
 c_k&=\frac{\phi(x_k)}{\sqrt A}+O(A^{-1}),\notag\\
 F_K(k)-\Phi(x_k)
   &=\frac{\phi(x_k)}{\sqrt A}
      \left\{\frac12+\frac{\gamma_3}{6}(1-x_k^2)\right\}
           +O(A^{-1}),                              \label{eq:pb-grid}\\
 x_k&=(k-\mu)/\sqrt A,\qquad
 \gamma_3=A^{-1}\sum_i a_i(1-2p_i),\quad|\gamma_3|\le1.\notag
\end{align}
All constants here are absolute. To prove the mass formula, use
Fourier inversion, the local cumulant expansion, and
\[
 \left|\prod_{i\notin F}(q_i+p_i e^{it})\right|
 \le\exp\left\{-2\left(A-\sum_{i\in F}a_i\right)\sin^2(t/2)\right\}.
\]
For at most four deletions, $\sum_{i\in F}a_i\le1$.
Every fixed-order Bernoulli cumulant of order at least two is bounded
by a constant times $a_i$. The local Taylor remainders have the same
bound because $q_i+p_i e^{it}$ stays uniformly away from zero near
zero. Thus the Fourier integral of the error from the leading Gaussian
is $O(A^{-1})$, independently of $k$.

For the CDF introduce independent $U\sim\mathrm{Unif}[-1/2,1/2]$.
The exact identity is $F_K(k)=F_{K+U}(k+1/2)$.
Near zero, comparison of the characteristic function with
\[
 e^{-At^2/2}\{1+\kappa_3(it)^3/6\},\qquad\kappa_3=A\gamma_3,
\]
has CDF-inversion error bounded by a constant times
\[
 \int (A|t|^3+A^2|t|^5+t^2/|t|)e^{-cAt^2}\,dt=O(A^{-1}).
\]
The last term is the error from the jitter factor $\sinc(t/2)$.
At every nonzero peak $t=2\pi j+u$, $|u|\le\pi$,
\[
 |\sinc(t/2)/t|\le C|u|/j^2.
\]
The product modulus therefore bounds that peak by $O(1/(Aj^2))$.
Summing these bounds and using the Gaussian tails proves the jittered
CDF expansion uniformly in the threshold. Expanding its half-grid
shift gives \eqref{eq:pb-grid}. This proof uses a continuous jittered
CDF and actual integer-grid values of $F_K$.

The required weighted moments satisfy, uniformly for every $k$,
\begin{align}
 |N_1(k)|&\le C\ell A^{-3/2},\notag\\
 |N_2(k)-\sigma^2c_k|&\le C\sigma^2/A,\notag\\
 0\le N_4(k)&\le C(\sigma^4+\eta^2\sigma^2)/\sqrt A.
                                                    \label{eq:weighted-coefficients}
\end{align}
Here is a direct Fourier proof retaining the vanishing spread factors.
Put $f_i=q_i+p_i e^{it}$, $s=e^{it}-1$, $\Psi=\prod f_i$, and let
$\Psi_i,\Psi_{ij}$ denote deleted products. For $Y_i=B_i-p_i$,
\[
 \E[Y_i e^{itB_i}]=a_i s,\qquad
 \E[Y_i^2 e^{itB_i}]=a_i f_i+(1-2p_i)a_i s,
 \qquad |\E[Y_i^j e^{itB_i}]|\le a_i\quad(j\ge2).
\]
Weighted orthogonality gives the exact cancellation
\[
 \widehat N_1=s\sum_i a_i e_i\Psi_i
                  =-s^2\sum_i a_i p_i e_i\Psi_i.
\]
Its absolute integral is $O(\ell A^{-3/2})$.
For the second moment, subtracting $\sigma^2\Psi$ leaves
\[
 s\sum_i e_i^2(1-2p_i)a_i\Psi_i
       +s^2\sum_{i\ne j}u_i u_j\Psi_{ij},
 \qquad u_i=a_i e_i,\quad\sum_i u_i=0.
\]
Use the exact identity
\[
 \Psi_{ij}=\Psi+(1-f_i)\Psi_i+(1-f_j)\Psi_j
                         +(1-f_i)(1-f_j)\Psi_{ij}.
\]
The cross term is bounded by
$C\sigma^2(t^2+|t|^3+At^4)e^{-cAt^2}$; the singleton term has
integral $O(\sigma^2/A)$. This proves the second estimate without
dividing by an individual characteristic function.
For the fourth moment, the index partitions $4,31,22,211,1111$ and
\[
 \sum_i a_i e_i^4\le\eta^2\sigma^2,\quad
 \sum_i a_i|e_i|^3\le\eta\sigma^2,\quad
 \ell\le\sqrt A\sigma
\]
give respective absolute integral bounds controlled by
$C(\sigma^4+\eta^2\sigma^2)/\sqrt A$.
For example, the $31$ term is at most $C\eta\sigma^3/\sqrt A$;
$2\eta\sigma^3\le\sigma^4+\eta^2\sigma^2$ absorbs it.
Fourier inversion proves \eqref{eq:weighted-coefficients}, including
rare layers and layers of probability zero.

\subsection{Locating thresholds before conditioning}
At an actual positive closed threshold $y$, write
$y=k-\mu+\theta$, with $k$ nearest to $y+\mu$ and $|\theta|\le1/2$.
The exact decomposition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crossing-decomposition}
 F_h(y)=F_K(k)-c_k\Pp(T>\theta\mid K=k)+E_{k,\theta},
 \quad |E_{k,\theta}|\le
             \frac{C(\sigma^4+\eta^2\sigma^2)}{\sqrt A}.
\end{equation}
When $c_k=0$ the subtracted product is zero. To bound the remainder,
sum $N_4(j)/(|j-k|-1/2)^4$ over all $j\ne k$.
This retains all interleaving count layers and the closed convention.

The original denominator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resource-change}
 R_h-R_0=3\sum_i a_i r_i e_i+3\sum_i a_i r_i e_i^2
                                     +\sum_i a_i r_i e_i^3,
 \quad |R_h-R_0|\le C(\ell+\sigma^2+\eta\sigma^2).
\end{equation}
Consequently, for $P_h=(A+\sigma^2)^{3/2}/R_h$ and
$P_0=A^{3/2}/R_0$,
$P_h/P_0=1+O(\ell/A+\sigma^2/A)$ when $\sigma,\eta$ are small.
Dropping the nonpositive term in \eqref{eq:crossing-decomposition},
using \eqref{eq:pb-grid} and globally bounded normal derivatives,
first gives every positive discrepancy as $O(A^{-1/2})$.
The corresponding global ratio bound is
\[
 \mathcal C_h(y)\le C(1+x_k^2)\phi(x_k)
       +C(\sigma^4+\eta^2\sigma^2)+O((1+\sigma)/\sqrt A).
\]
Choose the spread bounds small and then $A$ large, depending on $c_0$.
Any active threshold with ratio at least $c_0$ has bounded $x_k$.
Thus $c_k\ge c_{c_0}/\sqrt A$ and
\begin{align}
 |\E(T\mid K=k)|&\le C\ell/A,\notag\\
 \E(T^2\mid K=k)&=\sigma^2\{1+O(A^{-1/2})\},\notag\\
 \E(T^4\mid K=k)&\le C(\sigma^4+\eta^2\sigma^2).
                                                    \label{eq:conditional-bulk}
\end{align}
Only at this stage have we divided the weighted estimates by $c_k$.

\subsection{An absolute-moment bound from two conditional samples}
At the localized count, we need the bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-l1}
                   \E(|T|\mid K=k)\ge c\ell/\sqrt A.
\end{equation}
The count-conditioned law is the classical rejective sampling law
\cite[Section~2]{BoistardLopuhaaRuizGazen2012}.
The relative estimates used here follow directly from our grid formula.
For at most three fixed coordinates $F$, direct factorization gives
\[
 \Pp(B_F=b_F\mid K=k)
  =\Pp(B_F=b_F)\frac{c_{-F}(k-\sum b_F)}{c_k}.
\]
The deleted variance differs by at most $3/4$ and the standardized
argument by $O(A^{-1/2})$. The mass estimate in \eqref{eq:pb-grid}
therefore makes the ratio $1+O(A^{-1/2})$, uniformly even when the
configuration probability tends to zero.

Take two independent copies $B,B'$ of the count-conditioned law.
Let $C=\{i:B_i\ne B'_i\}$ and $m=|C|$, an even integer.
Given $C$ and the common successes outside $C$, the change signs are
uniform balanced signs: the product of their odds has one factor
$p_i/q_i$ for every changed coordinate, independently of the sign.
Generate these signs by a uniform perfect matching of $C$ and
independent opposite Rademacher signs in each pair.
For a fixed matching, moment interpolation gives mean absolute value
at least $1/\sqrt3$ times the Euclidean norm of its coefficients
$e_i-e_j$. Averaging matchings gives, for $m\ge2$,
\[
 \E(|T-T'|\mid C)
 \ge\sqrt{2/3}\,
       \frac{\sum_{i<j\in C}|e_i-e_j|}{(m-1)\sqrt m}.
\]
The relative deletion ratios imply
\[
 \Pp(i,j\in C)\ge c a_i a_j,\qquad
 \E(m\mid i,j\in C)\le CA.
\]
For the first identity, if $\pi_{ab}$ are the conditional two-coordinate
state probabilities, the exact probability is
$2(\pi_{11}\pi_{00}+\pi_{10}\pi_{01})
 =4a_i a_j\{1+O(A^{-1/2})\}$.
For the second, fix the two change signs. This fixes two coordinates
and therefore the remaining count in each of the two independent
conditional samples. The one-coordinate relative deletion ratios
in these reduced samples bound the probability of a difference at
coordinate $l$ by $Ca_l$. Summing gives $\E(m\mid i,j\in C)\le2+CA=O(A)$. Jensen's inequality for $m^{-3/2}$
then yields
\[
 \E|T-T'|\ge cA^{-3/2}\sum_{i<j}a_i a_j|e_i-e_j|.
\]
Orthogonality gives
$\sum_j a_j|e_i-e_j|\ge A|e_i|$, so the sum is at least $A\ell/2$.
The triangle inequality $\E|T-T'|\le2\E|T|$ proves
\eqref{eq:conditional-l1}.

\subsection{The phase loss and the exact final comparison}
Write $M_1=\E(|T|\mid K=k)$,
$W=\Pp(T>\theta\mid K=k)$ and $L=\theta+W$.
Equations~\eqref{eq:conditional-bulk}--\eqref{eq:conditional-l1} give
\[
 |\E(T\mid K=k)|\le M_1/4,
 \qquad M_1\ge c\frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma+\eta}
\]
for $A$ large. The second inequality is interpolation between the
second and fourth moments. Thus
$\E(T^+\mid K=k)\ge3M_1/8$. Cauchy's inequality gives
\[
 \nu:=\Pp(T>0\mid K=k)\ge cM_1^2/\sigma^2
                              \ge cM_1/(\sigma+\eta).
\]
For small enough $\sigma+\eta$, $\nu\ge8M_1$.
If $\theta\ge M_1/4$, then $L\ge M_1/4$.
For $0\le\theta<M_1/4$, removing the lower positive values leaves
positive first moment at least $M_1/8$; Cauchy's inequality again
gives $W\ge cM_1^2/\sigma^2\ge cM_1$.
If $\theta=-u<0$ and $u\le\nu/2$, then $L\ge\nu/2$.
If $u>\nu/2\ge4M_1$, first-moment Markov gives
$\Pp(T\le-u\mid K=k)\le1/4$, hence $L\ge1/4$.
These cases prove the relative bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-phase}
 L\ge c\max\left(\frac\ell{\sqrt A},
                      \frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma+\eta}\right)>0,
 \quad |\theta|+\theta^2+W\le CL,
 \quad\sigma^2\le C(\sigma+\eta)L.
\end{equation}

Compare with the exact unit-diameter closed discrepancy
$D_0(k)=F_K(k)-\Phi(x_k)$, and set
$\alpha_k=\phi(x_k)/\sqrt A$.
At the bounded standardized threshold, normal Taylor expansion gives
\[
 D_h(y)-D_0(k)
 =-c_kL+(c_k-\alpha_k)\theta+E_{k,\theta}
                                      +O((\sigma^2+\theta^2)/A).
\]
Multiply by $P_0$, and use \eqref{eq:resource-change} to include
$(P_h-P_0)D_h$. Every final error contains $L$:
the normal, mass and denominator terms are $O(L/\sqrt A)$, and
the crossing term is at most $C(\sigma+\eta)^3L$.
The leading coefficient $P_0c_k$ has a positive lower bound.
Consequently
\[
 \mathcal C_h(y)-P_0D_0(k)
 \le\{-c+C(\sigma+\eta)^3+C/\sqrt A\}L
 \le-\kappa\frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma+\eta}.
\]
Choose the spread bounds and $A_0$ in that order.
The comparison uses the exact common-diameter branch, so no
spread-independent grid error remains. Its branch lies below the
full common-diameter ratio. Reflection of a negative strict maximizing
threshold preserves $a_i,r_i,\sigma,\eta$ and treats the other side.
This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:spread}.
If $p_i\in J\Subset(0,1)$, then
$\eta\le\sigma/\sqrt{\min_{p\in J}p(1-p)}$, giving its linear-$\sigma$
corollary.

\subsection{Why the probability-free gain has the smaller scale}
Fix $c_0<\ce$. Choose a fixed, sufficiently large IID unit-diameter
binary group of size $m$ with $p=\pe$, ratio above $c_0$, and a
positive closed maximum strictly above its negative strict maximum.
The phase expansion in Section~\ref{sec:iid-phase} supplies such groups, with $m$ above
any prescribed count-variance cutoff. Put $a=p(1-p)$, $r=p^2+(1-p)^2$.
Add a Bernoulli($s$) coordinate. For small $s$, its common-diameter
maximum still has the positive closed sign, by continuity of the finite
set of threshold branches. Denote a winning integer by $k(s)$.

Give the group diameter $g_c=1+s(1-s)\eta/(ma)$ and the rare coordinate
diameter $g_s=1-\eta$, with $0<\eta<1/4$.
This preserves the weighted mean diameter one. At raw threshold
$k g_c$ the CDF is exactly
\[
                 (1-s)F_{\mathrm{group}}(k)
                         +sF_{\mathrm{group}}(k-1),
\]
the same as the common-diameter array, since $0<g_s/g_c<1$.
The centered threshold, variance and third-moment sum are
\[
 y_k=kg_c-mpg_c-sg_s,\quad
 V_h=mag_c^2+s(1-s)g_s^2,\quad
 R_h=marg_c^3+s(1-s)\{s^2+(1-s)^2\}g_s^3.
\]
For fixed $m,p$, the corresponding finitely many smooth branch
formulas are analytic near $(s,\eta)=(0,0)$.
They agree with the common-diameter branch at $\eta=0$ and are
independent of $\eta$ at $s=0$. A bounded mixed derivative therefore
gives
\[
               0<\mathcal C_{\mathrm{com}}(s)-\mathcal C_h
                                      \le K_m s\eta,
\]
where the lower strictness follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:spread}.
Meanwhile
$\sigma^2=s(1-s)\eta^2\{1+s(1-s)/(ma)\}$.
Taking $s=\eta^4$ gives $\sigma\sim\eta^3$ and a gain of order
$\eta^5$, equal to the order $\sigma^2/(\sigma+\eta)$ supplied by
the theorem. These arrays stay above $c_0$ by continuity.
Thus a uniform gain proportional to $\sigma$ fails without a
probability floor, even for competitive finite binary arrays.

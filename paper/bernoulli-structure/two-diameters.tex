\section{Two two-point summands with arbitrary diameters}\label{sec:two}

We now prove Theorem~\ref{thm:two}. Normalizing $V=1$, any maximizing
sequence of two-point pairs with $\mathcal C\ge c>0$ has $1/\sqrt2\le R\le1/c$,
uniformly integrable second moments $\sum_i\E[X_i^2\ind_{\{|X_i|>L\}}]\le1/(cL)$,
and bounded thresholds $y$. Coordinatewise weak limits of two-point laws
remain supported on at most two points, preserve $V=1$, and attain the
two-summand binary supremum $C_{2,\mathrm{bin}}$.

\subsection{Top threshold and equal-diameter boundary}

For a centered two-point variable on $\{-b,a\}$ ($a,b>0$) with variance
$v=ab$, its third absolute moment satisfies
$\rho=ab(a^2+b^2)/(a+b)\ge2(\sqrt2-1)v^2/a$. For two such summands with upper
support endpoint $L=a_1+a_2$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives $R\ge2(\sqrt2-1)V^2/L$.
At the top closed threshold $y=L$, where $F(L)=1$, the ratio is therefore
bounded above by
\[
 \frac{z\{1-\Phi(z)\}}{2(\sqrt2-1)}
 \le\frac{e^{-1/2}}{4(\sqrt2-1)}<\frac{37}{100},
 \qquad z=\frac{L}{\sqrt V},
\]
using $1-\Phi(z)\le e^{-z^2/2}/2$. Reflection covers the bottom strict
threshold. Equal diameters ($h_1=h_2$) were already shown in
Section~\ref{sec:common} to have ratio $<37/100$, whereas a single active
coordinate achieves $C_1>37/100$.

\subsection{Stationarity equations for unequal interior diameters}

Scale the larger diameter to $1$ and write the smaller diameter as
$t\in(0,1)$, with upper atom probabilities $p,q\in(0,1)$. Set
\begin{gather*}
 a=p(1-p),\qquad b=q(1-q),\qquad
 r_p=p^2+(1-p)^2,\qquad r_q=q^2+(1-q)^2,\\
 V=a+t^2b,\qquad R=ar_p+t^3br_q.
\end{gather*}
The uncentered support is $\{0,t,1,1+t\}$, and the three remaining interior
closed thresholds have data
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{Uncentered threshold}&F&\text{Centered threshold }y\\\hline
 0&(1-p)(1-q)&-p-tq\\
 t&1-p&-p+t(1-q)\\
 1&1-pq&1-p-tq.
 \end{array}
\]
With $z=y/\sqrt V$, $D=F-\Phi(z)$, $C=DV^{3/2}/R$, $\alpha=\phi(z)/\sqrt V$,
$A=(3D+z\phi(z))/(2V)$, and $\lambda=D/R$, an interior maximum on a selected
branch must satisfy $g_p=g_q=G=0$, where
\begin{align}
 g_p&=F_p+\alpha+A(1-2p)-\lambda(1-2p)^3,\notag\\
 g_q&=F_q+\alpha t+At^2(1-2q)-\lambda t^3(1-2q)^3,\notag\\
 G&=\phi(z)RB+3Dwt a\sqrt V\,(r_p-tr_q),                \label{eq:gap-gradient}
\end{align}
and $(w,B)$ for the three thresholds $0,t,1$ is respectively
\[
 \bigl(1-q,\ p[(1-p)-t(1-q)]\bigr),\qquad
 \bigl(q,\ -p[(1-p)+qt]\bigr),\qquad
 \bigl(1-q,\ (1-p)[p+t(1-q)]\bigr).
\]

\subsection{Compact variance bound and certified exclusion}

In variance-one units, probability stationarity of coordinate $i$ means that
\[
 Q_i(u)=G_i(y-u)-F(y)+\phi(y)u+A(u^2-s_i)
                 -C\bigl\{|u|^3-b_i-3M_i u\bigr\}
\]
takes equal values at the two support points of $X_i$; since $\E Q_i(X_i)=0$,
both values vanish. Using $Cb_i\le D\le1$, $|M_i|\le s_i\le1$, $\phi(y)<2/5$,
and $|A|\le13/8$ gives $C|u|^3\le4+(2/5+3C)|u|+(13/8)u^2$, so $|u|<3+8/C$.
At $C\ge37/100$, the larger normalized diameter $1/\sqrt V$ is less than
$2(3+800/37)=1822/37$, which implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-compact-domain}
 V>\frac{1369}{3319684}>\frac1{2500},\qquad
 R\ge \frac{V^{3/2}}{\sqrt2}>\frac{V}{75}.
\end{equation}

The certificate \path{certificates/structure/two_arbitrary_gaps-base.json}
(together with its continuation and 37,421-leaf independent arithmetic replay;
see \path{certificates/structure/README.md}) covers $(p,q,t)\in[0,1]^3$ for
all three interior thresholds, certifying that every box is excluded by
\eqref{eq:two-compact-domain}, by $C\le D\sqrt{a/r_p^2+b/r_q^2}<37/100$, or
by a nonzero interval enclosure of $g_p$, $g_q$, or $G$. Thus no interior
stationary point can achieve $C\ge37/100$, leaving only the one-coordinate
maximizer with ratio $C_1>37/100$. Finally, adjoining a second symmetric
two-point coordinate of diameter $\eps\downarrow0$ shows that the supremum
over pairs with both variances positive remains $C_1$ and is unattained.

\begin{abstract}
We study the Berry--Esseen ratio of independent centered two-point
variables, allowing zero summands. At every fixed number of available
coordinates, every maximizing array with a common support diameter has
identically distributed nonzero coordinates. With at most two coordinates,
the same conclusion holds for arbitrary support diameters, and only one
coordinate is nonzero. A uniform binomial phase expansion determines
the asymptotic choice of active count when zeros are allowed: eventually
at most four zeros are needed, and their frequencies are explicit.
For binary arrays with ratios bounded away from zero, large total count
variance and small diameter deviations, replacement by a common diameter strictly
increases the ratio. The gain is quantified without an individual
probability floor, and improves when the probabilities stay in a compact
subset of $(0,1)$.
The finite classifications use interval certificates with explicitly
stated domains. The replacement argument is analytic.
\end{abstract}

\section{The extremal questions}
Let $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be independent centered real variables and put
\[
 V=\sum_i\E X_i^2>0,\qquad R=\sum_i\E|X_i|^3<\infty,
 \qquad
 \mathcal C(X)=\frac{V^{3/2}}R
   \sup_y\left|\Pp\{\textstyle\sum_iX_i\le y\}-\Phi(y/\sqrt V)\right|.
\]
Here $\Phi$ and $\phi$ are the standard normal distribution function and
density. A binary coordinate has the form $h_i(B_i-p_i)$, where
$h_i>0$ and $B_i\sim\Bern(p_i)$. Its support diameter is $h_i$.
Parameters $p_i=0,1$ give a centered zero coordinate.
For binary coordinates,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:binary-moments}
 V=\sum_i h_i^2p_i(1-p_i),\qquad
 R=\sum_i h_i^3p_i(1-p_i)\{p_i^2+(1-p_i)^2\}.
\end{equation}

The conjectured unrestricted bound is
\[
 \mathcal C(X)\le\ce:=\frac{3+\sqrt{10}}{6\sqrt{2\pi}}.
\]
Esseen's fixed-law asymptotics give this value as a necessary lower
bound, using repeated copies of a binary law with upper mass
$\pe=(4-\sqrt{10})/2$ \cite[pp.~160--163]{Esseen1956}.
Schulz proves the sharp bound for binomial sums
\cite[Theorem~1]{Schulz2016}; a companion paper proves it for independent
binary coordinates having a common diameter \cite{BellFinite2026}.
The present paper identifies the finite maximizers and proves
replacement theorems beyond exact diameter equality. It also explains
the zero padding visible in finite binomial optimization. The integer
threshold cannot remain at its preferred phase while the probability
remains at its asymptotic optimum; a smaller active core can have a
better phase and a larger ratio.

There are two separate difficulties. Unequal masses change both the
variance and the third-moment denominator. Unequal diameters can split
a convolution atom, so the objective need not be differentiable under
support motions. Our finite common-diameter argument varies probabilities
at a fixed integer threshold. Our diameter comparison replaces the
whole diameter vector and keeps the exact probabilities of crossing
each count layer.

Let $B_m$ be the maximal ratio for $m$ identically distributed active
binary coordinates. Let $C_n^{\mathrm{com}}$ be the maximum over at most
$n$ common-diameter binary coordinates. The maximum allows zero
coordinates. Let $C_1$ be the one-coordinate binary maximum; explicitly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c1}
 C_1=\max_{0<p<1}
 \frac{\sqrt{p(1-p)}}{p^2+(1-p)^2}
       \left\{\Phi\!\left(\sqrt{\frac p{1-p}}\right)-p\right\}.
\end{equation}
Bentkus and Kirsa prove that this is also the unrestricted
one-coordinate constant \cite[Theorem~1]{BentkusKirsa1989}.

\begin{theorem}[Common diameter]\label{thm:common}
For every $n\ge1$,
\[
 C_n^{\mathrm{com}}=\max_{1\le m\le n}B_m.
\]
Every maximizing array consists of identically distributed active
binary coordinates and zero coordinates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Two arbitrary binary diameters]\label{thm:two}
Over arrays with at most two binary coordinates, the maximal ratio is
$C_1$. Every maximizing array has exactly one active coordinate.
If both variances must be positive, $C_1$ is an unattained supremum.
\end{theorem}

Theorems~\ref{thm:common} and~\ref{thm:two} include finite interval
calculations. Their precise arithmetic premises and the analytic
boundary arguments are given in Sections~\ref{sec:common}
and~\ref{sec:two}. The records and verifier are described in
\path{certificates/structure/README.md} and \path{verify/structure/}.
These finite classifications do not assert uniqueness of the maximizing
active count or probability. The eventual binomial phase results are
given in Section~\ref{sec:iid-phase}.

\begin{theorem}[Small diameter spread]\label{thm:spread}
Fix $c_0>0$. There are $A_0,\sigma_0,\eta_0,\kappa>0$, depending
only on $c_0$, with the following property. Let
$X_i=g_i(B_i-p_i)$, where $g_i>0$ and $0<p_i<1$. Put
\[
 a_i=p_i(1-p_i),\quad A=\sum_i a_i,\quad
 h_0=\frac{\sum_i a_i g_i}{A},\quad e_i=g_i/h_0-1,
 \quad\sigma^2=\sum_i a_i e_i^2,\quad\eta=\max_i|e_i|.
\]
If $A\ge A_0$, $0<\sigma\le\sigma_0$, $\eta\le\eta_0$ and
$\mathcal C(X)\ge c_0$, then
\[
 \mathcal C(B_1-p_1,\ldots,B_n-p_n)-\mathcal C(X)
                 \ge\kappa\frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma+\eta}>0.
\]
The comparator preserves every individual probability. If all
probabilities belong to a fixed compact $J\subset(0,1)$, the gain is
at least $\kappa_J\sigma$ for some $\kappa_J>0$.
\end{theorem}

The maximum deviation condition controls diameters carried by arbitrarily
small probabilities. The constants in Theorem~\ref{thm:spread} are
qualitative. Section~\ref{sec:spread} also gives a competitive binary
family for which the gain has exactly the smaller order
$\sigma^2/(\sigma+\eta)$.

For every finite-support sum we use both atom sides:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:branches}
 \sup_y|F(y)-\Phi(y/\sqrt V)|
 =\sup_y\max\{F(y)-\Phi(y/\sqrt V),\,
               \Phi(y/\sqrt V)-F^-(y)\},
\end{equation}
where $F(y)=\Pp(S\le y)$ and $F^-(y)=\Pp(S<y)$.
Reflection interchanges the branches. Between consecutive atoms,
the positive closed discrepancy decreases. Thus its maximum occurs at
an atom, and one selected positive closed threshold suffices after
reflection, including ties.
